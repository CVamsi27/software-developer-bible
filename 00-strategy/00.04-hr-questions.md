# HR Round Strategy for Senior Full Stack Engineers

Ten minutes into your first recruiter call, before you have written a single line of code, the recruiter asks: *"What are your compensation expectations, and do you need visa sponsorship?"* Many senior engineers treat the HR screen as a formality and either undersell their level, give a vague non-answer, or discover four rounds later that the role's salary band or work-authorization rules cannot work. Treat the HR screen as a **mutual fit check** that aligns scope, hard constraints, and compensation early.

```text
Role Thesis ─► Clear Prompt Answers ─► Surface Hard Constraints Early ─► Offer Comparison & Negotiation
```

## Definition

An **HR or recruiter screen** is a mutual alignment conversation covering role scope, motivation, timeline, work authorization, and compensation expectations. It lets the employer verify basic match — and lets you confirm the role is worth investing 6–10 hours of technical interviews.

> **Textbook Definition:** An employment screening interview is a mutual assessment of a candidate's suitability and an employer's role conditions, including motivation, availability, and compensation expectations.

## First Principles

Both sides start with incomplete information. Surfacing hard constraints early (location, remote eligibility, notice period, visa sponsorship, and salary band) saves everyone a wasted loop. Keep your facts consistent from screen to offer: truthful, calm specificity builds far more trust than trying to game every question for an imaginary "right" answer.

## 1. Arrive With a Two-Sentence Role Thesis

Before the call, read the live job posting and one real product or engineering artifact (docs, changelog, or public blog post). Skip generic flattery (*"I love your mission"*) and connect one real product problem to your experience:

> *"I saw your team is scaling its multi-tenant billing platform across Europe. I've owned idempotent billing workflows and PostgreSQL tenancy boundaries in production, so I'd love to confirm whether this role owns core backend reliability or mainly customer-facing UI."*

That single paragraph signals genuine research, senior scope, and clear intent — and immediately draws out useful role details from the recruiter.

## 2. How to Handle Common HR Prompts (With Concrete Scripts)

| Prompt | Winning structure | Quick example / tip |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Tell me about yourself** | Current scope → 1–2 relevant highlights → why this next step | Keep it under 90 seconds; tie directly to the posting's stack and domain |
| **Why this company?** | Specific product/engineering challenge → matching proof → what you want to build | Cite something real you read on their site or docs |
| **Why are you leaving?** | Neutral push + positive pull toward what you want next | Never vent about your current manager; focus on scope, domain, or growth |
| **Compensation expectations** | Ask for the budgeted band first; if pressed, share a researched range | *"Could you share the budgeted range for this level? Based on senior full-stack roles in this market, I'm targeting X–Y depending on total package."* |
| **Visa / location / notice** | State your exact status and timeline plainly | *"I'll need German Blue Card support and have a 60-day notice period — does this specific opening support relocation?"* |
| **Layoff or career gap** | Plain 1-sentence fact + what you built or focused on since | No defensiveness or over-explaining needed |

## 3. Break Down an Offer Component by Component

Never compare two offers by a single headline number. Separate **guaranteed recurring cash**, **conditional bonuses**, **one-time payments**, and **illiquid equity**. Use current role/level/location data (remembering that self-reported sites like Levels.fyi vary in sample size and recency).

Here is an illustrative breakdown (arbitrary numbers to show the math, not a market benchmark):

| Component | Illustrative amount | How to evaluate it |
| ---------------------- | ------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Annual base salary** | €100,000 | Guaranteed recurring gross cash — your primary anchor |
| **Target bonus (15%)** | €15,000 | Conditional; ask historical payout rate and eligibility in year 1 |
| **Annualized equity** | €20,000 | Public RSUs are liquid after vest; private options/VSOP depend on strike price, dilution & exit |
| **Sign-on bonus** | €10,000 | One-time cash; check clawback/repayment clauses if you leave within 12–24 months |
| **Benefits value** | €5,000 | Pension match, health/transit subsidies, learning budget — useful, not cash |

Read those totals separately:
- **Guaranteed recurring base:** €100,000
- **Target recurring gross cash (base + full bonus):** €115,000
- **Modeled annual comp (with equity):** €135,000 (treat private-startup equity separately as an upside bet — see [Find Startup Roles](00.05-find-startup-roles.md))
- **Year-1 gross cash + equity (with one-time sign-on):** €145,000 — remember year 2 drops back by €10,000 unless base grows.

## 4. Negotiate a Specific Gap Calmly

Before countering, decide your walk-away floor and your top priority (base, level, sign-on to bridge a lost bonus, relocation support, or remote flexibility). Then make one clear, evidence-backed ask:

> *"Thank you for the offer — I'm excited about the team and the platform scope. Based on the senior ownership we discussed and comparable roles in this market, my main gap is base salary. Is there room to move base from €95k to €105k, or bridge year one with a sign-on bonus?"*

**Hard rules:**
- Never invent a fake competing offer — recruiters verify or call bluffs quickly.
- Ask for final terms, equity grant details, bonus conditions, and relocation repayment clauses **in writing** before resigning your current job.

## 5. Questions That Actually Protect Your Decision

Ask questions whose answers could change whether you say yes:

- *"What does strong performance look like in the first 90 days, and what is the hardest engineering problem the team faces right now?"*
- *"Why is this role open — growth, new initiative, or backfill?"*
- *"How are technical decisions made, how often is on-call paged, and how are promotions evaluated?"*
- *"Does this specific requisition support my location and work-authorization requirements?"*
- *"What are the exact remaining interview rounds, timeline, and compensation components?"*

## 6. Log What You Learned Immediately After the Call

Right after hanging up, record five lines in your private tracker: confirmed salary band, visa/location answer, exact interview stages, open questions for the hiring manager, and promised follow-up date. If one of your own answers felt shaky, sharpen that bullet or STAR story before the next screen.

## Summary

Treat the HR screen as a mutual filter: arrive with a concrete role thesis, answer motivation and constraint questions directly, and verify work authorization, location, and salary band before investing in a long technical loop. Compare offers by separating guaranteed base, conditional bonus, one-time sign-on, and risky private equity, then negotiate one concrete gap calmly and confirm every material term in writing.

# Distributed Transactions

A checkout can have a committed stock reservation and a payment whose response timed out. The charge may have succeeded even though the caller cannot yet prove it.

## Definition

An **atomic distributed transaction** commits or aborts one logical transaction across multiple transactional participants, which can fail independently. A local database transaction does not automatically include another service. **Two-phase commit (2PC)** supplies a shared decision among compatible participants. A **saga** and an **outbox** solve related service-coordination problems with different atomic boundaries: a saga commits steps locally and compensates later; an outbox commits outgoing intent with one local mutation.

> **Textbook Definition:** An atomic distributed transaction spans multiple transactional resource managers and uses a commit protocol to ensure that all participating branches reach the same commit-or-abort outcome despite failures, under the protocol's recovery assumptions.

## First Principles

Choose the required invariant before the protocol. If compatible participants must share one commit/abort decision, use an atomic commit protocol and design its recovery. If the business accepts visible intermediate states, a workflow can use local commits and explicit compensation.
A local transaction does not include another service, and a message broker alone supplies neither contract.

## How It Works


### 1. How two-phase commit reaches a decision

**Two-phase commit (2PC)** separates preparing the participants from announcing the shared outcome:

1. **Prepare:** each participant either refuses or durably records enough transaction state to honor a later commit. A prepared participant may retain locks and cannot safely infer an abort merely because its coordinator stops responding.
2. **Decide:** the coordinator durably records commit only after every required participant prepares. Otherwise it records abort under the protocol's decision rules. It sends that decision to the participants and retries notification during recovery.
3. **Recover:** a participant that missed the decision needs the authoritative outcome. Coordinator recovery, durable logs and the implementation's recovery protocol must prevent conflicting commit/abort decisions.

```text
Coordinator → prepare → participants record votes
            → durable decision → commit/abort notifications
                            └── lost notification: recover the SAME decision
```

2PC coordinates **atomicity**, not every aspect of isolation or visibility. Participants' isolation levels, application reads and protocol integration still determine what other operations can observe. Blocking while the outcome is unknown can retain locks and delay unrelated work; availability and throughput depend on the implementation and workload, not a universal “very low” rating.

PostgreSQL exposes prepared transactions for an external transaction manager. A prepared transaction can survive a session ending and continues to hold acquired locks until resolved. Keep its lifetime bounded through the manager's recovery process; an application timeout is not permission to guess the global decision.

### 2. Local commits require a recovery workflow

A **saga** records a business process as local committed steps with recovery behavior. A compensation is another business action, such as releasing a reservation or refunding a charge; it does not erase the first commit. Some effects cannot be reversed, and compensation itself can fail.

| Coordination | Who tracks the workflow | Cost to evaluate |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Choreography | Participants react to events and retain their own progress | Distributed ownership, causal tracing and cyclic reactions |
| Orchestration | A coordinator persists step identity, outcome and next action | Coordinator recovery, versioning and responsibility boundaries |

Service count alone does not select the approach. Choose from workflow ownership, visibility and recovery needs. Neither approach supplies isolation automatically: reserve stock or enforce a conditional mutation at its authoritative boundary so two workflows cannot both claim the same inventory.

#### Trace an uncertain payment

1. The inventory service commits a reservation with a stable workflow identity.
2. The payment request uses a stable provider-supported idempotency key.
3. The provider accepts the charge, but the response is lost.
4. Persist an **unknown outcome** and reconcile using the provider's contract. Do not label the charge rejected or immediately free the reservation simply because the call timed out.
5. Once the outcome is known, complete the order or run the appropriate compensation. Record compensation attempts and keep failed recovery visible for operator action.

A durable workflow must resume after a process crash. An in-memory list of completed callbacks cannot do that; logging a failed refund does not make it recovered. Define retry limits, expiry rules, irreversible steps and escalation before treating the workflow as reliable.
The [saga chapter](20.6.3.01-saga-pattern.md) owns the fuller coordination treatment.

A distributed lock or lease can limit who attempts work, but it does not make writes across databases atomic. Enforce the business invariant at the authoritative write boundary and use fencing or conditional versions when a former owner could resume after its lease expires.

### 3. Keep outgoing intent inside the local commit

The **transactional outbox** commits a business mutation and publication intent in the same database transaction. A separate relay publishes that intent after commit. It closes the writer's crash gap while retaining a separate broker acknowledgment and consumer-effect boundary.

| Boundary | Guarantee and remaining obligation |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business row + outbox row | Atomic local commit when both use the same transaction |
| Outbox relay + broker | Recoverable publication with possible duplicates; define acceptance and relay ownership |
| Consumer + local projection | Dedup marker and effect can commit together in one database |
| Consumer + external provider | Use supported idempotency/reconciliation or document ambiguous/duplicate outcomes |

The outbox is a building block for a workflow, not a global transaction. Use its [canonical mutation/intent example and crash table](20.6.3.06-transactional-outbox.md) rather than reimplementing a relay inside this comparison chapter. Change data capture (CDC) and polling have different operational costs; neither makes broker publishing part of the original database commit.

### 4. Choose coordination from the invariant and recovery boundary

| Required behavior | Candidate | Check before choosing |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One commit decision across compatible stores | Atomic commit protocol such as 2PC | Participant support, isolation, durable decisions and blocked-transaction recovery |
| Long-running order/reservation process with permitted intermediate states | Saga with local transactions | Business compensation, concurrency invariants and uncertain external outcomes |
| Accepted local change must trigger later work | Outbox | Same-transaction intent, relay recovery, ordering and effect-aware deduplication |
| One authoritative store can enforce the invariant | Local transaction | Keep the invariant local before introducing cross-service coordination |

An e-commerce order can combine a local reservation transaction, a saga and an outbox; these solve different boundaries. A cross-institution transfer may require explicit settlement and reconciliation contracts rather than compatible atomic-commit participants. State the assumptions instead of claiming one pattern fits every payment system.

These patterns move coordination boundaries; they do not remove coupling in schemas, ordering, ownership or recovery. Name the invariant and the party that can authoritatively enforce it before introducing cross-service coordination.

### 5. Test recovery at each durable boundary

Test failures before and after each durable decision, including a prepared participant missing its coordinator and a provider success whose response is lost. Verify that concurrent workflows preserve the business invariant, retries reuse identity and compensation can resume after restart. Monitor unresolved prepared transactions, unknown outcomes, stalled steps and relay lag with an owner for recovery.

## Summary

Distributed work needs an explicit invariant and recovery contract. 2PC coordinates an atomic decision among compatible participants; sagas coordinate visible local commits and compensation; an outbox makes local publication intent durable. None removes uncertainty, isolation requirements or the need to operate recovery.

# Microservices Interview Questions

## How to practice

Answer a definition in a few sentences, then expand into a failure trace and a decision boundary. For a design prompt, clarify requirements, name the authority/invariant, trace the normal operation, then test timeout, duplicate, crash and rollback. Use the linked canonical chapters for executable examples; this bank focuses on reasoning.

## First Principles

A remote operation can succeed while its reply is lost. Start every distributed-systems answer by naming who owns the decision, what durable evidence survives a crash and how a retry distinguishes an existing result from new work. Then change one condition: remove a dependency, duplicate a message or let an old worker continue after failover. The answer must preserve the business invariant under that change, rather than only name a pattern.

## Deep-dive answers

### Foundations

#### Q1: What is microservices architecture?

**Answer:** Microservices organize software around business capabilities with independently deployable ownership boundaries and explicit communication contracts. Separate processes alone do not establish that independence: shared writable tables, coordinated releases or a long synchronous call chain can create a distributed monolith.

Consider a modular monolith first when one team can release and scale the application effectively. Extract when a concrete autonomy, scaling or failure boundary justifies network uncertainty, deployment coordination and operational cost. **Follow-up:** What are the benefits and drawbacks? Explain the useful independence and its extra compatibility, data and recovery work.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../01-service-topology/20.6.1.04-strangler-fig.md).

#### Q2: How do microservices communicate?

**Answer:** Use a request/response contract when a caller needs an outcome before proceeding; use durable messages/events when work can complete later. Both remain coupled to schemas, meaning and ownership. A protobuf type does not replace domain validation, and publishing an event after an unrelated database commit leaves a crash gap.

Trace an order commit plus outbox intent, publication acknowledgment and consumer completion separately. A publish promise can wait for acceptance while business work remains asynchronous. Choose deadlines, identity, retry and ordering from the operation, rather than describing all messages as fire-and-forget.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../02-communication-and-messaging/20.6.2.06-event-driven-architecture.md).

#### Q3: What is service discovery?

**Answer:** Discovery resolves a stable logical name to current routes/endpoints. A registry, DNS/platform controller or load balancer can own that mapping; client-side discovery additionally needs endpoint selection and refresh logic in each supported client.

A normal Kubernetes Service DNS answer commonly gives a stable cluster IP; a headless Service can expose endpoint addresses. Readiness and registration are evidence, not proof that the next call succeeds. Handle no eligible endpoints, expiry, long-lived connections and removal/draining. Random selection is not round-robin.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../01-service-topology/20.6.1.01-service-discovery.md).

#### Q4: What is an API Gateway?

**Answer:** A gateway routes client traffic and applies chosen edge policies such as transport termination, authentication and demand limits. The domain service still verifies object/tenant authority: a valid identity is not permission to read every order.

Bound deadlines, connections, request/response sizes and retries. Preserve trusted identity instead of accepting spoofable forwarded headers. User-specific cache keys require authority and freshness dimensions. A backend for frontend (BFF) can tailor aggregation to a client, but fan-out and gateway business logic create additional latency/availability and ownership costs.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../01-service-topology/20.6.1.02-api-gateway.md).

#### Q5: What is the difference between synchronous and asynchronous communication?

**Answer:** Synchronous communication couples the caller's progress to the requested outcome. Asynchronous communication separates acceptance from later completion, so it needs durable progress, recovery and a user-visible pending/failure contract.

Neither determines throughput or complexity by itself. A fast bounded RPC may outperform a persistence-heavy queue; a queue can absorb a bounded burst but cannot create downstream capacity. Async consumers still face schema coupling, deadlines, duplicate effects and retained-data costs.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../02-communication-and-messaging/20.6.2.05-message-brokers.md).

### Mechanisms

#### Q6: What is the Saga pattern?

**Answer:** A saga coordinates local commits through explicit progress and recovery actions when the business permits intermediate states. Compensation is a new business operation, not global transaction rollback. Inventory must enforce its own reservation invariant before compensation becomes relevant.

Trace a payment timeout as an unknown result: retain the stable step identity and reconcile before issuing another charge or releasing stock. Persist workflow state and action intent, handle duplicate/late outcomes, and escalate unresolved compensation. Choreography distributes reactions; orchestration makes process state explicit. Neither removes durable recovery work.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.01-saga-pattern.md).

#### Q7: What is the Circuit Breaker pattern?

**Answer:** A circuit breaker rejects new protected calls when its measured failure policy is met. Closed admits calls, open rejects them, and half-open permits bounded recovery probes after cooldown. Already running calls still need deadlines and resource limits.

Test simultaneous probes and late outcomes from earlier states. Classify dependency failure separately from validation, authorization and local saturation. A fallback is optional: rejecting an unsafe mutation is preferable to inventing success. Retry composition determines whether the breaker counts attempts or whole operations and must fit one total budget.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../04-resilience/20.6.4.01-circuit-breaker.md).

#### Q8: What is event sourcing?

**Answer:** Event sourcing makes accepted events the authoritative history and derives state by deterministic interpretation. Snapshots and projections can store derived state; the claim that state is never stored is incorrect. A message broker or audit log alone does not establish an event-sourced system.

Enforce aggregate invariants against a known version and append with an atomic expected-version check. Keep snapshot state paired with its exact source version, retain compatible schemas, and prevent replay from repeating historical external effects. History, deletion, backup and retention policy are explicit costs, not automatic audit guarantees.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.03-event-sourcing.md).

#### Q9: What is CQRS?

**Answer:** Command Query Responsibility Segregation (CQRS) separates models used to enforce writes from models used to answer reads. They may share one process/database and can be synchronous. CQRS does not require event sourcing or two codebases.

An asynchronous read projection needs atomic progress, a version/dedup contract and a defined freshness response. Complete-state snapshots may tolerate version gaps; incremental deltas often cannot. Queries still authorize tenant/object access, and lagging stock or payment views cannot authorize business mutations.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.04-cqrs.md).

#### Q10: How do you handle distributed transactions?

**Answer:** Start with the invariant and compatible participants. One local transaction is simplest when one store owns the decision. Two-phase commit coordinates atomic outcome with explicit participant isolation and prepared-state recovery costs; it is not universally forbidden or equivalent to a lock.

Use a durable saga when intermediate states and compensation fit. Use an outbox when a local commit must trigger later work. Broker acknowledgment loss can cause duplicate delivery; transaction-local effects require a marker and mutation together, while external effects require provider-supported idempotency/reconciliation. Compare known rejection with unknown outcome before selecting recovery.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.02-distributed-transactions.md).

### Senior decisions

#### Q11: How would you design a microservices system for an e-commerce platform?

**Answer:** Clarify checkout latency, order volume, stock semantics, payment uncertainty, tenant authority and deployment ownership first. Keep inventory reservation atomic at its owner; persist order progress and stable payment identity. Notifications and analytics can complete later through durable publication and separately bounded consumers.

Choose capability boundaries that enable real independence, not one service for every table. Avoid a mandatory chain through user, catalog, inventory, payment and shipping for every screen. Explain catalog read freshness, reservation expiry, reconciliation, event schema compatibility, failure budgets and rollback before adding CQRS or a broker.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.01-saga-pattern.md).

#### Q12: How do you handle service failures in microservices?

**Answer:** Separate observed remote failure from local overload, caller cancellation and an unknown write result. Bound total deadlines/attempts, use cooperative client cancellation and retain resource permits until actual settlement. Retries need stable identity and a classified reason to repeat.

A breaker controls new admission; a bulkhead controls in-flight resources; queues control bounded waiting. Cached degradation needs correct tenant/user scope and acceptable age. Reconcile effects that may have committed, and allocate recovery capacity so the serving pool cannot consume it all.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../04-resilience/20.6.4.02-bulkhead-pattern.md).

#### Q13: How do you implement distributed tracing?

**Answer:** Propagate trace context across actual HTTP/RPC and message boundaries, and create spans around meaningful work. Background or batched consumers may need links rather than pretending every event shares one current parent. Context headers are diagnostic input, not authorization.

Head sampling decides early; tail sampling needs enough collected spans and an operational budget to decide later. Neither automatically captures every slow/error path. Correlate minimized logs and bounded-cardinality metrics, initialize supported instrumentation at the required time, and check exporter failure/privacy overhead. A trace describes observed work, not durable workflow authority.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../40-platform/40.4-observability/01-pillars/40.4.1.03-tracing.md).

#### Q14: How do you ensure data consistency across services?

**Answer:** Name each invariant before selecting consistency. Stock availability or a unique reservation can require an atomic local decision; display fields may accept bounded lag. A saga exposes intermediate states and compensation, not strong isolation over every participating store.

Commit business data and publication intent together, then define per-key source/consumer ordering and atomic processed progress. Read-your-writes requires a meaningful source/version contract; simply routing to a primary cannot make an unrelated asynchronous projection current. Reconciliation detects drift but does not replace admission-time invariants.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.06-transactional-outbox.md).

#### Q15: How do you handle database per service pattern?

**Answer:** Service-owned data means other services do not mutate its private schema directly. Separate schemas/roles on one instance can enforce an initial boundary; a separate server is an operational isolation choice. APIs or narrow replicas replace cross-boundary queries according to freshness and availability needs.

An order often stores an accepted customer/contact/price snapshot rather than fetching mutable state to reconstruct historical meaning. Shared reporting reads are managed transitional dependencies, not universal impossibilities. Inventory the old jobs, grants, joins and recovery scripts before claiming independent ownership.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.05-decomposing-monolith-database.md).

### Design scenarios

#### Q16: Design a URL shortener like bit.ly

**Answer:** Estimate redirect/create demand, retention, alias rules and abuse requirements. Allocate unique short codes through a constraint-backed collision policy or unique allocator; base62 is an encoding, not uniqueness or secrecy. Define expiry, revocation and destination scheme validation before caching redirects.

A permanent cached redirect can outlive revocation; choose HTTP status/cache policy deliberately. Keep analytics out of the critical redirect path under a bounded delivery/loss contract. Hot keys, miss storms, cache invalidation, authorization for management and destination-fetch SSRF need separate controls; no universal 99.9-percent read ratio is implied.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.01-url-shortener.md).

#### Q17: Design a real-time chat application

**Answer:** Persist an authorized message under a stable client action identity and server-assigned room sequence. A socket connection does not authorize every room or payload. Distinguish accepted, delivered and read states; presence expires and is only an estimate.

Replicas route to their local sessions through a defined fan-out layer; ordinary pub/sub is not retained offline history. Reconnect uses an authorized cursor and retained messages with duplicate-safe rendering. Bound slow-client buffers and payloads, support multiple devices/session ownership, and plan E2E encryption key distribution if the product requires it.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.15-chat-system.md).

#### Q18: Design a notification system

**Answer:** Separate the durable notification intent, recipient/channel preference decision and provider attempt. Resolve templates and authorized recipient data under a defined version/time policy; use priority lanes so time-sensitive verification does not wait behind bulk campaigns.

Reuse provider-supported idempotency identity where available and reconcile unknown acceptance; many providers do not guarantee it. Provider acceptance is not delivery to a device/mailbox/carrier. Track callbacks, suppression, quiet hours, retry limits and dead-letter ownership, with privacy and retention for notification content. Bound fan-out rather than unbounded Promise.all.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.07-notification-service.md).

#### Q19: Design a rate limiting system

**Answer:** Choose identity, dimensions, burst capacity and refill/window policy from the protected resource. Trust user/API-key identity from authentication; IP identity needs a trusted proxy policy and can group many users behind NAT. A token bucket bounds burst and average arrival rate, not concurrent occupancy.

Replicas need an atomic shared decision or a deliberate local-budget approximation. A Redis pipeline alone is not an atomic read-modify-write decision. Decide fail-open/closed/degraded behavior from abuse and business risk, bound limiter cardinality, and supply a truthful retry hint when one can be computed.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../20.3-rest-apis/04-edge-and-scale/20.3.4.01-rate-limiting.md).

#### Q20: Design a distributed caching system

**Answer:** Specify authoritative storage, freshness/invalidation, key scope and miss behavior before distributing cache nodes. Consistent hashing can reduce movement on membership change; virtual-node counts and moved fractions depend on topology and weighting rather than universal constants.

Replication needs a version/conflict/failover contract, not independent Promise.all writes called atomic. Protect hot keys and miss storms with bounded coalescing/backpressure. A reader racing database update plus cache deletion can repopulate stale data; use a tested version/invalidation strategy. Tenant/user authorization must remain valid on cache hits.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.29-distributed-cache.md).

### Operational follow-ups

#### Q21: How do you handle database migrations in microservices?

**Answer:** Use expand, backfill, switch and contract while old/new app versions coexist. New fields/defaults and dual representations must have clear write ownership and validation. Serialize migration execution for the owned schema and observe lock/duration/capacity impact; not every DDL operation belongs in a transaction.

Contract only after old consumers and rollback dependencies are retired. A down migration cannot restore deleted meaning or guarantee compatible rollback. Rehearse restore/roll-forward and source-position-aware backfill, rather than relying on a tool's migration-history table.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../03-data-and-consistency/20.6.3.05-decomposing-monolith-database.md).

#### Q22: How do you handle service versioning?

**Answer:** Define compatibility for requests, responses and events under actual consumer behavior. Optional fields are not automatically harmless when clients strictly validate or serialize differently. Protobuf field numbers, unknown enum values and persisted event schemas need their own evolution rules.

URL, header/media type and event-envelope versions are routing/contract choices, not a universal ranking. Reject unsupported versions explicitly, measure consumers, publish migration/deprecation conditions and retire versions after evidence. Use adapters at boundaries when possible; permanent parallel internal implementations raise maintenance cost.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../20.3-rest-apis/01-design-and-contract/20.3.1.03-versioning.md).

#### Q23: How do you monitor microservices?

**Answer:** Metrics summarize rates, errors, duration and resource pressure; traces locate observed request work; logs explain particular events. Define user-visible service-level indicators/objectives and owned alert response, then correlate the signals with safe identifiers.

Bound label cardinality and sensitive payloads. Dependency connectivity alone is not a complete health check. Keep liveness, readiness and startup roles distinct, including the risk of readiness removal concentrating load. Sampling/export failure, queue lag, unknown outcomes and recovery backlog are part of operating the system.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../40-platform/40.4-observability/INDEX.md).

#### Q24: How do you handle secrets management?

**Answer:** Use workload identity and least-privilege secret access, with explicit expiry, refresh and rotation overlap. Validate retrieved structure, coalesce concurrent refreshes, and decide failed-refresh behavior before an old credential expires. An arbitrary one-hour cache does not follow a leased credential's lifetime.

A lease expiring does not necessarily terminate existing database sessions; connection rotation is another contract. Avoid committed credentials and secret-bearing logs; inventory backups, CI and generated outputs. KMS remote signing supplies a signature over an exact input, not a private key to pass to a local signer.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../20.5-security/03-hardening-and-operations/20.5.3.04-secrets-management.md).

#### Q25: How do you handle blue-green deployments?

**Answer:** Blue-green runs two compatible application versions and switches routing after readiness/smoke/effect checks. Keep capacity, in-flight requests, sessions, streams, jobs and writer authority in the cutover plan. A shared database is common but not mandatory; separately replicated stores need an explicit state-transfer contract.

Routing rollback is safe only while old code/data/contracts can still handle accepted new effects. Scale old capacity before routing back, preserve complete route selectors and drain rather than instantly killing active work. Canary reduces exposure incrementally; neither strategy automatically proves zero downtime or instant rollback.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../01-service-topology/20.6.1.04-strangler-fig.md).

#### Q26: How do you handle circuit breaker in async systems?

**Answer:** Distinguish a publisher's broker acceptance from a consumer's downstream operation. A consumer breaker around a failed provider can pause/limit processing, leaving durable uncompleted work; it should not acknowledge and silently discard it. Lag indicates progress pressure, not a direct health signal for every downstream.

Bound waiting, retention, retry and poison-message escalation. A dead-letter publish to the same unavailable broker is not a reliable fallback. Preserve original identity and recovery authority, and handle lease/rebalance/cancellation while a partition or delivery is paused.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../02-communication-and-messaging/20.6.2.05-message-brokers.md).

#### Q27: How do you handle idempotency in distributed systems?

**Answer:** Use one stable identity per intended action, scoped to tenant/actor/operation and bound to a request fingerprint. Atomically claim work and persist its local mutation/result when one database owns them. Same key with different content is a conflict; simultaneous requests need defined pending/result behavior.

A cache GET followed by charge and SET is not atomic with the provider. Use its supported idempotency key and reconciliation for unknown outcomes, with documented retention. Expired keys can allow repeats; server-generated Date.now identities change on retry and defeat deduplication.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../30-architecture/30.3-infra-patterns/02-failure-and-recovery/30.3.2.03-idempotency-keys.md).

#### Q28: How do you handle distributed locking?

**Answer:** A lease can reduce concurrent work but can expire while its holder is still running. Atomic token-checked release stops an old owner deleting a successor's lease; it does not stop old work. A fencing token must come from a suitable ordering authority and be enforced by the protected resource.

Prefer a local invariant/transaction or expected-version change when possible. A lock keyed by from/to account pair does not serialize all withdrawals from one account, and two debit/credit calls are not an atomic transfer. Explain expiry, failover, stale-owner rejection and recovery rather than treating a Redis lease as money correctness.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../../../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/01-theory/30.2.1.14-distributed-locks.md).

#### Q29: How do you handle service mesh?

**Answer:** A mesh moves selected network policy into managed data-plane components and a control plane. Sidecar mode and ambient node-level/layer-seven components have different placement and policy boundaries; Istio ambient is not simply an eBPF proxy replacement.

Authenticated encrypted transport does not grant object/tenant permission. Retries need business-safe semantics and one total budget; traces still require context propagation and application spans. Measure policy compatibility, resource cost and rollback, including existing pods/connections, instead of assuming every mesh supplies identical behavior.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../01-service-topology/20.6.1.03-service-mesh.md).

#### Q30: How do you handle event-driven architecture at scale?

**Answer:** Commit durable publication intent with the authoritative local change, then operate transport and consumers with owned schemas, stable event identity and progress. Partition/order keys from the invariant and track oldest lag, replay budget and rebalance effects. A transactionalId setting alone does not execute a consume-transform-produce transaction.

Kafka output and consumed-offset transactions cover that Kafka boundary under configured consumers; external effects remain separate. Define full-state/delta compatibility, poison handling and controlled replay so history cannot accidentally send old payments/notifications again. Retention and snapshots determine whether a rebuild is possible.

Source: [Mechanism and worked example](../02-communication-and-messaging/20.6.2.03-kafka.md).

34. **What is the future of microservices?**

35. **How do you choose between microservices and monolith?**

   - Prefer the simplest structure that meets current release, scaling and ownership constraints. A well-separated modular monolith can be the right answer; extract only when the gain exceeds extra coordination and operations. [Migration decision](../01-service-topology/20.6.1.04-strangler-fig.md).

## Summary

A strong answer names authority, operation identity, uncertainty, actual resource lifetime and a recovery owner. Explain why the chosen boundary fits the invariant and what evidence would justify a more complex architecture.

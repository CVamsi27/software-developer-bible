# Saga Pattern


## Definition

A saga coordinates a business workflow through local committed transactions and compensating actions. Intermediate results can be visible, and compensation restores an acceptable business outcome rather than making the whole workflow an atomic rollback.

> **Textbook Definition:** A saga is a sequence of independently committed local transactions that coordinates a distributed business process, with compensating actions used to address previously committed steps when later steps fail.

## First Principles

Flight, hotel and payment services can commit independently. Once a flight reservation commits, failure to book the hotel cannot erase that committed history. The workflow must either complete the missing work or perform a meaningful cancellation. That recovery needs durable knowledge of what was requested, what completed and what remains uncertain.

## Follow a failed trip booking

```text
reserve flight → reserve hotel → charge payment → confirm trip
      │                │                │
 cancel flight    cancel hotel     refund payment
```

Suppose the flight reservation succeeds and hotel booking returns a definitive rejection. The workflow records the rejected hotel step, requests cancellation of the flight and records that cancellation's result. If cancellation fails, the trip remains in a compensation-pending state for retry and reconciliation.

A hotel timeout is different: the reservation might have completed remotely. Retrying or cancelling without a stable operation identifier risks a duplicate or an orphan. Query the operation's status or retry using the provider's idempotency contract before deciding what to compensate.

## Make the recovery record durable

| State | Evidence needed before advancing |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Step requested | Workflow ID, step ID, stable operation key and input |
| Step completed | Provider result or authoritative local commit identity |
| Step uncertain | Timeout/failure evidence and a reconciliation action |
| Compensation pending | Original outcome and stable compensation identity |
| Workflow finished | Confirmed business outcome, including any unresolved exception policy |

Persist state transitions and outgoing intent atomically where possible, for example using an outbox. A restart should resume from durable evidence. A stack of in-memory callbacks lost on crash is not a durable saga implementation. A timeout races with an operation that may continue; `Promise.race` alone does not cancel remote work.

Compensations must tolerate retries and possible races with delayed success. Choose order from domain dependencies; reverse order is common but not a universal rule. Some steps are irreversible or have only partial compensation, so place them deliberately and define escalation rather than claiming every action can be undone.

## Where coordination lives

In orchestration, one workflow owner decides the next step from persisted state. Replication and durable execution can prevent that owner from becoming an unrecoverable single point of failure. In choreography, participants react to events and own portions of progress and recovery. They remain coupled through event meaning and ordering even if they do not call each other directly.

Choose based on branching, visibility, ownership and recovery needs, not a fixed three-step/four-step cutoff. Both approaches need correlation IDs, duplicate handling, deadlines, retry budgets and operational views of stuck work. Broker delivery and a workflow engine supply mechanisms; they do not automatically define correct compensation.

## A saga does not replace every transaction

Use one database transaction when the required changes fit its boundary. Two-phase commit can coordinate atomic commit across compatible resource managers, with blocking and operational tradeoffs; it is not inherently limited to one database or incompatible with every service architecture. A saga deliberately accepts intermediate commits and lacks global isolation. Its local transactions may still acquire locks.

When partial state would violate a hard invariant, redesign the boundary or use an appropriate atomic mechanism. Reservations, escrow and a single authoritative owner can sometimes avoid an immediate cross-service invariant. A refund is a business action, not proof that no charge ever occurred.

## Summary

Trace a workflow through success, definitive failure and uncertain outcome. Persist progress and intent, give retries stable identities and design compensation as recoverable business work. The saga's contract includes visible partial state and operational reconciliation.

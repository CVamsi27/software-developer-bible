# Backup Strategy & Restore Drills

> **TL;DR:** **A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a strategy.** RPO sets how often you copy and RTO sets how you restore; the 3-2-1-1-0 baseline (three copies, two media, one offline/immutable, zero verification errors) is the floor; and restore drills are scheduled game days with a runbook you could execute at 3 AM — including the parts nobody practices, like partial restores and point-in-time recovery.

---

## First Principles

(→ 20.4.5.04 covers the DB specifics; the general axioms:)

1. **Backups exist for restore, not for storage.** RPO/RTO definitions (→ 20.4.5.04) only mean something when a restore has been *executed* — the drill is the backup; the artifact is an ingredient.
2. **Corruption replicates; history doesn't.** Point-in-time recovery (→ 20.4.5.04's PITR) is the defense against "the bad write synced everywhere" — which is why WAL/binlog archival outranks snapshot frequency in the design.
3. **Restores need rehearsal under pressure** — game days restore into isolated environments with measured timings; the gap between "we think it works" and "it took 4 hours" is the actual finding.

---

## Definition

A **backup strategy** is a specification of how data is copied, retained, and restored, defined by two numbers and one practice:

- **RPO (Recovery Point Objective)** — the maximum acceptable data loss, measured in time. It dictates *backup frequency*.
- **RTO (Recovery Time Objective)** — the maximum acceptable downtime. It dictates *restore mechanism* (restore from object storage takes hours; a warm replica takes minutes).
- **Restore drill** — the periodic act of restoring into a clean environment and verifying the data. It is what separates a backup from a hope.

```text
              RPO drives            RTO drives             the 3-2-1 rule
tier 0 (money) minutes              minutes (multi-AZ)     ≥3 copies, 2 media, 1 offsite
tier 1 (primary DB) hourly          < 1 h (snapshot+WAL)   + immutable (object lock)
tier 2 (analytics) daily            hours                  + versioned
tier 3 (derived) rebuildable         n/a                   no backup needed
```

Two things define correctness beyond the schedule. First, **a backup is only proven by a restore**: silent corruption, truncated uploads, and unreadable encryption keys all pass a "backup job succeeded" check, so the drill must verify row counts/checksums after restoring. Second, **backups must be protected from the primary's blast radius** — an attacker or a bad deploy with write access to production must not be able to delete the backups, which is what immutable (object-lock) and offline copies are for.

## The Problem

```
Incident:  DROP TABLE orders;  (or ransomware encrypts everything,
           or a bad migration truncates production)
Question:  what is our Recovery Point Objective (RPO) — how much data
           do we lose? And our RTO — how long until we are serving?
Answer:    whatever your backup schedule and restore practice actually deliver.
```

Teams fail this in two flavors: **no restores ever attempted** (corrupt/unreadable backups discovered mid-incident) or **no schedule math** (nightly fulls with 23-hour RPO for a business that tolerates 15 minutes). Strategy means choosing taxonomy, cadence, storage, and verification to hit explicit RPO/RTO numbers.

## Backup Taxonomy

### By scope of data copied

| Type | Copies | Restore cost | Storage cost | Notes |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Full** | Everything | Fastest (one artifact) | Highest | Baseline anchor |
| **Incremental** | Changes since LAST backup (any kind) | Slow chain: full + all incrementals in order | Lowest | Chain corruption risk; many tools |
| **Differential** | Changes since last FULL | Moderate: full + latest diff | Medium | Simpler chains; diffs grow toward next full |

Typical cadence: weekly full + daily differentials, or weekly full + hourly incrementals with periodic synthetic fulls to cap chain length.

### By representation

| Kind | What it is | Pros | Cons |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Logical** (pg_dump, mysqldump, SQL exports) | Portable statements/data | Cross-version friendly; selective restores | Slower on huge DBs; no PITR by itself |
| **Physical** (WAL archives, snapshots, pg_basebackup) | Block-level images | Fast; enables point-in-time recovery (PITR) | Version/platform-locked; restore is all-or-nothing |

Mature setups combine them: physical/PITR for fast full recovery within retention, logical dumps for long-term archive and granular rescue. PostgreSQL-specific mechanics — WAL archiving, `pg_basebackup`, pgBackRest, PITR walkthroughs — are covered in the database stack chapter at `20-backend/20.4-database/05-operations-and-scaling/20.4.5.04-backups-pitr-recovery.md`; this chapter stays at strategy level.

## The 3-2-1 Rule (and friends)

```text
3  copies of the data      (primary + two independent backups)
2  different media/systems (e.g., object storage in two providers/regions)
1  copy offsite / offline  (immutable bucket, air-gapped, other region)
```

Modern hardening adds:

- **3-2-1-1-0**: one copy *immutable/offline*, and **zero** verification errors (i.e., automated validation must pass).
- Immutability against ransomware: S3 Object Lock (compliance mode), WORM tapes, separate backup AWS account with restricted cross-account access only.

## Mapping RPO/RTO to Schedule

Definitions first:

- **RPO** = maximum tolerable data LOSS window (measured backwards from failure).
- **RTO** = maximum tolerable time to RESTORE service.

Your effective RPO ≈ backup/replication interval (+ replication lag). Your effective RTO ≈ detection + retrieval + restore + verify + cutover. Design backwards from the targets:

| Tier | Example workload | Target RPO | Target RTO | Recipe |
| ------ | ------------------------- | ---------: | ---------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gold | Payments, orders | ≤ 5 min | ≤ 1 h | Streaming replica + WAL shipping every minute + nightly verified snapshot offsite |
| Silver | Core app DBs | ≤ 1 h | ≤ 4 h | Hourly incrementals/WAL + nightly full, cross-region copy |
| Bronze | Internal tools, analytics | ≤ 24 h | ≤ 24-48 h | Nightly full to object storage, weekly restore test |

Sanity-check arithmetic:

```text
Silver target RPO 60 min:
  hourly incremental cadence (worst case 59 min loss) ✓

Target RTO 4h for a 500 GB database:
  restore throughput needed ≈ 500 GB / (4h - 30min slack) ≈ ~40 MB/s sustained
  → validates choice of physical restore over logical replay
  → drives parallelism requirements for the restore tooling
```

If the math fails, change the recipe (bigger restore instance, faster storage class, warm standby), not the target.

## Restore Drills & Game Days

Cadence that works in practice:

| Drill | Frequency | Scope |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Automated test-restore (CI job) | Daily/weekly per critical DB | Latest backup into ephemeral instance, run assertions |
| Manual restore rehearsal | Monthly | On-call engineer executes runbook against staging-sized dataset |
| Full game day | Quarterly | Timed end-to-end: declare incident → restore → cut over → measure RTO vs target |

Game-day script:

```text
1. Announce scenario: "region lost; restore orders DB to 10:32 UTC"
2. On-call runs ONLY from the runbook (notes every gap encountered)
3. Timebox each phase: locate artifacts (10 min) -> provision (15 min)
   -> restore/replay (bulk) -> consistency assertions -> app cutover
4. Record achieved RTO/RPO; compare to targets
5. File gaps as tickets: missing credentials, unclear steps, slow fetches
6. Rotate participants each quarter — the runbook trains people
   who were not in the room when it was written
```

The drill's product is an updated runbook, not a passing grade.

## Automated Verification

Trust, then verify automatically:

```bash
#!/usr/bin/env bash
# verify_backup.sh — runs after each backup completes
set -euo pipefail

BACKUP="$1"
EXPECTED_SHA="$(cat "${BACKUP}.sha256")"

# 1. Integrity: checksum matches manifest
echo "$EXPECTED_SHA" | sha256sum --check --status "$BACKUP" \
  || { alert "checksum mismatch: $BACKUP"; exit 1; }

# 2. Freshness: newest backup age under SLA
AGE_MIN=$(( ($(date +%s) - $(stat -c %Y "$BACKUP")) / 60 ))
[ "$AGE_MIN" -le 120 ] || alert "backup stale: ${AGE_MIN}min old"

# 3. Restorability: actually restore into throwaway instance
docker run --rm -v "$(pwd)/restore:/var/lib/postgresql/data" postgres:16 initdb -D /data
gunzip -c "$BACKUP" | docker exec -i pg-verify psql -c "\i /dev/stdin"

# 4. Semantic assertions against restored data
psql_verify "SELECT count(*) FROM orders WHERE created_at > now() - interval '25 hours';" \
  | assert_gt 0
psql_verify "SELECT max(created_at) FROM orders;" | assert_recent "PT2H"

notify "backup verified: $BACKUP (${AGE_MIN}min old, assertions passed)"
```

Verification ladder, cheapest first:

1. **Checksums** — bit-rot detection (also enforced by object-store ETag checks).
2. **Parse/load** — artifact decompresses and imports without error.
3. **Semantic assertions** — row counts in expected ranges, latest timestamps fresh, foreign keys valid.
4. **App smoke test** — boot the service against restored data; login, read a dashboard.

Alert on *absence* too: "no successful verified backup in 26 hours" is the alarm that saves companies.

## Runbook Template

```text
RESTORE RUNBOOK — <system>            Owner: <team>   Last drill: <date>

TRIGGERS: data corruption, accidental deletion, region loss, ransomware

PARAMETERS
  Target time (T):        ____________  (from incident commander)
  Target database/table:  ____________
  Environment: prod-dr / staging-verify

INVENTORY (fill in real values, keep updated)
  Artifact catalog:       s3://<bucket>/<prefix>/manifest.json
  Credentials:            vault path ____________ (break-glass: ________)
  Restore compute:        instance type ____ , IOPS ____, network ____
  Tooling image:          <registry>/<restore-tools>:<tag>

STEPS
  1. Freeze writers / enable maintenance mode          [cmd] ______
  2. Select artifact nearest T WITHOUT crossing it     [cmd] ______
     (list artifacts + timestamps: ______________________ )
  3. Fetch & checksum                                  [eta: ___]
  4. Provision clean target                            [eta: ___]
  5. Restore base + replay increments/WAL to T         [eta: ___]
  6. Verify: checksums, row counts, FK integrity, app smoke
  7. Cut over traffic / re-point DNS                   [cmd] ______
  8. Unfreeze writes; monitor error rate 30 min
  9. Postmortem: actual RPO ___ target ___ ; actual RTO ___ target ___

ROLLBACK PLAN: if restore fails twice, escalate to ____, consider
               point-in-time clone from cloud snapshot instead.
```

Keep one printed/PDF copy accessible when IdP and wiki are both down — the classic second-order failure.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it bites | Fix |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Backups never tested | Corruption/format drift found during outage | Automated test-restores + quarterly game day |
| Same account/region stores primary and backup | Ransomware/region loss takes both | Separate account, immutable, cross-region copy |
| Only logical dumps at TB scale | RTO impossible (hours→days) | Physical/PITR for speed, logical for archive |
| Incremental chain never consolidated | One corrupt link ruins week of backups | Synthetic fulls; cap chain length |
| RPO assumed = backup interval | Replication lag adds loss | Measure lag; include it in RPO math |
| Backups exclude secrets/config | Data restores, system doesn't boot | Vault snapshots + IaC state in scope |
| No freshness alarm | Silent backup failures for weeks | Alert on absence + staleness |

## Interview Questions

**Q: "How would you design backups for a payments database?"**

> Gold tier: streaming replica plus WAL shipping for near-zero RPO, nightly physical base backups with 35-day PITR, cross-region encrypted copies under object-lock for the 3-2-1-1 rule, and logical dumps retained yearly for audit. Every backup passes automated checksum+test-restore; quarterly game days prove RTO under 1 hour. The compliance requirement usually dictates 7-year retention for ledgers — that goes to cold archive, not the operational restore path.

**Q: "What does 'backup success' mean?"**

> Three levels: artifact exists, artifact verifies (checksum + loadable), artifact restores with correct data (semantic assertions + app smoke). Only the third counts. I'd automate all three and alert on their absence, because silent backup failure is the default state of unattended systems.

---

## 30-Second Interview Version

> I design backwards from RPO/RTO: tier workloads, pick cadence so worst-case loss fits RPO (hourly incrementals for 1-hour RPO), and size restore throughput so RTO is achievable. Follow 3-2-1(-1-0): three copies, two systems, one offsite and immutable, zero unverified. Verification is automated — checksums plus scheduled test-restores into throwaway instances with row-count and freshness assertions — and quarterly game days time the full runbook end-to-end. A backup untested by restore is just storage cost.

---

## Summary

- Choose taxonomy deliberately: physical/PITR for RTO, logical for portability and archive; fulls anchor, incrementals cheapen, differentials simplify.
- 3-2-1-1-0: copies, media diversity, immutability, verified-to-zero-errors.
- Effective RPO includes replication lag; effective RTO must be measured in drills, not asserted in docs.
- Automate the verification ladder and alert on stale/missing backups.
- The runbook is the deliverable; game days keep it true.

## Cheat Sheet

```text
BACKUP STRATEGY CHEAT SHEET
═══════════════════════════════════════
TAXONOMY
• Full / Incremental (since last) / Differential (since last full)
• Logical = portable, selective | Physical = fast, PITR-capable

RULES
• 3 copies / 2 media / 1 offsite (+1 immutable, 0 errors)
• RPO ~= interval + lag | RTO ~= detect+fetch+restore+verify+cutover

CADENCE
• gold: replica+WAL, <=5min RPO, 1h RTO
• silver: hourly incr + nightly full, 1h/4h
• bronze: nightly full, 24h+

VERIFICATION LADDER
checksum -> loads -> semantic asserts -> app smoke
alert on absence & staleness

DRILLS
daily auto test-restore | monthly manual | quarterly timed game day
product = improved runbook
```

# Technical Debt — Identification, Prioritization, Remediation

Three booking entry points implement the same availability rule. They have drifted: one accepts a slot another rejects, and rescheduling will add a fourth path. Whether this duplication is technical debt depends on the recurring cost or risk it creates, not the number of lines:

```text
3 entry points, 1 availability rule, 2 defects → debt = recurring cost, not line count.
```

## Definition

**Technical debt** is a design or implementation choice whose current form raises the cost or risk of later work. The financial metaphor distinguishes **principal** (work needed to change the design) from **interest** (recurring friction or risk while it remains). The choice can be deliberate or discovered after learning more.

> **Textbook Definition:** Technical debt is future engineering cost associated with a design or implementation decision that makes later modification or operation harder relative to an available alternative.

## First Principles

Engineers choose under incomplete knowledge and limited time. New requirements can reveal that yesterday's reasonable structure makes today's changes expensive. Remediation also consumes finite capacity and can break working behavior, so every imperfect module cannot be rewritten immediately.

A useful debt decision compares future relevant work with the cost/risk of changing the structure. Appearance alone is insufficient. A long function rarely changing may matter less than a small shared rule repeatedly causing mismatched billing.

## 1. Separate intent from prudence

These labels answer two different questions: did the team knowingly accept the future cost, and was that trade-off reasonable given what it knew at the time?

| Quadrant | Example | Management question |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Deliberate/prudent | Keep one storage adapter for launch; record expected replacement trigger | Was the speed benefit worth measured later friction? |
| Deliberate/reckless | Ignore known tenant boundary because release is near | Does immediate risk require remediation before more work? |
| Inadvertent/prudent | New information reveals a better boundary | Which future changes justify adapting it? |
| Inadvertent/reckless | No one knowingly waives a required compatibility check, because review ownership was never established; rollout breaks existing readers | Which missing review or control would prevent recurrence? |

Judge "prudent" or "reckless" from the evidence and engineering practices available at the time, not with hindsight. Repeated failures can show that a process needs to change; by themselves, they never prove the original decision was reckless. This is a reasoning aid, not four precise monetary categories. Bugs, missing security controls, and unsupported dependencies can require urgent fixes regardless of whether calling them debt is useful. Debt need never have been consciously chosen; the quadrant includes inadvertent debt discovered through later learning.

## 2. Make one debt item reviewable

For the booking case, record:

- **Evidence:** changes to one rule require three edits; two recent defects show different accepted slots.
- **Affected next work:** rescheduling will add a fourth caller and another chance to diverge.
- **Options:** unify one authoritative check; constrain callers now; or retain duplication with a documented review/test obligation.
- **Principal:** estimated implementation, behavioral tests, data migration if needed, and rollout/rollback effort.
- **Interest:** repeated engineer time, support incidents, and business risk, with uncertainty stated.
- **Owner and exit condition:** one rule is authoritative and entry-point behavior is verified under concurrent booking, not merely "class has fewer lines."

Here, first derive the intended availability rule from booking policy; the existing paths disagree, so copying one without checking policy could preserve a defect. Then route each entry point through that rule and enforce the no-overlap invariant at the authoritative write boundary with a database constraint or transaction/locking mechanism suited to the storage model. Test policy edge cases and competing requests. Repayment is complete when every caller follows the intended rule and concurrent requests cannot violate the invariant.

Never equate all repeated syntax with repeated knowledge. Different business rules can start alike and later change independently; forcing them through one generic abstraction can create new coupling.

## 3. Prioritize against actual work

A simple illustrative appraisal: a repair costing 100 hours and saving about three hours per week breaks even in roughly 33 weeks **if** that work continues and the estimate is right. Include rollout risk and maintenance of the new abstraction. A security or data-integrity constraint can override that economic calculation.

Use frequency of change, concrete failure impact, affected roadmap, support burden, and dependency risk. A score is a decision aid, not objective measurement: averaging ordinal 1–5 labels and dividing by estimated hours never supplies a universal priority. Record assumptions and revisit them when the roadmap changes.

The five current [DevOps Research and Assessment (DORA) delivery metrics](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/) describe team delivery throughput and instability. Treat them as context, not proof that one module's debt caused a change: workload, staffing, and other changes can move the same measures. Compare affected tasks and incidents before and after a repair.

Schedule larger structural work as a bounded item. Use opportunistic cleanup when it lowers the cost of current work without hiding risk; a fixed sprint percentage is an allocation policy, not evidence that debt is declining.

## 4. Migrate across a chosen boundary

Choose the pattern matching where the duplication sits. A **strangler fig** moves ownership of selected capabilities between systems. **Branch by abstraction** keeps callers stable while one implementation is replaced. In either case, define who owns writes and what outcome callers can rely on; the pattern alone never settles data or side-effect risks.

Move capability ownership gradually — route each capability to one authoritative implementation at a time:

```text
request → routing decision → old capability (authoritative for remaining scope)
                         └→ new capability (authoritative for migrated scope)
```

Choose one capability and assign a single write owner. This pattern repays debt when it removes a duplicated ownership boundary, not merely when traffic reaches the new system. Plan data cutover and rollback with the [zero-downtime deployment guidance](30.3.5.01-zero-downtime-deployments.md); a route percentage must never make both implementations authoritative for conflicting writes.

Keep callers stable while replacing an implementation: an adapter can hold callers steady while one implementation changes. Define the shared semantic contract, including idempotency and error meaning. Once an uncertain remote operation selects a provider, retries must retain that provider identity; switching providers can duplicate the effect. Use **shadowing** only for pure or isolated paths: run a non-authoritative implementation, compare normalized outputs, and suppress side effects and sensitive logs.

## 5. Prove repayment at the boundary

Characterization tests record current observable behavior before a change, including awkward cases. Then distinguish behavior to preserve from a known defect to fix. For the booking example, test the invariant at the real write boundary: a mock insert call proves neither correct scope nor protection against concurrent booking.

Prioritize contracts, invariants, failure/recovery paths, and integration boundaries. Coverage reports identify unexecuted code; 100% line coverage cannot establish correctness, and a universal 100/80/60 target never ranks risk.

Documentation should carry the actual public interface, recovery, and decision contract: identity, constraints, version boundaries, evidence, and owner. An architecture decision record (ADR) explains alternatives and consequences in context; generic "PostgreSQL is ACID" text never explains why it was selected here. Keep it current when assumptions change.

Once the exit condition is met, remove obsolete adapters and flags. Schedule unrelated structural work separately rather than bundling it into every feature.

## Summary

Technical debt becomes actionable through concrete future friction or risk, a bounded option and an owner. It can be deliberate or discovered through learning. Prioritize the affected work, preserve observable contracts during replacement and prove improvements at failure/authority boundaries; cleaner-looking code alone is not repayment evidence.

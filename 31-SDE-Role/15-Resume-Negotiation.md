## Resume Best Practices

### Structure

```text
RESUME STRUCTURE (1 page for <10 yrs, 2 pages max for 10+ yrs):

1. Header
   ├── Name, Phone, Email, LinkedIn, GitHub/Portfolio
   └── Location (no full address)

2. Summary (2-3 lines, optional for experienced)
   ├── YOE + specialization + key achievement
   └── Example: "Staff Engineer with 10+ years building
       distributed systems serving 10M+ users at scale"

3. Experience (Reverse chronological)
   ├── Company, Location, Dates (MM/YYYY format)
   ├── Bullet for each major achievement
   └── Focus on impact: "Improved X by Y% by doing Z"

4. Skills
   ├── Languages, Frameworks, Tools, Cloud
   └── Categorized, not just a list

5. Education
   ├── Degree, University, Year
   └── GPA (only if 3.5+), Honors, Relevant coursework

6. Optional Sections
   ├── Patents, Publications, Open Source, Speaking
   └── Only if significant and relevant
```

### STAR Bullet Format

**Formula:** `Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]`

```text
❌ Weak: "Responsible for API development"
✅ Strong: "Designed and built RESTful API layer processing 50K req/s,
            reducing p99 latency by 40% through Redis caching layer"

❌ Weak: "Worked on frontend features"
✅ Strong: "Led migration of legacy jQuery codebase to React,
            improving page load time by 60% and reducing bug rate by 35%"

❌ Weak: "Fixed bugs in production"
✅ Strong: "Implemented distributed tracing with OpenTelemetry,
            reducing MTTR from 4 hours to 15 minutes across 200 microservices"

❌ Weak: "Wrote unit tests"
✅ Strong: "Achieved 95% code coverage with Jest, integrating into CI/CD
            pipeline and catching 98% of regressions before deployment"
```

### ATS Optimization

```text
ATS (Applicant Tracking System) Tips:
═══════════════════════════════════════

1. Use standard section headers: Experience, Education, Skills
2. Include keywords from the job description naturally
3. Use .docx or PDF (NOT .png, .jpeg, or proprietary formats)
4. Avoid tables, columns, infographics — ATS can't parse them
5. Spell out acronyms on first use: "Amazon Web Services (AWS)"
6. Use standard fonts: Arial, Calibri, Helvetica
7. No header/footer content — ATS often misses it
8. Save as PDF unless specified otherwise
```

## Company-Specific Resume Tips

### Google

```text
- Summarize impact clearly (non-engineers review resumes first)
- Include specific numbers and percentages
- Focus on system design and scale
- Open source contributions valued highly
- 1-page strongly preferred
```

### Amazon

```text
- Embed Leadership Principles in bullet points
  - "Invented new caching strategy" → Invent and Simplify
  - "Owned end-to-end delivery" → Ownership
  - "Mentored 5 junior engineers" → Hire and Develop the Best
- Use metrics religiously (efficiency, cost savings, scale)
- STAR format is expected
- Previous startup experience is a plus
```

### Meta (Facebook)

```text
- Focus on impact: "Moved metric by X%"
- Emphasize full-stack capabilities
- Move fast culture: "Shipped feature in 2 weeks"
- Open source projects heavily weighted (especially React-related)
- Hackathon projects are relevant
```

### Apple

```text
- Highlight hardware-software integration if relevant
- Focus on quality, detail, and craftsmanship
- Privacy and security experience is valued
- End-to-end ownership stories resonate
- Design sensibility is a differentiator
```


## Negotiation Strategies

### Never Accept First Offer

```text
NEGOTIATION RULES:
═══════════════════════════════════════

1. NEVER give a number first
   → "I'm focusing on finding the right fit. What's the
      budgeted range for this role?"

2. ALWAYS get multiple offers
   → "I have other conversations in progress. Can you share
      the timeline for your process?"

3. Let them name a number
   → If pressed: "Based on my research and experience, I'm
      looking for roles in the $X-Y range."

4. Don't lie, but don't volunteer
   → "I'm still evaluating. I'd love to see your offer first."

5. Negotiate total compensation, not just base
   → Include: Base + Bonus + RSUs + Sign-on + Annual grant
```

### Compensation Components

```text
COMPENSATION BREAKDOWN:
═══════════════════════════════════════

Base Salary:
  - Guaranteed, paid bi-weekly/monthly
  - Negotiable: typically 10-30% above initial offer
  - Cap varies by level and location

Annual Bonus:
  - Target: 10-20% of base at junior, 20-50% at senior+
  - Paid annually or bi-annually
  - Usually pro-rated for first year

RSUs (Restricted Stock Units):
  - Vesting: 4-year standard (1-year cliff, then monthly/quarterly)
  - Refresher grants: Usually at year 2-3
  - Negotiate: Front-loading, larger initial grant

Sign-on Bonus:
  - One-time: $10K-$100K+ depending on level
  - Often clawback if leaving within 1 year
  - Can negotiate two-year sign-on for tax efficiency

Performance Bonus / Target Bonus:
  - 10-30% of base at target
  - Can exceed 200% of target for top performers
  - Not guaranteed but historically paid

Perks:
  - Relocation assistance
  - Meal/transit subsidies
  - Education budget
  - Gym/wellness benefits
```

### Negotiation Scripts

```text
SCENARIO: Initial Offer Received

"I'm excited about the role and team! Based on my experience
and [competing offers/market research], I was hoping we could
look at the compensation. I'd love to accept if we can get
closer to [specific number]. Could you check if there's
flexibility on [base/signing/RSUs]?"

SCENARIO: Competing Offer

"I appreciate the offer! I do have another offer from [Company]
for [amount]. They're also strong, but I prefer your team and
culture. If you can match the total comp, I'd happily sign today."

SCENARIO: Negotiating Level

"I'm confident I can deliver at the [next level] based on my
experience with [specific achievement]. Would it be possible
to reassess the level based on my background?"
```

### When to Accept

```text
ACCEPTANCE SIGNALS:
═══════════════════════════════════════

✅ Good time to accept:
  - "This is our final and best offer"
  - Manager is a known reference with positive feedback
  - Offer is competitive with market (check levels.fyi)
  - Deadline is approaching and you've exhausted extensions
  - You have no other pending interviews

❌ Red flags:
  - Unwilling to put verbal offer in writing
  - Pressure to accept same day ("exploding offer")
  - Significantly below market with no path to adjustment
  - Negative blind/Glassdoor reviews about the team
  - High attrition on the team
```

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Listing responsibilities instead of achievements | Use STAR bullet format with metrics |
| Generic resume without keywords | Tailor resume for each company/role |
| Accepting first offer without negotiation | Always negotiate — 80%+ expect it |
| Not having competing offers | Stagger interview timelines to overlap |
| Revealing current salary | Redirect: "I'm targeting roles in the $X-Y range" |

## Summary

- Resume optimization focuses on quantifiable impact metrics and STAR-format achievement descriptions
- Negotiation leverages competitive offers, market research (Levels.fyi, Blind), and timing strategy
- Total compensation includes base salary, equity/RSUs, sign-on bonus, and annual performance bonus
- Interview timing between competing offers creates leverage for maximizing compensation packages
- Professional brand building through GitHub, LinkedIn, and personal projects strengthens candidate positioning

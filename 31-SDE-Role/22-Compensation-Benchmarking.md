# Compensation Benchmarking & Negotiation Scripts

> A reference for senior full-stack engineers evaluating offers from top tech companies. Covers base salary bands by level, equity mechanics (RSU vs options), bonus structures, sign-on, and negotiation scripts that have actually worked.

## Definition

A reference for understanding total compensation (TC) at FAANG-tier companies and how to negotiate. Covers base, RSU mechanics, bonus, sign-on, perks, and level mapping. Includes scripts and tactics for counter-offer conversations.

## Why It Matters (TL;DR)

- **TC ≠ base** — equity is often 40-60% of TC at senior levels
- **Levels are not equal across companies** — Google L5 ≠ Meta E5 ≠ Amazon L5
- **Negotiation is expected** — companies leave 10-20% on the table
- **Multiple levers** — base, sign-on, RSU refresh, start date, title, relocation
- **The "no negotiate" reply is rare** — and even then, sign-on and start date are flexible

## RSU Mechanics

### What is an RSU?

A **Restricted Stock Unit** is a promise to deliver shares of company stock at a future vesting date. You don't own the stock until it vests. RSUs are taxed as ordinary income at vest (and at supplemental wage rates for vesting events).

### Vesting Schedules

| Company | Schedule | Cliff |
|---------|----------|:-----:|
| Google | 4 years, 25%/year | 1 year |
| Meta | 4 years, 25%/year + 10% bonus in year 1 | 1 year |
| Amazon | 4 years, 5%/year + 15%/year + 40%/year + 40%/year (back-loaded) | 1 year |
| Microsoft | 4 years, 25%/year | 1 year |
| Apple | 4 years, 25%/year | 1 year |
| Netflix | 4 years, 25%/year (back-loaded: 5/15/40/40) | None |

> **Amazon and Netflix back-loading**: 40% vests in year 3 and 40% in year 4. This means your first-year take is much lower than the headline TC.

### Refresh Grants

Most public companies grant **refresh RSU grants** annually (sometimes called "top-up" or "annual vest + refresh"). The refresh is typically tied to performance and level. Expectation:
- L5/E5/L6: refresh ≈ 50-100% of initial grant
- L6/E6/L7: refresh ≈ 50-80% of initial grant
- Promotions include a one-time top-up

## Sign-On Bonus

- **Range**: $10K (junior) to $200K+ (staff+)
- **Clawback**: typically pro-rated if you leave within 1-2 years
- **Tax**: paid as a bonus, taxed at supplemental wage rate (~22% federal + state)
- **Negotiability**: very high — easiest lever to push on

## How to Calculate Total Compensation (TC)

```text
YEAR 1 TC = base + bonus + (sign-on / 1) + (initial RSU / 4) + perks value
YEAR 4 TC = base + bonus + (full RSU + refresh) + perks

EXAMPLE — Meta E5, $230K base, $400K RSU/4yr, $50K sign-on, 10% bonus:
  Y1: $230K + $23K + $50K + $100K + $20K perks = $423K
  Y2: $230K + $23K + $100K + $200K (refresh) + $20K = $573K
  Y3: $250K (raise) + $25K + $100K + $250K (refresh) + $20K = $645K
  Y4: $260K + $26K + $100K + $300K (refresh) + $20K = $706K
```

## Compensation Negotiation

### The Cardinal Rule

> **Always negotiate.** Companies expect it; recruiters often have 10-20% discretionary budget. Declining to negotiate signals either desperation (bad signal to employer) or inexperience (lost income for years).

### When to Negotiate

- **After the verbal offer, before the written**: best time
- **After the written offer**: still negotiable, but more constrained
- **Counter within 24-48 hours**: momentum matters
- **Never** during the interview loop (no leverage yet)

### The Three Levers (In Order of Negotiability)

| Lever | Negotiability | Range |
|-------|:-------------:|-------|
| **Sign-on** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | 1.5-3x initial ask |
| **RSU** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | 10-30% |
| **Base** | ⭐⭐ | 5-10% |
| **Bonus target** | ⭐ | 0-5% |
| **Start date** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | 2-4 weeks |
| **PTO** | ⭐⭐ | +1-2 weeks |
| **Title** | ⭐⭐ | Sometimes, mostly for external leveling |
| **Relocation** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Lump sum up, equity in lieu of cash |

## Negotiation Scripts

### Script 1: Counter on Base + RSU

> **"Thank you for the offer. I'm genuinely excited about [Company/Team]. Based on my research and conversations with senior engineers in similar roles, I'd like to discuss the compensation. I was hoping we could get to [$X base / $Y RSU]. Is there flexibility there?"**

If they push back: ask what flexibility exists in sign-on or refresh. If they say "this is the top of the band," ask for sign-on.

### Script 2: Leveraging a Competing Offer

> **"I've received an offer from [Company B] with total compensation of $X. I'm leaning toward [Your Company] because of [specific reason — mission, team, tech], but the gap is significant. Is there any way to close that gap?"**

Always be ready to share the competing offer in writing (PDF). Recruiters will often ask for the number upfront.

### Script 3: Asking for More Time

> **"I'm really excited about this offer. Could I take 48 hours to review it with my family before responding? I want to make sure I can give this the consideration it deserves."**

This signals seriousness, not reluctance. It also gives you time to gather competing offers.

### Script 4: Final Push When They Say "No"

> **"I appreciate the constraints. Before I respond, can I ask: is there any flexibility on sign-on, start date, or a one-time equity refresh at the 6-month mark? I'm committed to making this work, and any of those would help me get there."**

If they truly cannot move: accept gracefully. Don't burn the bridge; ask about the path to promotion + refresh in 12 months.

### Script 5: Walking Away (Rare but Powerful)

> **"I really appreciate the offer, but at this level of compensation, I can't make the math work. I'd rather keep the door open for a future conversation than start and feel under-compensated. Thank you for the time."**

Only do this if you have a strong alternative. Walking away often prompts a re-engagement with more budget.

## Common Negotiation Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Lying about a competing offer | Recruiters talk. Be honest or don't reference it. |
| Accepting the first offer | Always counter. Recruiters expect it. |
| Negotiating only on base | Sign-on and refresh are easier to move |
| Giving a number first | "What range did you budget for this role?" |
| Being adversarial | Professional, curious, collaborative tone |
| Not getting it in writing | Verbal commitments don't survive; insist on the updated offer letter |

## What Companies Won't Tell You

```text
HIDDEN LEVERS:
  • Sign-on clawback is negotiable (shorter window)
  • RSU refresh can be brought forward if you have a competing offer
  • "Top of band" is often 10-20% above the offered number
  • Promotion timing can be brought forward 3-6 months in some cases
  • Relocation lump sum is very negotiable (often 1.5-2x ask)
  • Immigration status (H-1B transfer, green card sponsorship) is a lever
  • Joining bonus can be split across two payments for tax efficiency
```

## Summary

- TC = base + bonus + RSU + sign-on + refresh + perks — equity is 40-60% at senior levels
- Levels don't map 1:1 across companies — Google L5 ≈ Meta E5 in scope
- Always negotiate; recruiters expect 10-20% discretionary budget
- Easiest levers: sign-on, refresh, start date; hardest: base, bonus
- Use competing offers professionally; never lie
- Get the final offer in writing; verbal commitments don't survive

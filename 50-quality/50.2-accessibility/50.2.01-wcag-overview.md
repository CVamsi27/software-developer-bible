# WCAG Overview

## Definition

WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) is a set of guidelines developed by W3C (World Wide Web Consortium) to make web content more accessible to people with disabilities. It gives teams a shared way to describe and check barriers in content and interaction.

> **Textbook Definition:** The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines are a W3C standard of testable success criteria for making web content perceivable, operable, understandable and robust, with defined conformance levels and requirements.

Consider a booking form whose error is only a red border. Someone who cannot distinguish that color may not know what failed; someone listening to the page may not encounter the change at all. The question is whether the user can complete and recover from the task, not merely whether the interface looks familiar.

## First Principles

An interface carries information through presentation, structure and interaction. Users may perceive that information through sight, magnification, speech or braille and operate it with a pointer, keyboard, voice or switch. A cue that depends on only one channel can exclude another.

Separate the meaning from its presentation: identify the field and its error in text, associate them programmatically and preserve a usable route back to the field. Checking those relationships requires inspecting the implemented behavior as well as the markup.

## How principles become checks

The four principles are often abbreviated **POUR**. They organize related questions, rather than prescribe four independent fixes. [W3C explains their relationship](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/intro#understanding-the-four-principles-of-accessibility).

| Principle | Question in the booking example |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Perceivable | Is the error available as text and through the supported presentation? |
| Operable | Can the user reach the field and submit without a pointer? |
| Understandable | Does the error say what happened and how to correct it? |
| Robust | Do the browser and assistive technology expose the control's name, state and relationships? |

A **guideline** describes a goal. A **success criterion** states a testable requirement, with conditions and exceptions. A **technique** describes one possible implementation. A technique is not the entire standard, and an audit tool's rule is not necessarily a complete test of the corresponding criterion. [WCAG's layers of guidance](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#wcag-2-layers-of-guidance) distinguish these roles.

## Trace one error across the whole task

1. A visible label identifies the email field and is associated with its input. Placeholder text alone does not keep that information available while typing.
2. Submitting an empty field produces a specific text error. The application marks the field invalid and connects the message to it.
3. A focus/notification strategy makes the failed submission discoverable without competing announcements. The user can return to the field, correct it and keep other entered values.
4. On correction, obsolete error state is removed. The server still validates the request and may return a different error, which needs the same recoverable treatment.

The [forms chapter](50.2.05-forms-and-labels.md) owns the working implementation. Adding aria-invalid alone does not implement these steps, and a clean initial form screenshot does not establish them.

## What a conformance claim means

**Level A** requires the applicable A criteria; **AA** includes A and AA; **AAA** includes all three levels. They are cumulative requirements, not percentages of a checklist or audit scores. A stated target must name the WCAG version and level.

Conformance applies to full pages and complete processes, with additional requirements about supported technologies and non-interference. A checkout cannot claim success by evaluating only its first page while excluding the payment step. A passing component audit is useful evidence within a larger evaluation; it is not automatically a page or product conformance claim. [Understanding conformance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/conformance.html).

The standard is technical guidance; a specific legal or contractual obligation depends on its own scope and referenced version. Record that requirement explicitly rather than equating every project with the same law or level. Conformance also does not cover every possible user need.

## Version matters when choosing the target

Use published standards rather than treating an emerging draft as the established requirement. WCAG 2.2 adds criteria addressing barriers such as obscured keyboard focus, dragging-only operations, repeated data entry and accessible authentication. For example, a sticky footer can hide the focused input even though its outline exists. These additions change what needs evaluation. [What is new in WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/).

WCAG 2.2 removes the older Parsing criterion, 4.1.1. This does not make duplicate IDs or malformed relationships harmless: they can still break labels, names and state. Keep the target version visible when reporting findings. [WCAG version guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/).

## Real-World Decisions

Evaluate complete representative journeys in their consequential states: loading, errors, empty results, dialogs, expanded controls and success. Include keyboard operation, zoom/reflow, themes, text alternatives and supported assistive technology. Automated rules can expose repeatable defects, while human evaluation checks meaning, task completion and interaction.

A native button supplies useful semantics and activation behavior. A custom element with role=button still needs those behaviors implemented and tested. The [ARIA](50.2.02-aria.md) and [keyboard](50.2.03-keyboard-navigation.md) chapters explain the distinction.

## Common Mistakes

- Reporting an audit score as conformance without naming version, level and evaluated scope.
- Adding semantic attributes while leaving the task unusable.
- Fixing initial markup but excluding validation, overlays or third-party process steps.
- Removing important relationships because an obsolete criterion no longer appears.

## Best Practices

Set the target and supported environment, design the interaction around the user's task, and keep evidence of actual evaluation. Give findings an owner and a reproducible failing state. Recheck the affected journey after a fix; correcting a label cannot establish that its dialog focus also works.

## Summary

WCAG connects user barriers to testable criteria. Explain the failed task, implement the necessary relationships and behavior, then evaluate complete pages and processes against a named version and level. Automated findings support that evaluation alongside manual and assistive-technology checks.

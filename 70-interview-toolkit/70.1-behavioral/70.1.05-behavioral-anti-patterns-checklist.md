# Behavioral Anti-Patterns: The Self-Audit Checklist

## TL;DR

Most behavioral rounds are lost not on story selection but on **delivery defects**: rambling setups over 90 seconds, blame language, stories with no numbers, humble-bragging, generic answers that could belong to anyone, and conflict-free stories that signal you avoid hard things. This chapter is a diagnostic instrument: a defect catalog with fixes, a red-flag word list, recovery phrases for when you blank mid-answer, and a record-transcribe-grade drill protocol you can run solo or bolt onto mock loops.

## First Principles

Anti-patterns are classification errors evaluators are *trained* to catch:

1. **Every anti-pattern is a credibility leak**: blame-shifting (unowned failure), vagueness (unverified claim), "we" everywhere (no personal agency) — each maps to a doubt the interviewer now must resolve against you.
2. **The checklist works because stories are rehearsed artifacts**: auditing your bank against the failure modes *before* the interview is cheaper than discovering one live.

## Why it matters

Interviewers write debrief notes within minutes of you leaving. The notes contain quotes — and defects are more quotable than strengths. "Rambled," "blamed the PM," "no measurable outcome" are sentences that sink an otherwise qualified candidate because the interviewer cannot defend a hire against them. The good news: every common defect has a mechanical fix, and all of them are visible in a recording of yourself. Candidates who run even three graded practice sessions typically eliminate the top four defects; candidates who never audit themselves usually perform their defaults under pressure.

## Definition

**Behavioral anti-patterns** are the *delivery* defects that lose a round independently of story quality — the answer is judged on **evidence density and ownership signals**, and a technically impressive story can fail on how it was told.

```text
defect family        concrete symptom                        what it signals
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
structure            > 90-second setup before the action     weak prioritisation
unsupported claims   no numbers, no artefacts                unverifiable experience
ownership gaps       "we" throughout, blame language         no personal contribution
fake humility        humble-bragging, self-praise framing    low self-awareness
safe stories         no conflict, no failure, no risk        curated, therefore thin
rigidity             one rehearsed story forced onto any Q   memorisation, not judgment
```

Three properties define the discipline this file exists to enforce:

1. **Anti-patterns are observable, so they are correctable** — "rambles", "blames", "has no numbers" are each detectable in a recording, which is why the fix is a **drill protocol** (record, transcribe, mark the defect, re-deliver) rather than more reading.
2. **Ownership language is the highest-leverage fix** — replacing "we decided" with "I proposed, the team agreed because…" changes the signal at the same factual content; blame language ("the PM kept changing scope") inverts an otherwise good story into a red flag.
3. **Calibration comes from mocks, not from self-review** — the defect catalog is only actionable against a real question set under time pressure, because the failure mode is performance under stress (blanking, over-running, losing the thread), which self-study cannot surface.

The checklist's role in the toolkit is diagnostic: the story banks (70.1.01–70.1.04) supply the content, and this file supplies the delivery standard those stories are measured against.

## The Defect Catalog

Eight defects account for most failed behavioral rounds. Grade yourself against each.

### 1. Rambling (setups over 90 seconds)

**Symptom:** Situation + Task take 3+ minutes; the interviewer's eyes glaze before your Action starts. You include three projects' context when one sentence would do.

**Why it kills:** Interviewers score 4-6 competencies per answer. If setup eats the clock, Actions get compressed — and Actions carry the senior signal.

**Mechanical fix:** Budget by segment. Setup ≤ 45 seconds, Action 60-75 seconds, Result ≤ 30 seconds. Total target: 2 minutes. Write your setup as three sentences max:

```text
Sentence 1: where + role        ("I was tech lead on payments, 6 engineers")
Sentence 2: what was at stake   ("a migration our biggest client depended on")
Sentence 3: the tension         ("and we were 3 weeks behind with a hard date")
```

**Self-test:** Transcribe any answer. If Situation+Task exceed 150 words, cut until they don't.

### 2. Blame language

**Symptom:** Stories where someone else was the problem: "the PM kept changing scope," "management didn't listen," "the legacy team blocked us."

**Why it kills:** Interviewers assume they'd be the next person you complain about. One blame moment can override two strong stories.

**Mechanical fix:** Convert every actor into a constraint with legitimate interests. Not "the PM changed scope constantly" but "priorities shifted weekly because sales commitments drove the roadmap — which I understood, but it made fixed plans fragile." Same facts; ownership posture. The only acceptable failure-owners in your stories: you, your process, your communication.

### 3. Metric-free stories

**Symptom:** Results like "it went really well," "everyone was happy," "we shipped successfully." No numbers anywhere.

**Why it kills:** Without measurement, impact is unverifiable and the interviewer mentally downgrades the story to "claimed, not demonstrated."

**Mechanical fix:** Every story needs at least two numbers: one cost/effort number (time saved, incidents avoided, revenue) and one outcome number (% improvement, adoption, latency). If you genuinely lack metrics, use bounded estimates and label them: "roughly 30 engineer-hours a quarter — we estimated from ticket counts."

| Weak result | Metric-fixed result |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Adoption improved a lot" | "Weekly active usage went from 12% to 41% in two months" |
| "Reliability got better" | "Change-failure rate fell from 18% to 6% over two quarters" |
| "The team liked it" | "Onboarding survey scores rose from 3.1 to 4.6 out of 5" |

### 4. Humble-bragging

**Symptom:** Fake modesty: "I guess I just naturally tend to fix everyone's problems," or framing every story so you're flawlessly heroic. Also: taking sole credit for team outcomes.

**Why it kills:** Reads as low self-awareness; interviewers probe for cracks and find the story collapses under follow-up questions.

**Mechanical fix:** Include one genuine limitation or mistake per story, owned plainly. Name collaborators by contribution: "the designer caught what I missed here." Paradoxically, specific vulnerability raises credibility of every other claim.

### 5. Genericness

**Symptom:** Answers that fit any candidate at any company: "I communicated clearly and we worked together as a team." No names of technologies, no constraints, no texture.

**Why it kills:** Debrief notes need specifics to defend a hire. A generic answer gives them nothing to write except "fine."

**Mechanical fix:** Force three concrete nouns into every answer: a technology ("Kafka consumer lag"), a number ("40K events/sec"), a human detail ("our newest hire ran the migration script"). Specificity is memorability.

### 6. No-conflict stories

**Symptom:** Every narrative resolves smoothly; nobody ever disagreed; deadlines were never threatened. Or worse: "I've been lucky, I haven't really had conflicts."

**Why it kills:** Senior roles ARE conflict management. A conflict-free history signals avoidance or invisibility — both disqualifying for staff-level roles.

**Mechanical fix:** Audit your bank: every competency should have at least one story with genuine tension (disagreement, failure, ambiguity, missed deadline). If a real conflict doesn't exist, pick a smaller one honestly — a contested design decision counts.

### 7. Story-question mismatch

**Symptom:** You answer the question you rehearsed, not the one asked. Asked about influencing without authority, you tell your mentoring story because it's the one you know best.

**Why it kills:** Interviewers log "didn't answer the question" — it reads as either poor listening or a thin story bank being stretched.

**Mechanical fix:** Map every story to 3-4 competencies it legitimately covers (see the mapping table below). In the room: restate the competency silently, check the map, open with one line connecting them: "For influencing without authority, the clearest example is the testing-overhaul story."

### 8. The unfinished arc

**Symptom:** Stories that end at Action — you never state the Result, or the interviewer must extract it with "so what happened?"

**Why it kills:** Result is where seniority lives: measured impact plus institutionalized learning. Cutting it wastes everything before it.

**Mechanical fix:** End every answer with the same skeleton: "Result: [number]. And we kept [process/artifact] afterward, which [second-order effect]." Rehearse endings separately — they're the part candidates forget under pressure.

### Competency-to-story coverage matrix

Build this table for YOUR bank. Gaps show where mismatch risk lives.

| Competency | Story 1 | Story 2 | Story 3 | Covered? |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------- | -------- |
| Conflict / disagreement | Design-flow data test | Director mandate commit | Missed-commitment recovery | yes |
| Failure / growth | Graph DB tripwire | Hidden slippage | — | partial |
| Influence without authority | Testing overhaul | Notifications pilot | Identity consequence map | yes |
| Ambiguity / judgment | DB choice one-way door | Rules-based ranker | Deferred VP decision | yes |
| Delivery under pressure | Launch slice-and-flag | Quarter-end reporting | — | partial |

## Red-Flag Words and Phrases

Words that predict debrief damage. Audit transcripts for these:

| Category | Red flags | Replace with |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Blame | "they refused", "management forced", "the PM made us", "typical sales behavior" | "the constraint was", "priorities shifted because", "we aligned on" |
| Vague superlatives | "amazing", "huge win", "game-changer", "massive success" | the actual number |
| Absolutes | "always", "never", "everyone agreed", "nobody could" | "in that context", "the three teams involved" |
| Passive ownership | "it was decided", "things slipped", "the deadline moved" | "I recommended", "I missed", "I renegotiated" |
| Dismissiveness | "just a junior dev", "simple task", "obviously" | name the person's contribution; delete "obviously" |
| Humble-brag | "I guess I'm just good at", "luckily I happened to", "not to brag but" | plain statement of action taken |
| Victim framing | "I had no choice", "there was nothing I could do", "it wasn't my call" | "my options were X and Y; I chose Y because" |

Also flag filler patterns in transcripts: "kind of", "sort of", "you know", "like" appearing more than twice per minute. They don't fail interviews individually, but density above ~5% makes answers sound uncertain.

## Recovery Phrases When You Blank

You will blank at least once in a loop. What separates candidates is whether the silence becomes 8 awkward seconds or a controlled pause.

**When you lose the thread mid-story:**

```text
"Let me restart that last point cleanly."
"Stepping back — the key thing here was [core tension]."
"I'll come back to the details; the headline is [result]."
```

**When you don't have an experience for the question:**

```text
"I haven't faced exactly that. The closest situation I have
is [adjacent story] — want me to use that?"
"Honestly, that hasn't happened to me. Here's how I would
approach it if it did: [framework answer]."
```

**When the honest answer is negative (have you fired someone? led a reorg?):**

```text
"No — and I'd want to be upfront rather than bend a story.
Here's adjacent experience I do have: [X]."
```

**When you realize mid-answer the story contradicts something you said earlier:**

```text
"Let me correct something from earlier — the timeline there
was actually Q3, not Q2."
```

Owning a small correction instantly beats hoping nobody notices. Interviewers notice.

**Universal stall (buys 5 honest seconds):**

```text
"Good question — give me a second to pick the right example."
```

Used once per interview this reads as thoughtful. Used five times it reads as unprepared — which it is.

## The Practice Drill Protocol

Solo drill loop. One full cycle takes ~45 minutes and covers 4 stories.

```text
DRILL CYCLE (per story, ~10 min)
──────────────────────────────────────────────
1. PROMPT   pick a question at random (deck, friend, or
            random number → story list). No choosing
            favorites — randomness is the training.
2. RECORD   answer aloud, standing, timed. Camera on if
            possible. 2-minute soft cap.
3. TRANSCRIBE  auto-transcribe (phone voice memo apps,
            Whisper, OS dictation). Reading beats listening:
            defects hide in audio, glare in text.
4. GRADE    score against the checklist below.
5. RE-RECORD once with fixes. Compare transcriptions.
```

### The grading sheet

Score each recorded answer 0/1/2 per line (0 = failed, 1 = partial, 2 = clean):

```text
STRUCTURE
[ ] Setup ≤ 45 sec / 150 words          (0 1 2)
[ ] Explicit Task ("my job was...")     (0 1 2)
[ ] Actions are verbs you took          (0 1 2)
[ ] Result stated without prompting     (0 1 2)

CONTENT
[ ] ≥ 2 concrete numbers                (0 1 2)
[ ] ≥ 3 specific nouns (tech/human)     (0 1 2)
[ ] Real tension present                (0 1 2)
[ ] One owned limitation/mistake        (0 1 2)

LANGUAGE
[ ] Zero blame words                    (0 1 2)
[ ] Zero absolutes/superlatives         (0 1 2)
[ ] Filler rate < 5%                    (0 1 2)
[ ] Answered the question ASKED         (0 1 2)

DELIVERY
[ ] 90-150 seconds total                (0 1 2)
[ ] Ended with process/institutional    (0 1 2)
    learning

SCORE: __/28    Target: 24+ after 3 cycles
```

### Progression schedule

| Session | Focus | Success criterion |
| ------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 | Structure only | All setups ≤ 45 sec; results stated unprompted |
| 2 | Content | Numbers + specificity on every story; tension present |
| 3 | Language | Blame words gone from transcript; filler < 5% |
| 4 | Coverage | Random prompts across ALL competencies; no mismatches |
| 5 | Pressure | Answers standing up, after a fake coding round, tired |

Ten stories drilled once each beats three stories drilled ten times — variety trains retrieval, and retrieval is what fails in rooms.

## Pairing With Mock Loops

Solo drills build structure; mocks expose what drills can't — interaction under judgment. Use both:

**What solo drilling cannot catch:**

- Follow-up fragility: your story survives take one but collapses on "why not escalate?"
- Pace mismatch: interrupt-happy vs silent interviewers need different calibration.
- Energy: solo recordings feel lively at take one, flat at take twelve; mocks reveal your true room energy.
- Question interpretation under stress.

**Mock loop protocol (90 minutes):**

```text
1. Partner plays ONE interviewer archetype per session:
   - the driller      ("why?", "what exactly did YOU do?")
   - the skeptic      ("that sounds too clean", "really?")
   - the friendly one (lulls you into rambling)
2. 5 questions x 10 min including follow-ups. No mercy pauses.
3. Partner writes QUOTES (not summaries) — the exact phrases
   that would land in a debrief, good and bad.
4. Debrief 20 min: partner reads quotes back. Grade together
   with the same 14-line sheet.
5. Re-record the two worst answers within 24 hours while
   corrections are fresh.
```

Rotate partners: engineers grill differently than managers. One mock with a non-engineer is worth three with peers — non-engineers catch jargon dependence instantly.

**Cadence:** 2 solo cycles per week + 1 mock per week for the 3 weeks before a loop. Final 48 hours: no new stories; polish endings and follow-up answers only.

## Pre-Interview Final Check

Morning-of, 15 minutes:

1. Read your top 5 story ENDINGS only (results + institutionalized learnings).
2. Say the recovery phrases out loud once — priming matters.
3. Skim the red-flag word table; pick your personal top 3 offenders and consciously suppress them today.
4. Set your pace anchor: "setup in 3 sentences" written at the top of your notepad.
5. One glass of water within reach. Rambling correlates with dry mouth more than people admit.

## Summary

1. Eight defects cause most behavioral failures; all are mechanical and fixable with drills.
2. Cap setups at 45 seconds / 150 words; protect Action and Result time — that's where scoring happens.
3. Two numbers minimum per story; bounded estimates labeled as estimates are acceptable.
4. Zero blame language: convert actors into constraints with legitimate interests.
5. Keep one conflict/failure story per competency; conflict-free histories read as avoidance.
6. Memorize recovery phrases; controlled honesty about blanking beats improvised bluffing.
7. Run the record-transcribe-grade cycle — transcription exposes defects audio hides.
8. Pair solo drills with weekly mocks using quote-based feedback; rotate interviewer archetypes.

# Behavioral — Section Revision

Read the chapters in the [section learning flow](INDEX.md), then close them and work through these prompts. Explain the mechanism before checking an answer; return to the linked chapter when the reasoning is incomplete.

## Stakeholder Management Stories: Product Partnership, Influence, and Trade-off Communication

Source: [Stakeholder Management Stories: Product Partnership, Influence, and Trade-off Communication](70.1.01-stakeholder-management-stories.md)

**Q:** How can a stakeholder disagreement story show partnership without pretending everyone agreed?

**A:** State each constraint, the options and trade-off, how evidence changed the decision, who owned it, and the measured result. Distinguish compromise from unresolved risk.

## Leadership Questions: Decisions, Influence & Team Outcomes

Source: [Leadership Questions: Decisions, Influence & Team Outcomes](70.1.02-leadership-questions.md)

**Q:** What keeps a STAR example credible when adapting it to a leadership question?

**A:** Use your actual role, decisions, evidence, and outcome. Sample stories demonstrate structure; they should not be presented as experiences you did not have.

## Judgment Calls & Ambiguity: Deciding Under Incomplete Data

Source: [Judgment Calls & Ambiguity: Deciding Under Incomplete Data](70.1.03-judgment-calls-ambiguity.md)

**Q:** How do you explain a decision made with incomplete information?

**A:** State what information is missing, which consequences can or cannot be reversed, how you will decide with the available evidence, what feedback you will monitor, and when you would revise the decision.

## Technical Decision Stories: From Constraint to Verified Outcome

Source: [Technical Decision Stories: From Constraint to Verified Outcome](70.1.04-system-design-behavioral.md)

**Q:** What does a technical-decision story need beyond the selected technology?

**A:** The binding requirements, rejected options and trade-offs, migration/operational consequences, your contribution, and evidence of the result.

## Behavioral Anti-Patterns: Make the Evidence Easy to Follow

Source: [Behavioral Anti-Patterns: Make the Evidence Easy to Follow](70.1.05-behavioral-anti-patterns-checklist.md)

**Q:** Why can a polished story still be a weak answer?

**A:** If it does not answer the prompt or identify your contribution and result, its structure cannot establish judgment. Use the checklist to expose missing links; do not add invented conflict or metrics.

## Summary

Choose real experiences, identify your contribution, and check that each claimed result has supporting evidence. Treat uncertainty and disagreement as decision inputs rather than gaps to conceal.

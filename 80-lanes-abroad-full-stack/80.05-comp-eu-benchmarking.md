# Compensation Benchmarking — Compare the Actual Offer

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

Two senior offers can both say "€110,000 total compensation" while leaving completely different cash in your bank account every month: one pays €105k guaranteed base plus €5k bonus, while the other pays €85k base plus €25k in illiquid startup options that may expire worthless if you leave early. Comparing European offers cleanly means breaking every package into guaranteed cash, conditional pay, equity risk, and real post-tax household costs.

> **Formal definition:** Compensation benchmarking compares the components and conditions of pay against dated evidence from comparable roles, levels, and employment markets.

## First Principles

Gross salary, net take-home pay, and speculative startup equity measure three different things. Collapsing them into one headline number hides tax differences, currency risk, and liquidity risk. Likewise, a country's legal visa salary threshold is a **minimum immigration floor**, never a fair market benchmark for a senior engineer.

## 1. Gather Clean, Comparable Evidence

Start by asking the recruiter for the role's **budgeted band, level, and location** during the first screen. Supplement that with dated data from comparable companies and roles (remembering that self-reported sites vary in sample size and can skew toward public tech companies).

Always record five qualifiers alongside every figure in your private workspace:
1. **Currency and date:** London pays in British pounds (GBP), Zurich in Swiss francs (CHF), Stockholm in Swedish kronor (SEK), and Eurozone hubs in euros (EUR) — convert only with a dated exchange rate. (The United Kingdom (UK) and Switzerland are not European Union (EU) member states.)
2. **Employment type:** Full-time employee (`W-2` or local payroll with employer social contributions and paid leave) versus contractor or freelancer (higher gross rate, zero paid leave or employer benefits).
3. **Company stage:** Public tech (liquid Restricted Stock Units (RSUs)) versus Series A–C startup (options or Virtual Stock Option Plan (VSOP)).
4. **Role level:** Senior Individual Contributor (IC), Staff IC, or Tech Lead.
5. **City and tax jurisdiction:** Net take-home differs sharply between Berlin, Amsterdam, Dublin, Zurich, and London.

## 2. Break Every Offer Into Six Separate Buckets

| Component | What to confirm in writing before comparing |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Base salary** | Annual gross figure, currency, 12 versus 13 or 14 monthly installments, and contracted weekly hours |
| **Performance bonus** | Target percentage versus guaranteed floor, payout formula, and whether year 1 is prorated |
| **Public-company RSUs** | Share count or dollar value, 4-year vesting schedule with quarterly cadence, and tax withholding |
| **Private startup equity (Employee Stock Ownership Plan (ESOP) or VSOP)** | Strike price, fully diluted valuation, dilution risk, post-termination exercise window, and exit liquidity |
| **Statutory and extra benefits** | Employer pension match, public versus private health insurance split, paid vacation days, on-call pay |
| **Relocation support** | Covered items, gross versus net reimbursement, cash advance timing, dependant coverage, and clawback period |

### Worked example: reading one offer honestly

Suppose an employer offers **€80,000 base plus €8,000 target bonus (10%) plus €5,000 sign-on bonus**:
- **Guaranteed recurring gross cash:** €80,000 per year.
- **Target recurring gross cash (if bonus pays 100%):** €88,000 per year.
- **Year-1 target gross cash (including one-time sign-on):** €93,000 — remembering year 2 drops back by €5,000 unless base increases.
- **Private startup options or VSOP:** Model separately across zero-exit, modest-exit, and strong-exit scenarios; never add an unvested private percentage straight onto base salary (see [Find Startup Roles](../00-strategy/00.05-find-startup-roles.md) for the full dilution math).

## 3. Stress-Test Your Household Budget Before Accepting

Gross pay tells you nothing until you run it through the country's current tax and social-contribution calculator for your exact tax class and household setup. Then subtract realistic local quotes for:
- Rent plus upfront deposit (often 2–3 months' cold rent in Germany and the Netherlands)
- Health insurance contributions and local transit
- Childcare, schooling, or private tutoring (never assume immediate free spots before checking local waitlists)
- Moving costs and an emergency buffer (reimbursements arrive *after* you pay deposits out of pocket)

Model **two** scenarios in your private notes: a base case, and a downside case where a partner's job search takes 6 months longer than planned or a start date slips due to visa processing.

## 4. Netherlands 30% Ruling: Conditional Tax Break, Not Guaranteed Extra Pay

If you compare a Dutch offer against a German or Irish one, verify the exact **expat tax scheme** rules rather than assuming a flat 30% tax cut:

- **2026 minimum taxable salary:** More than **€48,013 excluding the untaxed allowance** for the standard expertise category (different thresholds apply for eligible master's graduates under 30). Distance and prior-residence rules plus a valid tax decision also apply, and employers are not legally forced to pass on the full allowance. Duration is up to five years, minus relevant earlier stays. [Dutch Tax Administration](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/coming-to-work-in-the-netherlands-30-percent-facility)
- **2026 cap and 2027 step-down:** The untaxed allowance is capped at **€78,600 per year** (based on a €262,000 salary ceiling; prorated for partial years). The maximum rate stays **30% in 2026** and generally steps down to **27% from 2027**, subject to transition rules depending on when the ruling started. [Dutch government transition rules](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/inkomstenbelasting/belastingvoordeel-buitenlandse-werknemers)

Always compare the offer **with and without** the expat allowance so your long-term budget survives once the ruling expires.

## 5. Negotiate the Constraint That Actually Moves the Needle

Once you know where the gap sits (base salary, upfront moving cash, level, or remote flexibility), raise one specific, evidence-backed point calmly:

> *"Thank you for the offer. Based on the system ownership we discussed and comparable senior roles in this market, my main priority is base salary. Is there flexibility to move base to €X, or to bridge year one with a signing or relocation allowance?"*

Mention a competing offer only when you genuinely hold one, and confirm every agreed term **in writing** before resigning — a verbal *"we'll review your salary in six months"* is not a contract clause. See the [Visa & Relocation Playbook](80.06-visa-relocation-playbook.md) for statutory immigration thresholds and [Salary Negotiation Guide](80.31-salary-negotiation-guide.md) for full counter-offer scripts.

## Summary

Benchmark European offers with dated, like-for-like evidence in the right currency, and separate guaranteed recurring base, conditional bonus, one-time sign-on, and risky private equity before comparing totals. Run net take-home and household cash-flow budgets under both base and delayed-start scenarios, treat tax concessions like the Dutch expat ruling as conditional, and negotiate the specific term that solves your real constraint in writing.

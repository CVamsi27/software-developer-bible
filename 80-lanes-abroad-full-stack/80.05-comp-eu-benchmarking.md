# Compensation Benchmarking — Compare the Actual Offer

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



Compensation depends on the employer, level, employment type, location, and date. Build a dated comparison from role-specific evidence; a city label or unsourced company range cannot establish a current market band.

> **Formal definition:** Compensation benchmarking compares the components and conditions of pay against dated evidence from comparable roles, levels and employment markets.

## First Principles

Gross pay, spendable income, and speculative equity measure different things. Converting them into one number without assumptions hides risk. A visa salary threshold is an eligibility constraint, not evidence that an offer is competitive or that sponsorship is guaranteed.

## Collect comparable evidence

Start with the employer's published band and ask which level and location apply. Supplement it with dated observations from similar roles. Record currency, base, bonus target, equity type, grant date, and whether the person is an employee or contractor. Self-reported data can be selective; a handful of observations is not the whole market.

Keep the actual figures, sources, dates, and negotiation notes in the [private canonical workspace](personal/README.md). Refresh them before a negotiation. London uses GBP, Zurich CHF, and Stockholm SEK; converting to EUR is useful only with a dated exchange rate. The UK and Switzerland are not EU member states.

## Compare cash and equity separately

| Component | Establish before comparing |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Base | Annual gross amount, guaranteed payments, currency, contracted hours |
| Bonus | Target versus guaranteed amount, payout conditions, joining-year treatment |
| Public-company equity | Number of units, vesting schedule, tax treatment, price sensitivity |
| Private-company options | Strike price, fully diluted share count/date, dilution, exercise window, liquidity and tax risks |
| Benefits | Employee deductions, employer pension, insurance, leave, on-call compensation |
| Relocation | Reimbursement limits, cash advance, dependant coverage, repayment clauses |

A private option percentage cannot be added to base as annual cash compensation. Model it separately, including a zero-liquidity outcome. A signing payment is one-time; spread it only when showing an explicit comparison horizon.

For a hypothetical offer of €80,000 base, €8,000 target bonus, and €5,000 signing payment, guaranteed recurring gross cash is €80,000. Target first-year gross cash is €93,000 if the bonus pays fully. Neither figure is net income, and the bonus and signing payment need their own conditions.

## Build a household budget from actual quotes

Use the payroll estimate for the relevant tax year and circumstances. Then account for rent, deposit, insurance contributions, transport, childcare or schooling, moving costs, and an emergency reserve. Do not assume free healthcare, a school place, subsidized childcare, or a second household income before eligibility and availability are confirmed.

Compare a base-case budget with a delayed job start or a second earner not finding work immediately. Keep financial preferences and family information in private notes.

## Dutch expat scheme: conditional, not a guaranteed net uplift

The scheme permits an untaxed employer allowance under conditions; it does not mean every migrant receives 30% of salary tax-free. For 2026, the ordinary expertise salary requirement is **more than €48,013 excluding the untaxed allowance**. Other categories have different rules. Distance/history requirements and a valid decision also apply, and an employer is not obliged to pay the full allowance. The decision can last up to five years, reduced by relevant earlier residence or work. [Dutch Tax Administration](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/coming-to-work-in-the-netherlands-30-percent-facility)

In 2026, the allowance is also capped at €78,600 for a full year, based on a €262,000 salary ceiling; part-year use reduces that limit. The maximum percentage remains 30% in 2026 and generally becomes 27% in 2027, with transition rules depending on when the decision began. Confirm eligibility, allocation between employer and employee, and the payroll calculation; do not promise a fixed annual net gain. [Dutch government transition rules](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/inkomstenbelasting/belastingvoordeel-buitenlandse-werknemers)

## Negotiate the constraint that matters

Ask for the budgeted band and explain the role evidence behind your request. Base, level, signing payment, equity, relocation, and working arrangements may have different flexibility. There is no universal rule to counter only on equity, always counter, or never counter without another offer.

A useful response is: “Thank you. Based on the responsibilities and the comparable roles I researched, I would like to discuss [specific term]. Is there flexibility there, or in [alternative]?” Mention a competing offer only if it exists, and state its relevant terms accurately.

Get the final package and conditions in writing before accepting. A promised review is not a guaranteed raise. See the [relocation guide](80.06-visa-relocation-playbook.md) for route-specific salary and employer requirements; do not copy visa thresholds into market-pay tables.

## Summary

Compare dated, like-for-like role evidence. Separate recurring cash, conditional payments, equity risk, payroll estimates, and relocation costs. Negotiate from actual constraints and documented terms.

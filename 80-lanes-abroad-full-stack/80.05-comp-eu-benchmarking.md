# Compensation Benchmarking — EU Mid-Stage

> **EU comp is structured very differently from US FAANG.** Base is lower, equity is lower, but 30% ruling + lower cost of living + social safety net can put EU net comp surprisingly close to US numbers. This file is the 5-tier city comp table + negotiation scripts.

## TL;DR

- **Tier 1 cities (Amsterdam, Berlin, Munich, London):** EUR 75-100K base + 0.05-0.5% equity for senior at mid-stage
- **Tier 2 (Helsinki, Stockholm, Copenhagen):** EUR 65-90K
- **Tier 3 (Vienna, Lisbon, Warsaw, remote-EU):** EUR 60-85K
- **Tier 4 (Paris, Madrid, Rome):** EUR 70-100K
- **Premium (London, Zurich):** GBP 90-130K / CHF 120-160K
- **Always counter on equity, not base.** Base is usually fixed; equity has more flex.
- **NL 30% ruling: 30% of salary tax-free for 5 years.** Worth EUR 15-25K/year net.
- **EU Blue Card minimum: ~EUR 48,300/year (2024-25).** All Tier 1-4 base salaries clear this.

## The NL 30% Ruling (huge deal)

The Netherlands has a **30% ruling** for highly skilled migrants: 30% of your salary is tax-free for 5 years.

**For a EUR 90K base:**
- Without ruling: ~EUR 65K net (after tax)
- With ruling: ~EUR 75K net (after tax)
- **Boost: ~EUR 10K/year net** (more in higher brackets)

To qualify:
- Recruited from abroad (you must be moving TO NL)
- Have a job offer with base > EUR 5,331/month (2024)
- Have specific skills in shortage occupation list (IT qualifies)

**This is a EUR 50K+ net benefit over 5 years.** When negotiating, ask: "Is the offer inclusive of the 30% ruling, or exclusive?" Companies will sometimes quote a higher gross so the ruling is built in.

## EU Blue Card Minimum (2024-25)

The EU Blue Card requires a minimum salary threshold:

- **Standard:** EUR 4,025/month (EUR 48,300/year)
- **IT shortage occupation:** Lower threshold applies, often EUR 3,909/month

All Tier 1-4 base salaries above this easily. The Blue Card gives you:
- Faster processing (4-12 weeks vs 6+ months)
- Family inclusion (spouse gets work permit)
- Path to permanent residency after 18 months
- Mobility: can move to other EU countries after 18 months

## Family Comp Considerations

When comparing offers, factor in:

| Factor | Tier 1 impact | Notes |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Spouse job market** | NL/UK/DE have strong Pega/consulting/IT | Make sure your spouse's career works in the city |
| **International school** | EUR 15-30K/year per child | Amsterdam, London, Munich all have good options |
| **Housing** | Munich > Amsterdam > Berlin > Helsinki | Rent in Tier 1 is 30-50% of net, often more |
| **Healthcare** | Included in social system, mostly free | EU has universal healthcare, US doesn't |
| **Childcare** | Subsidized or free in most EU countries | Better than US for working parents |
| **Public transport** | Excellent in Tier 1 | Often a company perk |
| **30% ruling** | NL only, but huge | See above |
| **Pension** | Often 5-10% employer contribution | On top of base |

**Total comp adjustment for family in Tier 1:**
- Spouse working (if applicable): +EUR 50-80K household
- International school: -EUR 20K/year
- Housing: -EUR 15-25K/year
- Childcare: -EUR 0-5K/year (subsidized in most EU)
- 30% ruling: +EUR 10K/year net
- Pension: +EUR 5-10K/year (employer contrib)

## Negotiation Scripts

### When they ask "What are your salary expectations?"

**Don't go first if you can avoid it.** Say:

> "I'm flexible based on the full package. Based on my research, senior full-stack roles in [city] are in the EUR X-Y range base + 0.0Z% equity. I'm more interested in the right role and team. Can you share the budgeted range for this position?"

If pressed:

> "For a senior full-stack role in Berlin, my target is EUR 85-95K base + meaningful equity. I'm open to negotiating based on equity, bonus, and benefits like relocation."

**For Netherlands specifically, add:**

> "I'm also relocating from India with my family, and the 30% ruling is a major factor for me. Can you confirm whether your offer is inclusive or exclusive of the 30% ruling?"

### When they make an offer

**Always counter. Always.** Even if the offer is good, counter. The first offer is rarely the final offer.

**Counter structure:**

> "Thank you for the offer — I'm very excited about [specific thing]. Based on my research and competing opportunities, I'd like to discuss the base salary. I was thinking [X% higher] or [higher absolute number]. I'm also interested in [more equity / signing bonus / relocation package / 30% ruling clarification]. Can we discuss?"

### When you have a competing offer

**Use it as leverage, but don't bluff.**

> "I have a competing offer at [Y] that I'm seriously considering. It's [Y% higher in base / more equity / better location]. I prefer [this company] because [specific reason], but the compensation gap is meaningful. Can you match or come closer?"

### When they say "this is our top of band"

> "I understand. Can we get creative on the package? Options I'm open to: signing bonus, additional equity, accelerated vesting, more vacation, relocation budget, education stipend, 30% ruling clarification (for NL), or a 6-month performance review for a base adjustment."

## When to NOT negotiate

- If the offer is already at the top of band for the role
- If you have no competing offers and no leverage
- If the role is your "dream role" and comp is secondary
- If you're being paid in equity at a hot company (early-stage, big upside)

**When to ALWAYS negotiate:**
- You have a competing offer (even a verbal one)
- The base is below the median for the role and city
- The equity is below 0.05%
- The relocation package is bare-bones
- The signing bonus is zero

## The Closing Script

When you accept:

> "Thank you for the offer. I'm excited to join [company] as [role]. I've reviewed the package, discussed with my family, and I'm happy to accept. Could you send the formal offer letter with all the details we've discussed, including the 30% ruling clarification [if NL] and the relocation timeline?"

When you decline (keep the door open):

> "Thank you for the offer and the time. After careful consideration, I've decided to pursue [other opportunity] which is a better fit for my [specific reason: family location, comp, role]. I have great respect for [company] and would love to stay in touch. If anything changes in 6-12 months, I'd welcome a conversation."

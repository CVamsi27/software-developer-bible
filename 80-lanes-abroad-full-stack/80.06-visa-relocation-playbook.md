# Visa & Relocation Playbook — Eight European Countries

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

Receiving a European job offer does not automatically mean you can legally start on your preferred date or afford the move on day one. An international move requires three separate conditions to line up at the same time: a **qualifying job offer**, **legal residence and work permission**, and **enough upfront cash for deposits and transition months**.

This guide compares work-visa routes across eight European destinations. Use [Compensation Benchmarking](80.05-comp-eu-benchmarking.md) for household budgets, and keep personal relocation decisions in your private workspace.

## First Principles

1. **Eligibility rules are legal gates, not flexible human resources (HR) policies.** Missing a degree recognition check, a salary floor, or an approved sponsor license can block a visa even when the hiring team wants you urgently.
2. **Entry, residence, and work rights are separate permissions.** A tourist or visitor visa never grants work permission, and a partner's right to work depends on their own dependent residence status.
3. **Cash goes out before your first local paycheck arrives.** Security deposits, flights, insurance, and embassy wait times require accessible savings up front even when an employer promises later reimbursement.

All figures below are **minimum statutory route thresholds** checked against official government sources on **2026-10-09** — they are legal eligibility floors, not competitive senior salary targets or processing-time guarantees. Six countries are European Union (EU) members; the **United Kingdom (UK)** and **Switzerland** operate independent immigration systems. Always recheck the official page for your application date and exact occupation.

## Country-by-Country Details

### Germany

- **EU Blue Card:** 2026 gross annual thresholds are **€50,700** (standard) and **€45,934.20** for eligible shortage occupations (including IT specialists and recent entrants, subject to Federal Employment Agency approval). You still need a recognized degree (or qualifying IT experience under the specialist track) and a job contract lasting at least 6 months.

  Employers do not need a UK-style sponsor license, though they must submit employment forms. Permanent settlement can be reached after **27 months with A1 German** or **21 months with B1 German** once all other conditions are met. [Official Blue Card guide](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card)
- **Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card):** A job-search residence permit, **not** blanket permission for immediate full-time work. Recognized skilled workers qualify directly; otherwise, the points track requires baseline qualification and language criteria plus at least 6 points. University recognition alone does not prove your specific degree matches. [Official route and self-check](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card)
  - **2026 subsistence proof:** **€1,091 per month** via a blocked account, formal commitment declaration, or qualifying part-time job (which can be topped up with blocked funds). A blocked account is for the Opportunity Card, not a general Blue Card requirement. Weigh the out-of-pocket living runway carefully against interviewing remotely from your current country. [Official funding FAQ](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/opportunity-card/questions-answers)

### Netherlands

- **Highly Skilled Migrant (HSM):** Normally requires an **IND-recognized sponsor**. For a first application in 2026, gross monthly thresholds (excluding 8% holiday allowance) are **€5,942 (age 30+)** and **€4,357 (under 30)**, with a reduced threshold for eligible recent graduates. Extensions and employer switches carry their own transition rules. [IND income requirements](https://ind.nl/en/required-amounts-income-requirements)
- **Expat Tax Ruling:** Applied for separately from the visa. Maximum untaxed allowance remains **30% in 2026** and steps down to **27% in 2027** subject to transition rules (see [Compensation Benchmarking](80.05-comp-eu-benchmarking.md) for caps). [Dutch government expat scheme](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/werk/inkomstenbelasting/belastingvoordeel-buitenlandse-werknemers)

### Ireland

- **Critical Skills Employment Permit:** Requires a job offer lasting at least **2 years**. Standard listed-occupation floor is **€40,904**; the higher general-occupation track requires **over €68,911**. Stamp 4, long-term residency, and citizenship are separate applications — do not treat "2 years to permanent residence" as automatic. [Critical Skills requirements](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/permit-types/critical-skills-employment-permit/)
- **Partner work rights:** Eligible spouses and de facto partners may receive **Stamp 1G**, permitting salaried employment without a separate work permit (excluding self-employment or running a business). [Irish immigration permission conditions](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/registering-your-immigration-permission/information-on-registering/immigration-permission-Stamps/)

### Sweden

- **Work Permit:** For first-time applications decided on or after **1 June 2026**, salary must normally reach **90% of the Swedish median wage** at submission time (**SEK 34,470 per month** as published) and match Swedish collective-agreement or standard industry terms. [Migration Agency salary requirements](https://www.migrationsverket.se/en/word-explanations/salary-requirements-for-a-work-permit.html)

### Finland

- **Specialist or EU Blue Card:** 2026 floor is **€3,937 gross per month** (fringe benefits and daily allowances excluded). [Official income requirements](https://migri.fi/en/working-in-finland/income-requirement)
- **Permanent residence update (January 2026):** As of **8 January 2026**, permanent residence is no longer a simple 4-year rule — routes include a 6-year path with work-history and language requirements. [Official 2026 permanent-residence changes](https://migri.fi/en/-/aliens-act-s-provisions-on-permanent-residence-permit-amended-as-of-8-january-2026?languageId=en_US)

### United Kingdom

- **Skilled Worker Visa:** Requires a licensed sponsor and must meet **both £41,700 per year and the occupation's going rate** (whichever is higher; discounts apply only to specific qualifying categories). Ignore outdated blog claims of a universal £33,000 IT shortage floor. [UK Skilled Worker salary rules](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-job)

### Switzerland

- **Non-EU and EFTA Permits:** Subject to strict cantonal quotas, labor-market precedence (proving no Swiss, EU, or EFTA worker could fill the role), and specialist criteria. There is no universal Swiss francs (CHF) 95,000 floor or guaranteed 5-year B permit. [Swiss admission criteria](https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/arbeit/nicht-eu_efta-angehoerige/grundlagen_zur_arbeitsmarktzulassung.html)

### Austria

- **Red-White-Red Card (Other Key Workers):** 2026 minimum is **€3,465 gross per month**, plus at least **55 points** and a labor-market test (other Red-White-Red categories have different rules). [Official Other Key Workers route](https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/other-key-workers/)

## 4-Factor Household Decision Framework

Shortlist 2–3 cities using concrete evidence instead of arbitrary point scores:

1. **Both careers:** Live English-speaking openings, local language expectations, and your partner's legal work rights from day one.
2. **Schooling and childcare:** Real waitlists, local public-school language bridges versus international-school fees, and commute times.
3. **Cash-flow runway:** Net take-home pay, upfront rental deposits, insurance, and a 3–6 month buffer if one income starts later.
4. **Paperwork dependencies:** Degree recognition (`Anabin` and ZAB), sponsor license status, family civil documents (apostilled marriage and birth certificates), and embassy slot wait times.

## Relocation Package — What to Clarify and Negotiate

Relocation support is a negotiated offer term, not a legal entitlement. Ask for a written breakdown covering:
- **Immigration and legal fees** (for both you and dependants)
- **One-way flights and shipping** (lump sum versus receipt reimbursement, gross versus net after tax)
- **Temporary furnished housing** (30–90 days while hunting for a long-term lease and registering locally)
- **Start-date flexibility** (what happens if the embassy takes 4 extra weeks)
- **Clawback clause** (exact prorated repayment rule if you leave within 12–24 months)

## Realistic Relocation Dependency Chain (Why "90 Days" Is a Scenario, Not a Promise)

```text
1. Verify route & degree recognition (Anabin and ZAB)
       ─► 2. Sign contract (with lawful-start contingency clause)
            ─► 3. Book embassy slot & submit complete documents
                 ─► 4. Receive visa / entry permission
                      ─► 5. Travel, start work & complete local registration (Anmeldung / BSN / IRP)
```

Housing research and document apostilles can run in parallel, yet **never pay non-refundable rent or resign your current job** until you understand your embassy timeline and written start-date contingencies.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Refusing to sign a contract before visa approval:** Most embassies require a signed employment contract to *file* the visa. Instead, include a clause stating your start date adjusts automatically if visa issuance takes longer.
- **Underestimating upfront liquidity:** Reimbursed moving expenses still come out of your bank account first.
- **Confusing residence years with automatic permanent residency:** Language certificates, pension contributions, and continuous employment all count alongside years lived.

## Summary

Line up all three gates — a qualifying offer, a legal work route, and an upfront household runway — before committing to a European move.

Verify statutory salary thresholds and sponsor rules directly on each country's official portal for your exact application date. Model both careers and upfront deposits realistically, negotiate relocation terms and visa-delay start clauses in writing, and plan around concrete appointment milestones rather than an assumed 90-day calendar.

# Company Profiles — Research the Employer and the Role

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



A profile is a dated evidence sheet for a specific employer and opening. Company-wide stack lists, unsupported salary bands, and assumed interview questions can misdirect preparation. Use the shortlist in the [private targets](personal/README.md) for current priorities, and this guide for the research method.

## First Principles

A company may contain several products, teams, legal entities, and hiring processes. Evidence about one team does not automatically apply to another. A profile must therefore attach each claim to its scope, source, and date, while keeping unanswered questions visible.

## Establish identity before researching technology

Match the job URL, employer domain, legal entity, and location. Similar names can refer to unrelated businesses. For example, the [Thryve health company](https://www.thryve.health/about) describes wearable health-data integration; the earlier profile's Hetzner acquisition and open-source data-tooling claims were unsupported. Do not transfer those claims to a similarly named recruiter or software vendor.

An acquisition can also change the hiring entity. [Langfuse's announcement](https://langfuse.com/blog/joining-clickhouse) says it joined ClickHouse; a profile should distinguish the product from the employing organization rather than keep treating the old startup stage as current.

## Build one evidence sheet

| Field | Strong starting evidence | What remains to confirm |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product and users | Official product documentation and dated announcements | Which product the hiring team owns |
| Role and stack | Current job description, scoped repository/docs | Versions, responsibilities, production ownership |
| Interview | Written recruiter guidance for this opening | Rounds, time limits, tools, evaluation format |
| Work arrangement | Job location and employer policy for this role | Permitted work country, office attendance, contract type |
| Sponsorship | Recruiter's explicit answer and route requirements | Sponsoring entity, dependants, support and timelines |
| Compensation | Published band or written recruiter information | Level, currency, bonus/equity terms and eligibility |
| Business context | Dated company releases or filings | Which claims matter to the role; current uncertainty |

Record the source URL, observation date, and confidence beside each fact. A blog post demonstrates what its authors worked on; it does not prove what the current interview will ask. An open-source repository demonstrates that project's technology, not necessarily every internal service.

## Use a bounded research pass

For a thirty-minute first pass, spend five minutes checking identity and the opening, ten reading relevant product or engineering material, five checking work and sponsorship terms, five researching compensation, and five writing facts and unresolved questions. Go deeper on a specific unresolved decision before applying or interviewing.

Do not treat headcount, valuation, funding stage, or a claimed four-day week as current without a dated source. Recruiter-confirmed conditions for the actual role are more useful than an impressive company-wide statistic.

## Turn research into an honest motivation

Choose one product or technical decision you inspected, explain why it connects to your experience, and identify what you want to learn about the team. For example: “Your documentation describes [verified capability]. I worked on [actual related problem], so I would like to understand how this team handles [specific trade-off].”

Do not invent an engineering post, employee conversation, or recent launch. Keep names and outreach notes in the private target record, and verify the answer when the opening changes.

## Summary

Research the exact employer and role. Store dated facts once, keep uncertain claims explicit, and let the confirmed loop guide preparation.

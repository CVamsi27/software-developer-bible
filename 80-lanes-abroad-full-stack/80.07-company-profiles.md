# Company Profiles — Tier 1 & 2 EU Mid-Stage

> **1-pager per company** to read before the recruiter screen or hiring manager call. Focus: what they build, what stack they use, what they test, what comp they pay, who the team is.

## TL;DR

- **Tier 1 (6 target companies):** n8n, SumUp, Scalable Capital, sennder, THRYVE, Langfuse
- **Tier 2 (5 stretch companies):** Peter Park, Hostaway, GoGlobal, Booking.com, Spotify
- **Each profile:** 1-page with stack, interview process, comp, who to follow, recent news
- **Read 1-2 the night before your interview, and do 30 min of research before each one.** Work the 6 steps above, and write a 60-sec "Why this company?" answer per target.

---

## First Principles

1. **Startup risk profiles decompose into funding stage × engineering maturity × visa track record** — a Series A with 2 senior engineers and no sponsored hires before you is a different bet than a Series D with a relocation playbook; the profile sheet exists to make those axes comparable.
2. **Engineering blog + tech-talk trail predicts the interview loop better than the job ad** — what they publish reveals what they value measuring (→ 80.34's domain primer); profile research is cheap and compounds across every round.

## Tier 1 — Primary Targets

### n8n Berlin (EU-remote)

**What they do:** Open-source workflow automation platform. Self-hostable alternative to Zapier. 200K+ users, $5.2B valuation (2025), Series C.

**Stack:** TypeScript, Vue.js, NestJS, PostgreSQL, Redis, BullMQ for queues, Docker, Kubernetes, n8n.io for the platform itself (meta).

**What they test in interviews:**
- Take-home: small workflow engine, REST + minimal UI
- System design: workflow execution engine, event-driven system, state recovery
- Behavioral: async-first, English fluency, "Why n8n?"

**Comp:** EUR 80-100K base + 0.05-0.2% equity + 4-day workweek + remote-first.

**Team:** ~150 people, growing fast, fully remote in EU.

**Who to follow:** Jan Oberhauser (CEO, very active on X), engineering blog.

**Recent news (2024-25):** Series C $180M, valuation $5.2B, AI workflow features.

**Why you'd want to work here:** Open-source DNA, strong engineering culture, hot product, remote-first, 4-day workweek.

## How to research a company before the interview (30 min)

1. **Engineering blog (10 min):** Read 2-3 most recent posts. Look for: tech stack mentions, scale numbers, recent incidents, technical decisions.
2. **GitHub org (5 min):** Look at repos, recent commits, open issues. Tells you what they actually build vs what they say.
3. **LinkedIn (5 min):** Look at 5-10 engineers on the team. Where did they come from? What's their background? Find common ground for small talk.
4. **Levels.fyi + Glassdoor (5 min):** Real comp data. Set your anchor.
5. **TechCrunch / news (5 min):** Recent funding, product launches, leadership changes. Have a question ready.
6. **The role description (5 min):** Re-read it. Note every "must have" and "nice to have". Be ready to address each.

## The "Why this company?" 60-second answer template

For each company you apply to, write a 60-second answer to "Why [company]?" that includes:

1. **1 product feature or engineering blog post that excited you** (specific, not generic)
2. **1 specific person/team you'd learn from** (LinkedIn name + why)
3. **1 thing about the company culture that aligns with how you work** (async, ownership, etc.)
4. **Close with a recent product/launch** you're excited about

**Example for n8n:**
> "I read your engineering blog post on the new execution engine — the way you handled state recovery on partial failure is exactly the kind of distributed systems work I want to do. I'm also excited about your new AI workflow features based on what I saw in the recent changelog. Your culture of async-first communication + the 4-day workweek aligns with how I work best. And I'm particularly excited about the recent [Workflow Code] launch — that's the kind of feature that opens a new category."

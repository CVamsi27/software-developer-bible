# Company Profiles — Research the Employer and the Role

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

Preparing for an interview by reading a 2-year-old Glassdoor thread or assuming "everyone at Company X uses Go and asks LeetCode Hard" wastes hours on the wrong topics. Different teams inside the same company run different stacks, report to different legal entities, and run different interview loops.

A strong **company profile** is a tight, 30-minute, dated evidence sheet built around **one specific opening** — keeping verified facts separate from questions you still need to ask. Use [Find Startup Roles](../00-strategy/00.05-find-startup-roles.md) to discover live postings on employer Applicant Tracking System (ATS) boards first, then profile only the roles worth pursuing.

> **Formal definition:** Employer research is the collection and evaluation of scoped evidence about an organization and a specific role to support an application or employment decision.

## First Principles

A company brand hides multiple products, subsidiaries, and teams. Evidence about one product (or one acquired startup) does not automatically describe another team. Every claim in your profile therefore needs three tags: **what scope it covers**, **its source Uniform Resource Locator (URL) and date**, and **what remains unconfirmed**.

## 1. Verify Legal Identity First (Avoid Lookalikes and Acquisition Traps)

Before reading engineering blogs, match the job URL, employer domain, legal entity, and city:
- **Name collisions:** Unrelated companies often share a brand name. For example, [Thryve](https://www.thryve.health/about) builds wearable health-data integrations — do not mix its product profile with a similarly named staffing firm or Software as a Service (SaaS) vendor.
- **Acquisitions change who employs you:** [Langfuse announced joining ClickHouse](https://langfuse.com/blog/joining-clickhouse); researching it as a standalone seed startup would give you the wrong expectations about entity, scale, and compensation structure.

## 2. The 30-Minute Company Profile Template

Spend **30 minutes** before applying or taking a recruiter screen — not three hours:

| Time | Field | Where to check | What to ask the recruiter later |
| ------ | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 5 min | **Entity and opening** | Official ATS URL (`Ashby`, `Greenhouse`, `Lever`, or `Personio`), legal entity, city | Is this exact requisition still active and hiring for my location? |
| 10 min | **Product and engineering** | Official product documentation, changelog, public GitHub or engineering blog | Which exact service or surface does this team own? |
| 5 min | **Stack and interview loop** | Live job description (JD) requirements plus written recruiter overview | Exact rounds, take-home versus pairing, allowed tools and artificial intelligence (AI) policy |
| 5 min | **Work and visa terms** | Posting's location and remote wording plus employer careers FAQ | Does *this* opening support relocation, EU Blue Card, or remote work? |
| 5 min | **Compensation and business health** | Published band (if European Union (EU) or United States (US) disclosed) plus recent dated announcements | Budgeted salary band, bonus, Employee Stock Ownership Plan (ESOP) or Virtual Stock Option Plan (VSOP) structure, runway |

## 3. Worked Example: What a Finished Profile Looks Like

Below is a **publicly verifiable reference example** showing the level of clarity to aim for in your private target notes (checked on `2026-11-23`; always open the live ATS link to check current vacancies):

| Field | Recorded Evidence (Dated 2026-11-23) | Unresolved / Confirm in Screen |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Company and ATS** | **n8n** — workflow automation platform ([product site](https://n8n.io/), [live Ashby board](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/n8n)) | Confirm exact legal entity and remote country list for the specific open requisition |
| **Core product** | Visual and code workflow orchestration connecting triggers, webhooks, application programming interfaces (APIs), and AI agents | Which product surface (execution engine, editor user interface (UI), cloud platform, AI nodes) the open team owns |
| **Technical angle to study** | Trigger → versioned workflow execution → retries versus idempotency across flaky third-party APIs | Exact backend and frontend split plus database and queue stack on this squad |
| **Motivation hook** | *"I've built idempotent webhook and worker pipelines and care about making failure recovery obvious to operators — how does your execution team handle partial retries without duplicating side effects?"* | Interview loop stages, take-home versus live pairing format, compensation band |
| **Visa and work policy** | Never assume from old blog posts — check the live Ashby posting's location tags | Confirm relocation or remote-contract support for this specific requisition |

Notice what makes this profile useful: **zero guessed salary bands, zero invented interview leaks**, one concrete engineering trade-off tied directly to the product, and explicit open questions ready for the first 15-minute screen.

## 4. Turn Research Into an Honest Motivation Answer

In the recruiter and hiring-manager calls, tie **one verified product detail** to **one real project you actually built**:

> *"Reading your documentation, I noticed [verified product capability or workflow]. In my recent role I owned [real related technical problem], so I'd love to hear how your team handles [specific architectural trade-off]."*

Never pretend you read a post you didn't read or claim experience with a niche tool just because the company uses it. Keep all private outreach notes, target rankings, and recruiter names in your private workspace, never in a public repository.

## Summary

Research the exact employing entity and live requisition in a focused 30-minute pass: verify identity and acquisitions first, capture dated product and stack evidence, keep unconfirmed questions explicit, and turn one real product trade-off into an honest motivation hook for the interview.

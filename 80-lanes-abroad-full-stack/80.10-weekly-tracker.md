# Weekly Tracker — Turn Evidence into the Next Action

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

Reading twenty chapters in a week feels productive, yet candidates often freeze in the very next mock interview because reading creates *familiarity*, not *recall under pressure*. The [Four-Week Study Plan](80.13-study-plan.md) sets your weekly schedule; this tracker records **what you actually tested, where you broke down, and what single repair you will verify next**.

Keep your filled-in personal tracker and live application statuses strictly in your private workspace.

## First Principles

Effort alone does not prove readiness. A useful tracker pairs every practice block with an **observable output** (an unseen problem solved, a failure trace drawn, a clean checkout run) and a **delayed recheck** so you only move on once the gap closes.

## 1. Log One Short Row Per Practice Signal

Use this compact structure in your private study log (the three rows below are illustrative templates):

| Date | Practice attempt | What actually happened | Exact gap exposed | Concrete next repair | Delayed recheck |
| ------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Example | Unseen booking system design | Handled concurrent holds, missed late payment webhook after expiry | Incomplete recovery path | Trace hold-expiry and late-capture race end to end | Next design mock |
| Example | Subarray-sum coding variant | Worked on positives, failed once negative numbers added | Monotonic window assumption broke | Derive prefix-sum hash map solution cold | Re-solve in 4 days |
| Example | 4-hour take-home rehearsal | Ran locally, failed in clean clone due to missing database seed step | Reproducibility gap | Add `.env.example` and seed script, then re-test clean clone | Before next real submission |

## 2. Keep Study Progress and Job-Application Funnels Separate

Every Sunday, spend 15 minutes reviewing two separate streams:

1. **Skill repairs (under your control):** Compare planned hours versus actual hours, pick the **single biggest gap** from recent mocks or variations, and schedule a focused repair plus a delayed recheck. Keep already-solid skills on light spaced recall instead of re-reading familiar chapters.
2. **Application funnel (noisy and delayed):** Track submissions, human responses, technical loops, and offers in your private application tracker with exact dates. Small samples are noisy — five unanswered applications do not automatically mean your technical skills are weak; check role fit, visa constraints, and top-of-curriculum-vitae (CV) alignment instead.

## 3. Judge Readiness by Real Signals, Not Arbitrary Self-Scores

Replace vague confidence ratings (*"I feel 7/10 ready"*) with four concrete binary signals:
- **Coding:** Solved an unseen constraint variation, wrote edge-case tests, and stated accurate complexity.
- **System design:** Protected the core invariant and traced a timeout, duplicate, and crash-recovery path clearly.
- **Behavioral:** Delivered a truthful 2-minute Situation, Task, Action, Result (STAR) story that survived probing follow-up questions.
- **Take-home:** Delivered a complete required slice inside the timebox that starts cleanly from a fresh checkout.

Start applying as soon as your resume and core stories are solid — keep closing remaining gaps in parallel rather than waiting forever for an arbitrary problem count.

## Summary

Log concrete evidence from unseen practice attempts instead of counting chapters read. Separate skill-gap tracking from noisy job-application funnels, fix the highest-risk gap first, and verify improvement with a delayed changed-case recheck.

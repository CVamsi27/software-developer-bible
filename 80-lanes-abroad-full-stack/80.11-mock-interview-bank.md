# Mock Interview Bank — Twenty Practice Prompts

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



This is a separate interview drill, not a claim about a particular employer's loop. Choose prompts from the confirmed format, attempt them before consulting the linked lessons, and ask a partner to evaluate the reasoning. Personal examples must come from verified events, not the answer templates below.

## Run a bounded mock

For a 90-minute mixed rehearsal: spend five minutes on setup, fifteen on a behavioral prompt, thirty on coding, thirty on design, and ten on questions and feedback. A focused mock can use only one type. Record only with the participants' agreement.

Assess assumptions, correctness, tests, trade-offs, and communication. Record the missed mechanism and a delayed reattempt in the [weekly tracker](80.10-weekly-tracker.md). Four rehearsals are a possible schedule, not a guarantee of readiness.

## Behavioral prompts

### Q1: Tell me about yourself

Start with your actual role and relevant experience, give one or two supported contributions, and explain interest in this role. Avoid invented metrics and a complete employment chronology.

### Q2: Why are you leaving your current role?

Give the actual reason at an appropriate disclosure level and describe what you seek next. Layoffs or a career change do not require an invented success narrative.

### Q3: Why this company?

Connect a product or engineering decision you researched to your experience. Verify the claimed policy or launch. Use [company research](80.07-company-profiles.md) to separate facts from assumptions.

### Q4: Describe a technical disagreement

State both positions fairly, your responsibility, the evidence or process used, and what happened after the decision. Do not borrow the earlier fictitious database prototype story as personal history.

### Q5: Describe a failure or a changed approach

Distinguish an incident from a defect caught before release. Explain the consequence, corrective action, and later improvement. A compliance violation or audit finding needs real evidence.

### Q6: Describe your most impactful project

Explain the user problem, your contribution, and an outcome you can support. Do not infer clinic counts, uptime, or absence of breaches from code alone.

### Q7: How do you learn a technology?

Use a recent actual problem, the material you consulted, a small experiment, and the limit you discovered. Reading feeds alone is neither sufficient nor inherently a bad learning habit.

### Q8: What would you do in the first ninety days?

Propose learning the team's objectives and operating constraints, shipping a bounded contribution, and taking ownership as context permits. Confirm expectations rather than promising a leadership initiative before understanding the role.

## Technical prompts

### Q9: Implement a rate limiter

Define the trusted key, burst capacity, sustained rate, clock, and state lifetime. Test refill, rejection, invalid configuration, and expired keys. An in-process map needs bounded memory; Redis requires atomic transitions. TTL is not a general clock-skew solution. See [caching and Redis](../20-backend/20.8-caching/INDEX.md).

### Q10: Implement a retryable job queue

Specify enqueue, claim, attempt completion, delayed retry, and dead-letter states. Distinguish an in-memory exercise from durable work. Trace a crash after the side effect but before acknowledgment and decide how recovery avoids or reconciles duplicates.

### Q11: Implement a URL shortener

Define URL validation, code allocation/collisions, redirect policy, retention, and abuse constraints. Use a unique constraint to protect allocation. See [the URL-shortener case](../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.01-url-shortener.md).

### Q12: Merge k sorted linked lists

State the input ownership/mutation contract and handle empty lists. A real binary min-heap supports `O(N log k)` merging with `O(k)` heap storage; repeatedly sorting an array and using `shift()` is not that complexity. See [heap patterns](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/04-heaps-stacks-selection/70.2.4.01-heap.md).

### Q13: Find the longest substring without repeating characters

Choose whether “character” means UTF-16 code unit, Unicode code point, or grapheme cluster. Maintain a window whose elements are distinct and move its start monotonically. Include an example where a repeated element was already outside the window. See [sliding windows](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/01-linear-structures/70.2.1.01-sliding-window.md).

### Q14: Implement debounce and throttle

Specify leading/trailing behavior, receiver preservation, arguments, cancellation, and return type. A deferred call does not preserve the original function's synchronous return contract. Use the canonical [debounce](../10-frontend/10.1-javascript/04-memory-and-data/10.1.4.04-debounce.md) and [throttle](../10-frontend/10.1-javascript/04-memory-and-data/10.1.4.05-throttle.md) chapters after your attempt.

### Q15: Detect a linked-list cycle

Derive why slow and fast pointers meet inside a cycle and why reaching the end proves acyclicity. Test empty, singleton, self-cycle, and a cycle beginning after a prefix. Explain time and extra space. See [two pointers](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/01-linear-structures/70.2.1.02-two-pointers.md).

## Design prompts

### Q16: Design a workflow engine

Protect durable execution state, bounded retries, step ownership, and external side effects. Trace worker death while a step's result is unknown. Use [case 1](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-1-workflow-execution).

### Q17: Design a payment gateway

Separate intent, authorization, capture, refund, and reconciliation. Explain a provider timeout and idempotency payload matching. A saga is not two-phase commit. Use [case 2](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-2-payments).

### Q18: Design multi-tenant property management

Authorize membership and scope reads, writes, caches, jobs, storage, and analytics. Test cross-tenant denial and pooled connection context. Use [case 3](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-3-tenant-isolation).

### Q19: Design a parking booking system

Protect inventory ownership, hold expiry, and payment completion. Trace the race between releasing a hold and receiving successful payment. Use [case 4](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-4-inventory-and-booking).

### Q20: Design an event-driven logistics service

Choose the ordering key, topic layout, compatibility policy, acknowledgment boundary, and replay horizon. Explain a poison event without silently breaking domain ordering. Use [case 5](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-5-event-processing).

## Feedback record

For each attempt, record one correct mechanism, one gap, and the next test that can expose whether the gap is fixed. Score against the confirmed requirements, not a fabricated company rubric. Use [section revision](REVISION.md) for smaller retrieval checks between mocks.

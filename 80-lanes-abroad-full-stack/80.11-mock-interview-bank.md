# Mock Interview Bank — Twenty Practice Prompts

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

Reading chapters builds understanding, yet interviews test whether you can recall and apply that understanding aloud under a running clock.

Pick prompts from this 20-question bank matching your confirmed interview format, attempt them **closed-book** with a timer (or a peer), and log every exposed gap in your [Weekly Tracker](80.10-weekly-tracker.md). Always answer behavioral prompts with your own verified work history — never recite hypothetical templates.

## How to Run a Focused 90-Minute Mock

For a full-loop rehearsal: **5 min** setup → **15 min** behavioral (2 prompts plus follow-ups) → **30 min** technical coding → **30 min** system design → **10 min** concrete debrief. For a single-round mock, run one 45–60 minute block and spend the final 10 minutes recording the exact missed invariant and your delayed recheck task.

## Behavioral Prompts (Q1–Q8)

### Q1: Tell me about yourself
Give a crisp 90-second arc: current senior scope, 1–2 verifiable production highlights matching this posting, and why this exact next role makes sense. Skip reading your full chronological resume aloud.

### Q2: Why are you leaving your current role?
Give a calm, honest reason focused on what you want to build next (scope, domain, engineering culture, or relocation) — never vent about coworkers or invent a polished cover story for a layoff.

### Q3: Why this company?
Connect one verified product workflow or technical decision you researched (see [Company Profiles](80.07-company-profiles.md)) directly to a real problem you solved before.

### Q4: Describe a technical disagreement
Represent the other engineer's position fairly, show the criteria or evidence you proposed to resolve it, state the final outcome honestly, and share what you learned.

### Q5: Describe a production mistake or changed approach
Own the root cause clearly (distinguishing a production incident from a staging bug), walk through immediate containment, and highlight the systemic guardrail you added afterward.

### Q6: Describe your most impactful project
Frame the user or business problem, your exact personal technical ownership (*"I"* versus *"we"*), the hardest trade-off, and an outcome backed by real evidence.

### Q7: How do you ramp up on an unfamiliar technology?
Walk through a real recent example: official documentation and specifications you read, the small prototype or failure experiment you ran, and one sharp limitation you uncovered.

### Q8: What would you focus on in your first 90 days?
Balance learning the team's critical paths and operational pain points first, shipping a scoped production win early, and earning broader ownership once you understand real context.

## Practical & Coding Prompts (Q9–Q15)

### Q9: Implement a rate limiter
Clarify trusted key, burst versus sustained rate, clock source, and memory eviction. Test refill, rejection, invalid configuration, and key expiry, and compare bounded in-memory maps versus atomic Redis Lua scripts. See [Caching & Redis](../20-backend/20.8-caching/INDEX.md).

### Q10: Implement a retryable job queue
Model `enqueue`, `claim`, `complete`, `retry` (with backoff), and `dead_letter` states. Trace a worker crashing *after* executing an external application programming interface (API) call but *before* marking the job done.

### Q11: Implement a URL shortener
Clarify Uniform Resource Locator (URL) validation, short-code generation and collision handling (`UNIQUE` constraint), redirect status (`301` versus `302`), expiry, and abuse rate limits. See [URL Shortener](../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.01-url-shortener.md).

### Q12: Merge k sorted linked lists
Clarify whether input lists may be mutated and handle empty inputs. Implement a real binary min-heap for `O(N log k)` time and `O(k)` space — sorting an array repeatedly or calling `shift()` fails that bound. See [Heap Patterns](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/04-heaps-stacks-selection/70.2.4.01-heap.md).

### Q13: Longest substring without repeating characters
Clarify character boundaries (16-bit Unicode Transformation Format (UTF-16) code unit versus Unicode code point), keep the sliding window strictly duplicate-free, move `left` monotonically (`Math.max(left, prev + 1)`), and trace a duplicate that sits *before* `left`. See [Sliding Window](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/01-linear-structures/70.2.1.01-sliding-window.md).

### Q14: Implement `debounce` and `throttle`
Clarify leading and trailing edge options, `this` binding and arguments preservation, `.cancel()`, and why a deferred call cannot return a synchronous value. Check against [Debounce](../10-frontend/10.1-javascript/04-memory-and-data/10.1.4.04-debounce.md) and [Throttle](../10-frontend/10.1-javascript/04-memory-and-data/10.1.4.05-throttle.md).

### Q15: Detect a linked-list cycle
Prove why Floyd's slow and fast pointers always meet inside a cycle and terminate cleanly on acyclic lists; test empty, 1-node, self-loop, and cycle-after-prefix cases in `O(1)` extra space. See [Two Pointers](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/01-linear-structures/70.2.1.02-two-pointers.md).

## System Design Prompts (Q16–Q20)

### Q16: Design a workflow engine
Cover durable run snapshots, step leases and fencing, bounded retries, Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF) guards, and worker crashes with unknown remote outcomes. Check against [Case 1](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-1-workflow-execution).

### Q17: Design a payment gateway
Separate intent, authorization, capture, refund, and reconciliation; bind idempotency keys to exact payloads and handle provider timeouts without double-charging. Check against [Case 2](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-2-payments).

### Q18: Design multi-tenant property management
Verify tenant membership on every path, scope queries, caches, background jobs, storage, and analytics, and explain transaction-local Row-Level Security (RLS) under connection pooling. Check against [Case 3](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-3-tenant-isolation).

### Q19: Design a parking booking system
Enforce atomic hold ownership plus expiry and resolve the race between a hold-expiry worker and a late payment confirmation callback. Check against [Case 4](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-4-inventory-and-booking).

### Q20: Design an event-driven logistics service
Pick the ordering partition key, define schema compatibility, tie consumer deduplication to local database commits, and handle poison events without breaking entity order. Check against [Case 5](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md#case-5-event-processing).

## Debrief Checklist After Every Mock

Write down three lines immediately after the timer ends: **(1)** one mechanism you nailed, **(2)** the single biggest gap exposed, and **(3)** the exact changed-constraint follow-up you will re-test next week. Use [Section Revision](REVISION.md) for quick closed-book drills between full mocks.

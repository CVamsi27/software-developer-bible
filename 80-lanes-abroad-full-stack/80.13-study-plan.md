# Four-Week Interview Preparation Plan

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



This is an adjustable preparation cycle, not a four-week promise of an offer. Confirm the target role's loop first, then allocate practice to observed gaps. Keep current companies, dates, personal stories, and actual progress in the [private canonical workspace](personal/README.md).

## Start the search while preparing

Apply from the first week once the CV and public claims are consistent and the role's hard requirements fit. Preparation and screening can proceed together; completing this cycle is not a prerequisite for submission. For a personal campaign, use the [active private timetable](personal/reports/100-day-job-roadmap.md) and its actual available-hour budget. The ten-hour/week example below is a reusable preparation template, not an additional calendar to execute in parallel.

## First Principles

A fixed time budget forces selection. Adding every desirable exercise creates a plan that cannot be executed. Practice results provide feedback: an unfamiliar problem exposes a missing invariant, and a mock exposes a weak explanation. The next week's allocation should respond to that evidence.

## Set one realistic weekly budget

The example below uses ten hours per week, forty hours across four weeks. One possible cadence is five one-hour weekday sessions, a four-hour weekend session, and one hour for review. Choose a cadence that fits your work and recovery; there is no universal point at which retention collapses after three hours.

Use one schedule, not separate contradictory totals for algorithms, mocks, and projects. A real assignment or interview replaces lower-priority practice; it does not get added on top without changing the budget.

## Week 1: Establish evidence and find gaps

| Activity | Hours | Observable output |
| ---------------------------------- | ----: | -------------------------------------------- |
| Read the role and confirm its loop | 1 | Format, constraints, unresolved questions |
| Organize actual behavioral events | 2 | Supported examples and disclosure limits |
| Try unfamiliar coding variations | 3 | Failed assumptions and successful invariants |
| Run and review a focused mock | 2 | One prioritized improvement |
| Research an actual opening | 1 | Dated, role-scoped evidence |
| Weekly review | 1 | Adjusted next-week plan |

Use the [story-bank method](80.12-story-bank.md), [coding starting set](80.02-tech-screen-patterns.md), and [company research method](80.07-company-profiles.md). Do not assume every employer uses a take-home or that some algorithms are excluded by geography.

## Week 2: Deliver a bounded practical slice

| Activity | Hours | Observable output |
| ----------------------------------- | ----: | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Time-boxed practical rehearsal | 4 | Required path, important failure tests, runnable handoff |
| Review and correct the rehearsal | 2 | Highest-risk gaps addressed |
| Coding variations and delayed retry | 2 | Transfer rather than template recall |
| Behavioral follow-ups | 1 | Accurate, concise explanations |
| Weekly review | 1 | Plan adjusted from evidence |

Use the [take-home guide](80.01-take-home-playbook.md). A rehearsal budget is your choice; an employer's assignment limit governs a real submission. A project may be private. Choose a stack from the brief and your fluency rather than a stale version mandate.

## Week 3: Explain correctness and recovery

| Activity | Hours | Observable output |
| --------------------------------- | ----: | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Three design attempts with review | 4 | Invariants, workload, failure and recovery traced |
| Deepen one weak mechanism | 2 | Focused explanation and example |
| Coding variation | 1 | Boundary tests and complexity |
| Design mock and feedback | 2 | Evidence of the remaining gap |
| Weekly review | 1 | Final-week priorities |

Use [the twelve failure boundaries](80.08-system-design-case-bank.md) and follow their canonical links. A timer helps rehearse the confirmed interview format; reading the model answer first tests recognition instead of independent reasoning.

## Week 4: Test transfer and prepare the handoff

| Activity | Hours | Observable output |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----: | ------------------------------------------- |
| Mixed mock and review | 2 | Verified improvement and remaining gaps |
| Correct weak areas | 2 | Unseen reattempts |
| Tailor evidence to actual roles | 2 | Accurate CV/answers and verified motivation |
| Applications or scheduled-loop preparation | 3 | Role-specific, checked materials |
| Weekly review | 1 | Continue, change focus, or recover |

An application is an action you control; a response date is not. No self-score, mock count, or solved-problem quota guarantees an interview. Continue applying when appropriate while improving the evidence.

## Replan when the constraints change

When a real loop arrives, confirm its format and replace generic practice with the relevant rehearsal. When available time shrinks, cut scope rather than compressing all tasks. If fatigue or repeated missed sessions makes the cadence unrealistic, reduce it and protect recovery. Do not compensate for one missed session by automatically doubling the next.

Track actual results in the [weekly tracker](80.10-weekly-tracker.md). Keep its evidence rows separate from this schedule so the plan can change without rewriting history.

## Summary

Choose a sustainable budget, attempt meaningful tasks, use feedback to prioritize, and keep the arithmetic consistent. The plan organizes preparation; it does not predict hiring outcomes.

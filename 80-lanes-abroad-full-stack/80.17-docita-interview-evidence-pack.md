# Assemble a Docita Interview Evidence Pack

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



The [walkthrough guide](80.16-docita-resume-defense.md) explains the answer flow. This guide helps you choose the artifacts behind that answer. Keep the approved private packet in the canonical workspace; share a sanitized subset appropriate to the interview.

## First Principles

A claim about implementation, a claim about tested behavior and a claim about production outcome require different evidence. An evidence card must record the artifact and its scope so one kind of proof is not silently substituted for another.

For calculation practice, use the [hypothetical 25-clinic metrics scenario](../projects/docita/23-hypothetical-clinic-metrics.md). Its assumptions, synthetic samples and proposed targets must remain separate from actual evidence cards.

## Make one evidence card

For each important claim, record:

| Field | Example of the required detail |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Claim | The exact behavior or contribution you intend to describe |
| Source | Repository, revision, path, and relevant callers |
| Check | Command or procedure, environment, date, result, and limitations |
| Boundary | Which users, resources, failures, and workloads it covers |
| Disclosure | What may be shared and what must be removed |

A test filename is a lead. Open the assertions and execution result before treating it as proof. Mark configuration-only, unexecuted, skipped, or credential-blocked evidence accordingly.

## Start with three cards

| Card | Minimum useful artifacts | Challenge to prepare for |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tenant isolation | Enforcement path, two-tenant fixture, denial assertion | Can a join, background job, privileged role, or pooled connection escape scope? |
| Booking correctness | Actual invariant, transaction or constraint, concurrent result | Do distinct requests contend on the same resource? |
| Reliable side effect | Atomic durable write, claim/recovery path, failed-attempt evidence | What happens after remote success but before local acknowledgment? |

Choose different cards when the role calls for frontend accessibility, query performance, or migration work. You do not need every topic in one interview packet.

## Check the mechanism instead of borrowing an answer

A uniqueness check on `(patient_id, slot_start)` only rejects equal starts. It does not reject every overlapping interval. PostgreSQL `CHECK` constraints are not cross-row overlap constraints; exclusion constraints or correctly synchronized capacity checks may be suitable depending on the invariant. State what the actual implementation enforces, including active statuses and interval boundaries. See [PostgreSQL constraints](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-constraints.html).

Launching two promises does not force transactions through the vulnerable interleaving. Use controlled barriers or instrumented coordination where possible, then repeat relevant cases against the real database. `READ COMMITTED` does not generally prevent phantoms; serializable execution can abort and needs bounded retries. See [PostgreSQL isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html).

For request-scoped RLS, verify that all relevant operations use the same transaction and that tenant state cannot leak through a pool. Avoid interpolating a tenant value into raw SQL. PostgreSQL's `set_config` can accept a bound value with transaction-local scope; the application must still route subsequent operations through that transaction. See [configuration functions](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/functions-admin.html).

A room membership check does not authorize every clinical payload for every member of that room. A reconnect cursor needs a persisted, authorized replay source; WebSockets alone do not provide missed-event recovery.

## Add measurements when they answer a question

For a performance card, keep workload, environment, concurrency, warm/cold state, percentile, error rate, and comparable before/after results. For a product metric, keep its approved definition and period. Put actual figures in private records rather than repeating stale values across public guides.

Do not infer capacity limits from clinic count, enforce a universal 60/25/15 test split, or invent a “caught in CI” incident. A genuine decision without a numerical result can still be explained precisely. Missing evidence should remain a gap, not a scripted first-person story.

## Review the packet for disclosure and consistency

Use synthetic fixtures and sanitized examples. Patient identifiers, tenant identifiers, traces, and aggregates may still reveal personal data. Remove secrets and restricted infrastructure details. Make the résumé wording match the evidence's scope and confirm the actual rendered artifacts after editing.

A backup script is not a successful restore drill. A presigned upload URL is not proof that file content was validated. A redactor is not proof of complete privacy compliance. Use the [claim crosswalk](80.18-resume-claim-crosswalk.md) to narrow statements to the evidence available.

## Summary

Collect a few evidence cards with current source, executed checks, clear boundaries, and safe disclosure. Use them to explain decisions and failures rather than memorize a generic answer bank.

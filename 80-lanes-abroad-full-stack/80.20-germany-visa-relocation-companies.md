# Check German Employer Relocation Support

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

Many candidates waste weeks preparing for a German company because someone online called it "visa-friendly," only to hear at final round: *"We sponsor for our Berlin platform team, but this product role requires existing EU work authorization."* In Germany, relocation support is decided **per opening**, not permanently per company — so confirm four concrete employer commitments during the very first recruiter screen.

## First Principles

**Legal eligibility to work in Germany** and **practical employer relocation support** are two separate gates. German law sets the Blue Card or skilled-worker requirements (qualification, salary floor, qualifying contract); the employer decides whether they are willing to wait for embassy processing, fill out the official employment declaration (*Erklärung zum Beschäftigungsverhältnis*), and help fund your move.

## 1. Separate Legal Admission From Employer Support

Use the [Visa & Relocation Playbook](80.06-visa-relocation-playbook.md) for current statutory salary floors (e.g., 2026 EU Blue Card thresholds) and consult the [official EU Blue Card guide](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card) for legal criteria. Remember that *"English-speaking office"* or *"international team"* does **not** prove an opening sponsors non-EU candidates.

Check **four concrete employer commitments** in the first screen:

| Question to ask | What counts as real confirmation |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Does *this* opening hire candidates who need German work authorization?** | Written yes/no tied to the exact job ID and German legal entity |
| **2. Who prepares the employer paperwork and relocation logistics?** | Named internal HR owner or external relocation agency (e.g., Localyze/Jobbatical) |
| **3. What relocation costs are covered — and when are they paid?** | Written cap, lump sum vs. receipt reimbursement, family coverage, and clawback clause |
| **4. What happens if the embassy appointment delays the start date?** | Contract start-date contingency clause (not a verbal *"it usually takes 6 weeks"*) |

### Copy-paste recruiter question (use in screen #1 or outreach)

> *"I'm relocating from outside the EU and would apply via the EU Blue Card route. Does this specific opening support hiring a candidate who needs German work authorization, and does your team handle the employer employment declaration and relocation coordination?"*

**Remote-before-relocation warning:** Never assume you can start working remotely from your home country while waiting for a German visa — cross-border payroll and permanent-establishment tax rules often forbid it unless the employer explicitly uses an Employer of Record (EOR) or contractor bridge.

## 2. Verify Your Degree Recognition Early (Anabin vs. ZAB)

Do **not** wait until you have an offer to check qualification recognition — missing proof can add months to a Blue Card filing:

1. **Anabin checks foreign universities and degrees, not German employers.**
2. **University `H+` status alone is not enough:** both your **institution** (`H+`) **and** your **exact degree title** must appear as equivalent (`entspricht` / `gleichwertig`) in Anabin.
3. **If your exact degree title is missing from Anabin:** apply early for a **ZAB Statement of Comparability** (*Zeugnisbewertung*), which is the official individual evaluation used by embassies. (Also check whether the IT specialist experience route applies if you lack a matched degree.) See the [official foreign academic qualification guide](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/working-in-germany/recognition/foreign-academic-qualifications).

Always follow the exact checklist published by the German mission/consulate handling your jurisdiction, and keep passport scans, degree transcripts, and personal records strictly in your private workspace.

## 3. Plan Around Dependencies, Not an Optimistic 90-Day Countdown

```text
Confirm role & employer support
  ─► Verify Anabin / ZAB + civil documents (apostilled)
       ─► Receive qualifying contract (with visa-delay start clause)
            ─► Submit complete visa application at embassy / portal
                 ─► Visa approved ─► Travel, start work, Anmeldung & family steps
```

Start document collection and Anabin/ZAB checks **while interviewing** so an offer does not stall on paperwork. Treat any "90-day timeline" as a planning target only — embassy slot availability and local immigration offices (*Ausländerbehörde*) vary widely by city and season. Never sign non-refundable leases or flights before visa issuance.

## Summary

Confirm relocation support per requisition — never from a company's general reputation. Verify four concrete commitments early (non-EU eligibility, paperwork owner, written relocation budget/clawback, and start-date delay clause), check both university and exact degree comparability in Anabin/ZAB before an offer arrives, and sequence your move around real document and visa approvals rather than assumed timelines.

# Germany — Visa-Sponsoring Companies for Software Engineers

> **Germany is the largest EU tech market.** EU Blue Card is the primary visa path for senior engineers. The minimum salary threshold for IT shortage occupations is ~EUR 48,300/year (2024-25), well below what most tech companies pay. Most German tech companies routinely sponsor visas and handle the process for you.

## TL;DR

- **EU Blue Card** = fastest path for senior engineers. 6-12 weeks processing. Spouse gets work permit. Path to PR in 21 months (with B1 German) or 33 months.
- **Employer pays visa costs + relocation.** This is standard, not a negotiation lever.
- **100+ German tech companies sponsor visas.** The list below covers the most relevant for full-stack TypeScript engineers.
- **Berlin = most affordable + most English-friendly.** Munich = highest comp. Hamburg = growing hub.

---

## EU Blue Card — Quick Reference

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Visa name** | EU Blue Card (Blaue Karte EU) |
| **Process time** | 6-12 weeks |
| **Minimum salary (2024-25)** | EUR 48,300/year (IT shortage occupation — lower threshold) |
| **Family** | Spouse work permit included. Kids residency + school. |
| **Path to PR** | 21 months with B1 German, 33 months otherwise |
| **Path to citizenship** | 6-8 years (requires B1 German + civic test) |
| **Employer requirement** | Job contract + recognition of degree (or 3+ years experience) |
| **Where to apply** | German embassy in your home country |

**Key changes (2024-25):**
- Salary threshold lowered for shortage occupations (IT qualifies)
- Job seekers visa: 6 months to search for a job in Germany
- Recognition of Indian degrees: faster process via anabin database
- Spouse can work immediately (no language requirement for Blue Card holders)

## How to Find Visa-Sponsoring Companies

### Step 1: Check the anabin database
- **anabin.kmk.org** — lists recognized German employers and degree requirements
- If your degree is listed as "H+" = fully recognized = Blue Card eligible

### Step 2: Check job postings for visa language
Look for these phrases in job ads:
- "We sponsor work visas"
- "Visa sponsorship available"
- "Relocation support provided"
- "EU Blue Card eligible"
- "We welcome international candidates"
- "English-speaking team"

### Step 3: Ask in the recruiter screen
**Script:** "I'm currently based in India and would need visa sponsorship to work in Germany. Is that something you support for this role?"

**Most German tech companies will say yes** — it's standard practice. If they hesitate, it's a red flag (small company without HR infrastructure).

### Step 4: Check company blogs for visa info
Many German tech companies publish blog posts about their visa process:
- SumUp engineering blog
- GetYourGuide careers page
- N26 engineering blog
- Zalando tech blog

## Visa Application Timeline

| Days | Action |
|------|--------|
| **0-7** | Accept offer, sign contract. Share: passport, degree certificates, employment references |
| **7-14** | Employer initiates visa process. You gather: degree recognition (anabin check), health insurance, proof of funds |
| **14-30** | Apply at German embassy in your city. Biometrics + documents |
| **30-60** | Visa processing. Use this time to: research schools, find housing, sell/lease home |
| **60-75** | Visa approved. Book flights. Confirm temporary housing |
| **75-90** | Arrive in Germany. Register at local Bürgeramt. Open bank account. Start work |

**Total: 60-90 days from offer to starting work.**

---

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Don't wait for "perfect" documents.** Start the application with what you have. The embassy will request additional documents if needed.
2. **Don't skip the anabin check.** If your degree isn't recognized, you need additional verification (can take 2-4 weeks).
3. **Don't assume the company handles everything.** Some companies handle the process end-to-end; others expect you to drive it. Ask explicitly during the offer stage.
4. **Don't forget health insurance.** German health insurance is mandatory. Most companies enroll you in TK (Techniker Krankenkasse) or similar.
5. **Don't underestimate the apartment search.** Berlin/Munich housing is competitive. Start looking 2-3 months before arrival.
6. **Don't sign a lease before arriving.** Temporary housing first (Airbnb, Wunderflats, HousingAnywhere), then permanent apartment after 2-3 months.

# EU Regulatory Compliance — GDPR, BaFin, EU AI Act, Digital Services Act

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



Start with the product's regulated activity and data flows, then determine the
obligations. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Digital Services Act
(DSA), AI Act and financial-sector rules have different
scopes; a German software company is not automatically subject to every one.
This guide connects those distinctions to engineering decisions.

A **controller** decides why and how personal data is processed; a **processor** handles it on the controller’s behalf. Under the AI Act, a **provider** develops or places an AI system on the market under its name, while a **deployer** uses one under its authority. One organization can hold different roles for different activities. Identify the actual role and data flow before assigning obligations.

## First Principles

1. **A rule applies to an activity and a responsible actor.** Controller/processor, AI provider/deployer and regulated financial entity are different roles; establish them before assigning controls.
2. **A compliance claim needs observable behavior.** A deletion button, encryption setting or consent banner alone does not prove that the relevant data lifecycle or legal condition is satisfied.
3. **Copies cross system boundaries.** Exports, logs, processors and restored backups can retain the same personal information; obligations require a documented lifecycle across those copies.

## GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

### Legal basis before implementation

Identify the purpose, Article 6 basis and responsible controller for each data
flow. Consent is one possible basis, not a default requirement for every
operation. Health data also needs an applicable special-category condition;
a patient's ordinary service contract does not by itself settle that question.
[Commission legal-basis guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en)
[Special-category guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-individuals_en)

For international transfers, identify the recipient and applicable transfer
mechanism. EU hosting can reduce transfers but does not prevent overseas
support access or establish every GDPR requirement. Adequacy and appropriate
safeguards are distinct routes. [Commission transfer guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection_en)

### Data Subject Rights

Respond without undue delay and normally within **one month**, rather than a
fixed 30-day counter. Complex requests can receive a two-month extension when
the person is informed within the initial month. Verify identity proportionately
and document the response. Rights and exceptions depend on the applicable
processing conditions. [EDPB rights guide](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en)

A practical request workflow is: authenticate/verify the request → locate the
relevant data → assess the right and lawful retention exceptions → execute
across systems and recipients → record the outcome → respond. A CSV endpoint
is only the delivery format; it must enforce authorization and include the
required scope without exposing another person's data.

**Erasure:** marking a row `deleted=true` while keeping its identifiable data
is not erasure by itself. Remove or irreversibly anonymize data where required;
separately restrict and justify records subject to a retention exception. A
restore process must reapply deletions so backups cannot silently resurrect
an erased account. [EDPB erasure and exceptions](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/find-practical-info/faq_en)

### Technical Controls

Choose controls for the documented risks: least privilege, tenant boundaries,
key management, encrypted storage/transport, minimized logs and tested recovery.
GDPR does not prescribe one AES mode, a universal seven-year audit-log period
or a blanket ten-year retention period for every healthcare field. Determine
retention by jurisdiction, record class, purpose and applicable legal duties.
An audit log contains personal data too; access, minimization and expiry matter.

A data protection impact assessment (DPIA) is required for processing likely to create high risk, not automatically
for every website launch. Use it to describe actual data flows, risks and
mitigations rather than inserting a generic checklist as evidence.

### Breach response

Notify the authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours
of awareness**, unless risk to individuals is unlikely. Communication to
individuals has a different high-risk threshold and relevant exceptions.
Document the breach and assessment; incomplete initial information can be
supplemented. [EDPB breach guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/assess-the-risks/data-breaches_en)

## BaFin: identify the regulated financial activity

BaFin is Germany’s Federal Financial Supervisory Authority. The relevant requirements depend on the financial activity and entity status.
Know-your-customer (KYC) identity checks, anti-money-laundering (AML) controls, payment authentication and operational resilience are separate control
families. Identify the applicable law, supervising authority and outsourced
responsibilities before claiming a product is "BaFin compliant".

A biometric login or 3-D Secure integration alone does not establish every
strong-customer-authentication requirement. The actual authentication flow,
independent factors, exemptions and transaction context must be assessed.
Likewise, a suspicious-transaction score is an investigation signal, not an
automatic legal conclusion.

## Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)

DORA has applied since 17 January 2025 to entities within its financial-sector
scope. Engineering work includes information and communication technology (ICT) risk controls, major-incident reporting,
resilience testing and third-party arrangements. Reporting clocks depend on
incident classification and the applicable standards: do not replace them with
"every incident goes to BaFin within 24 hours". Threat-led penetration testing
is an additional requirement for selected entities, not an annual universal
rule for every fintech. [BaFin DORA overview](https://www.bafin.de/DE/Aufsicht/DORA/DORA_node.html)

## EU AI Act

Classify the **intended use**, actor role and product context. A healthcare
scheduling helper is not automatically high-risk simply because the customer
is a clinic. Medical-device AI and listed sensitive uses can trigger different
high-risk conditions; a general chatbot can instead face transparency duties.
An ordinary analytics dashboard is not automatically an AI system.

The Commission's current timeline, checked **2026-10-09**, reflects the AI
Omnibus that entered into force on 27 July 2026: Annex III high-risk rules apply
from **2 December 2027** and regulated-product Annex I rules from **2 August
2028**. This extension does not move every provision: prohibited-practice,
literacy, general-purpose-model and transparency obligations have separate
application dates. [Current Commission AI Act guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)

For a qualifying high-risk system, connect the risk-management, data-quality,
documentation, logging, oversight and robustness requirements to the actual
product lifecycle. Avoid claiming that an unimplemented hypothetical Docita
feature already demonstrates compliance.

## Digital Services Act (DSA)

The DSA's obligations depend on intermediary/service type, size and role.
Hosting, online-platform and very-large-platform duties differ. Moderation
notice handling, reasons and appeals are useful engineering mechanisms, but
there is no universal "remove every illegal-content report within 24 hours"
rule, mandatory academic-research API or quarterly report for every software
company. The Commission explains the proportional tiers and additional duties
for very large platforms. [DSA scope and responsibilities](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act)

## Summary

- Establish the regulated activity and responsible actor before choosing controls.
- Build data-rights workflows across copies and processors; soft deletion alone is insufficient.
- Distinguish request, breach and sectoral reporting clocks.
- Classify AI by intended use and verify the current provision-specific timeline.
- Treat working controls and documented evidence as the basis of a compliance claim.

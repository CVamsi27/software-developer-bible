# EU Regulatory Compliance — GDPR, BaFin, EU AI Act, Digital Services Act

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

In European senior full-stack interviews, compliance questions come up regularly — especially at fintech, healthtech, AI, and B2B SaaS startups. The biggest mistake candidates make is reciting myths (*"GDPR bans US servers,"* *"every fintech outage goes to BaFin in 24 hours,"* *"every clinic app is high-risk AI"*). This guide explains **which EU law applies to which activity** and **what concrete engineering controls each one actually demands**.

## First Principles

1. **Obligations depend on your product's activity and legal role, not your country.** Under GDPR, a **controller** decides *why and how* personal data is processed, while a **processor** handles data on a controller's behalf. Under the EU AI Act, a **provider** builds/places an AI system on the market, while a **deployer** uses it. One company can hold different roles across different features.
2. **Real compliance lives in verifiable system behavior.** A cookie banner or a `deleted = true` flag does not prove legal deletion or consent if backups, logs, or third-party webhooks silently keep raw personal data.
3. **Personal data leaks through copies.** Primary databases are only one copy — exports, analytics warehouses, logs, error trackers, subprocessors, and database backups all need an explicit lifecycle.

## Quick Scope Map

| Framework | Who it regulates | Primary engineering implications |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **GDPR** | Any service processing EU residents' personal data | Legal basis, data minimization, real erasure/export across copies, 72h breach awareness workflow |
| **BaFin / Financial law** | Regulated German financial & payment entities | Strong Customer Authentication (SCA), KYC/AML workflows, outsourcing & audit controls |
| **DORA** | EU financial entities & critical ICT providers (in force **17 Jan 2025**) | ICT risk management, major-incident reporting, resilience testing, vendor risk |
| **EU AI Act** | AI providers & deployers by **intended use & risk tier** | Risk classification, human oversight, logging, transparency; updated Omnibus dates below |
| **DSA** | Online intermediaries, hosting services & platforms (tiered by size) | Notice-and-action moderation flows, statement of reasons, appeals (VLOPs carry extra duties) |

## 1. GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

### Legal basis & international transfers

Before storing personal data, identify the **purpose, responsible controller, and Article 6 legal basis** (consent is only *one* of six bases — contract fulfillment or legal obligation often applies instead). **Special-category data** (such as health/clinical data) requires an additional Article 9 condition; an ordinary terms-of-service click is not enough. ([Commission legal-basis guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en) · [Special-category guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-individuals_en))

- **Transfer myth:** GDPR does **not** impose a blanket "EU-only hosting" law. Cross-border transfers require a valid mechanism (such as an adequacy decision or Standard Contractual Clauses). Equally, hosting servers in Frankfurt does **not** eliminate transfers if overseas support engineers or US SaaS vendors access raw personal data. ([Commission transfer guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection_en))

### Data subject rights (access, portability, and real erasure)

Respond without undue delay and normally within **one month** (not a rigid 30-day counter; complex requests may be extended by up to two additional months if the person is notified within the first month). ([EDPB rights guide](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en))

Design a clean end-to-end workflow:
```text
Verify requester identity ─► Locate data across DB, search, logs & vendors
  ─► Check lawful retention exceptions (e.g., tax invoices)
       ─► Execute export or erasure ─► Log completion ─► Reply within 1 month
```

- **Soft-delete trap:** Setting `deleted = true` while keeping identifiable rows live is **not** GDPR erasure. You must delete or irreversibly anonymize the data (except fields legally retained under a documented statutory duty, which should be restricted). Equally important: when restoring from an older backup after an outage, replay a deletion tombstone log so erased users are not silently resurrected! ([EDPB erasure & exceptions](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/find-practical-info/faq_en))

### Technical controls & 72-hour breach reporting

GDPR requires risk-appropriate security (least privilege, tenant isolation, encryption in transit/at rest, minimized logs, tested recovery) — it does **not** hardcode one AES cipher mode or a universal 7-year audit-log period. Retention periods depend on jurisdiction, record class, and purpose — and remember audit logs themselves contain personal data! Run a **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** when processing poses high risk to individuals, not blindly for every landing page.

If a personal-data breach occurs, notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours of becoming aware** (unless risk to individuals is unlikely); notifying affected individuals applies when risk is **high**. ([EDPB breach guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/assess-the-risks/data-breaches_en))

## 2. BaFin & DORA (Fintech / Financial Services in Germany & EU)

- **BaFin** is Germany's Federal Financial Supervisory Authority. Never claim a system is *"BaFin compliant"* loosely — specify the regulated activity (payments, lending, crypto custody) and separate **KYC** (identity verification), **AML** (transaction monitoring — where risk scores flag reviews rather than making legal rulings), and **Strong Customer Authentication (SCA)** (which checks independent factors and transaction context, not just plugging in 3-D Secure).
- **DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act):** Has applied since **17 January 2025** to regulated EU financial entities. Engineering teams focus on ICT risk controls, major-incident classification and reporting (using DORA's exact regulatory timelines — not a myth that every bug goes to BaFin in 24 hours), resilience testing, and third-party vendor governance. Threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) applies to designated significant entities, not every small fintech annually. ([BaFin DORA overview](https://www.bafin.de/DE/Aufsicht/DORA/DORA_node.html))

## 3. EU AI Act (Risk-Based Classification & 2026–2028 Timeline)

Classification follows **intended use**, not industry label: a basic clinic appointment chatbot is not automatically "high-risk AI" just because its customer is a doctor, whereas diagnostic medical-device AI or listed sensitive use cases can trigger strict high-risk duties (risk management, data governance, traceability logs, human oversight, robustness). Ordinary non-AI analytics dashboards are outside AI Act scope.

**Current official timeline (checked 2026-10-09, reflecting the AI Omnibus effective 27 July 2026):**
- **Prohibited practices, AI literacy, GPAI, and transparency rules:** follow their separate earlier application dates.
- **Annex III high-risk AI rules:** apply from **2 December 2027**.
- **Regulated-product Annex I high-risk AI rules:** apply from **2 August 2028**.
([Current Commission AI Act guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai))

## 4. Digital Services Act (DSA)

DSA obligations scale by service tier (intermediary, hosting service, online platform, and Very Large Online Platforms / VLOPs). Moderation notice-and-action flows, clear statements of reasons, and user appeals matter for hosting/platform products — but there is no blanket "delete every reported post within 24 hours" or mandatory academic-research API for every small SaaS app. ([DSA scope and responsibilities](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act))

## Summary

Identify the exact regulated activity and legal role (controller vs. processor, provider vs. deployer) before prescribing engineering controls. Implement real GDPR deletion/export across databases, logs, vendors, and backup restores; use exact DORA, GDPR (1-month rights / 72-hour breach), and EU AI Act Omnibus (Dec 2027 / Aug 2028) rules instead of compliance myths; and treat working, testable controls — never buzzwords — as your compliance evidence.

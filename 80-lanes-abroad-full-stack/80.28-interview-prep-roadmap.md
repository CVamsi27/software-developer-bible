# Choose Preparation from the Interview Format

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

If you have an architecture review in five days, spending those five days grinding dynamic programming or starting a brand-new side project is a classic priority trap. Always match your immediate preparation to the **next confirmed interview stage** and your **highest-risk gap**, fitting it inside the single weekly budget in your [Four-Week Study Plan](80.13-study-plan.md).

## First Principles

Interview formats measure completely different skills under a tight clock. Picking study tasks from the actual upcoming stage — then testing yourself cold before reading — maximizes return on every study hour.

## 1. Map the Upcoming Stage to Your First Action

Confirm stage format, duration, language, and allowed tools with the recruiter early, then jump straight to the matching preparation task:

| Next confirmed stage | Highest-return-on-investment (ROI) first task | Go here |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Recruiter screen** | Prepare a 2-sentence role thesis plus visa, compensation, and process questions | [Company Profiles](80.07-company-profiles.md) |
| **Live coding screen** | Solve 2 unseen constraint variations aloud with edge-case tests | [Tech Screen Patterns](80.02-tech-screen-patterns.md) |
| **Project or resume deep dive** | Trace 1 production workflow end-to-end with 2–3 proof cards | [Docita Walkthrough](80.16-docita-resume-defense.md) |
| **System design round** | Derive the core business invariant and trace duplicates, timeouts, and crashes | [System Design Method](80.03-system-design-eu-patterns.md) |
| **Behavioral interview** | Pick 5–7 real events matching team competencies and rehearse follow-ups | [Story Bank](80.12-story-bank.md) |
| **Take-home assignment** | Lock scope to timebox, deliver a working vertical slice and clean checkout | [Take-Home Playbook](80.01-take-home-playbook.md) |
| **Offer or negotiation call** | Break down base, bonus, equity, and relocation, then pick 1 priority ask | [Compensation Benchmarking](80.05-comp-eu-benchmarking.md) |

## 2. Run the 5-Step Tight Feedback Loop

```text
1. Attempt closed-book ─► 2. Pinpoint exact breakdown ─► 3. Study only that mechanism
       ▲                                                               │
       └────────────── 5. Log next action ◄── 4. Re-test changed case ◄┘
```

Do not confuse a brief thoughtful pause with a failure — hunt for the *real* defects: missing invariants, unsupported claims, broken edge cases, or unclear trade-offs. Use [Section Revision](REVISION.md) for quick cold recall and the [Mock Bank](80.11-mock-interview-bank.md) for timed rehearsals.

## 3. Respect Your Real Calendar and Log Interview Lessons

When a real assignment or interview hits your calendar, swap out lower-priority practice instead of stacking impossible hours. With only one week left before a project review, refine your walkthrough and evidence cards — skip building an unrequested portfolio application.

After every real interview, note the questions asked, any tough follow-ups, and direct feedback (keeping guesses separate from facts) in your private tracker so the next round gets sharper.

## Summary

Let your next confirmed interview stage dictate what you study today. Diagnose gaps through closed-book attempts, repair only the missing mechanism, verify transfer on a changed constraint, and keep all preparation inside one realistic weekly budget.

# Brush Up Without Rereading the Whole Library

Brushup starts with an attempt to recall or apply a mechanism. Use the gap you observe to choose the next chapter. A fixed thirty-day schedule cannot guarantee that every file has been learned; the earlier roadmap listed only a subset and pointed to many retired flat paths.

## First Principles

Familiarity with a chapter does not establish unaided recall or transfer to a changed case. A brushup must expose the missing mechanism first, then use a focused read and another attempt to determine whether the gap was repaired.

## Diagnose before reading

Pick a category from its learning-flow INDEX. Try one question in its separate `REVISION.md` or explain a small unfamiliar example. Name the assumption you could not justify. Then read the relevant chapter and try a changed case.

```text
recall or apply → identify the missing mechanism → focused reading
  → changed example → later recall
```

If the topic is new, begin with the category's introductory chapters instead of treating repeated failure as brushup. Revision tests recall after learning; it does not replace an explanation.

Follow each stack's passes and the category's chapter order. Do not flatten hundreds of chapters into one daily checklist. The INDEX owns navigation; this guide should not duplicate filenames that drift when categories change.

## Keep the reading session small

Choose one mechanism and one useful output: a trace, a comparison, a corrected example, or a short explanation. Read until you can explain the missing step. Return to the exercise rather than highlighting an arbitrary three facts in every file.

For example, a failed same-slot booking explanation might require distinguishing idempotency from a capacity invariant. Read the relevant transaction lesson, trace two distinct requests, and state what prevents the conflicting commits. Do not expand that session into every database feature unless another gap calls for it.

## Record what changed

| Record | Useful detail |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Attempt | The question or scenario you tried without notes |
| Gap | The incorrect assumption or missing step |
| Source | The canonical chapter used to resolve it |
| New attempt | A changed case and what you explained or implemented |
| Recheck | A later recall task within the available budget |

Keep actual progress in private records and use the [study plan](80.13-study-plan.md) for one time budget. Completing a read or recognizing an answer is a different observation from applying it correctly. Revise priorities when mocks or real work expose a more consequential gap.

## Summary

Use revision to locate a gap, canonical chapters to learn the mechanism, and changed examples to check transfer. Follow the INDEX flows rather than an oversized list of stale filenames.

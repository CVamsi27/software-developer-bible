# German Tech Salary Negotiation Guide

> **How to use:** Read before every offer negotiation. Use the scripts verbatim. Know your numbers before the call.

---

## 1. Blue Card Salary Thresholds (2026)

The EU Blue Card has a minimum salary requirement. For IT shortage occupations (which includes software engineering), the threshold is lower.

| Threshold | Annual Gross | Monthly Gross | Notes |
| ---------------------- | ------------ | ------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **IT Shortage (2026)** | ~€48,300 | ~€4,025 | Applies to you as a software engineer |
| **General (2026)** | ~€58,400 | ~€4,867 | For non-shortage occupations |
| **Under 30** | ~€45,500 | ~€3,792 | If you're under 30 with a recognized degree |

**Important:** The actual threshold changes annually. Check the latest figures at [bamf.de](https://www.bamf.de) before your interview.

## 4. Negotiation Framework

### The 3-Step Process

```
Step 1: Research (Before the call)
  - Know the market rate for your level
  - Know the company's funding stage and salary bands
  - Know your BATNA (Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement)

Step 2: Anchor (During the call)
  - Let them make the first offer
  - If they ask your expectation, give a range (top 20% of market)
  - Never accept the first offer

Step 3: Negotiate (After the offer)
  - Ask for 24-48 hours to consider
  - Prepare counter-offer with justification
  - Negotiate multiple components (not just base)
```

### The Magic Number

**Always negotiate 10-20% above the initial offer.** This is expected in German tech.

```
Initial offer: €90,000
Counter-offer: €100,000 - €105,000
Expected settlement: €95,000 - €100,000
```

## 5. Counter-Offer Scripts

### Script 1: Base Salary

> "Thank you for the offer. I'm excited about the opportunity. Based on my research of senior full-stack roles in Berlin and my experience building production healthcare SaaS systems, I was expecting a base salary in the range of €[X] to €[Y]. Would it be possible to adjust the base to €[target]?"

### Script 2: Equity

> "I appreciate the equity component. Could you help me understand the current valuation and dilution schedule? I'd like to factor this into my total compensation comparison. If the base salary isn't flexible, would you consider increasing the equity grant to [X]%?"

### Script 3: Signing Bonus

> "The base salary is slightly below my target. Would you be open to a signing bonus of €[X] to bridge the gap in the first year? This would make the total first-year compensation more competitive."

### Script 4: Relocation

> "As I'll be relocating to Germany, the relocation costs are significant. Would the company be able to provide a relocation package of €[X] to cover moving expenses, temporary housing, and visa costs?"

### Script 5: Vacation Days

> "I noticed the standard is 25 vacation days. In my previous roles, I've had 30 days. Would it be possible to match that? If not, could we add 2-3 additional days as part of the package?"

### Script 6: Remote Work

> "The role mentions hybrid work. I've been fully remote for the past [X] years and have maintained high productivity. Would you consider a fully remote arrangement, or at minimum 3-4 remote days per week?"

## 6. What NOT to Do

| Don't | Why |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Accept the first offer immediately | They expect negotiation |
| Say "I need more money because of rent" | Focus on market value, not personal needs |
| Give an ultimatum | Damages relationship |
| Negotiate via email only | Phone/video is more personal and effective |
| Lie about other offers | They may call your bluff |
| Negotiate after accepting | Negotiate before signing |

| **4** | Vacation days | Quality of life |
| **5** | Remote work | Quality of life and commute savings |
| **6** | Education budget | Career development |
| **7** | Hardware | Nice to have |

## 8. Blue Card Specific Tips

### Salary Negotiation for Blue Card

```
Your target salary MUST be above the Blue Card threshold
  IT shortage threshold: ~€48,300/year
  Your target: €80,000 - €120,000/year
  
   Well above threshold — no issues
   If offer is close to threshold — negotiate up
```

### Visa Timeline Impact on Negotiation

```
If company has experience with Blue Card:
  - They know the process
  - They may have HR support
  - Faster timeline (6-8 weeks)

If company is new to Blue Card:
  - Educate them on the process
  - Offer to help with documentation
  - Set expectations on timeline (8-12 weeks)
```

## 9. Relocation Package Negotiation

### What to Ask For

| Item | Typical Range | Script |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| **Moving expenses** | €2,000 - €5,000 | "Cover international moving costs" |
| **Temporary housing** | €1,000 - €3,000/month for 1-2 months | "First month in Airbnb covered" |
| **Flight costs** | €500 - €1,500 | "Cover one-way flight to Berlin" |
| **Visa costs** | €100 - €400 | "Cover visa application fees" |
| **Settling-in allowance** | €1,000 - €2,000 | "Initial setup costs (furniture, etc.)" |

### Script

> "Since I'll be relocating from [country] to Germany, there are significant upfront costs. Would the company be able to provide a relocation package covering [specific items]? I've seen packages of €[X] at similar companies."

## 11. Timing Tips

| When | What to Do |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Before interview** | Research salary ranges for the company |
| **During interview** | Don't discuss salary until they bring it up |
| **After technical round** | If asked, give a range: "I'm targeting €[X]-€[Y]" |
| **After offer** | Ask for 24-48 hours to consider |
| **During negotiation** | Call, not email — more personal and effective |
| **After agreement** | Get everything in writing before signing |

## 12. German Work Contract Tips

| Clause | What to Check |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Probation period** | Usually 6 months — confirm |
| **Notice period** | Usually 4 weeks to end of month during probation, 3 months after |
| **Non-compete** | Check duration and compensation (must be compensated in Germany) |
| **Equity/ESOP** | Check vesting schedule, cliff, acceleration |
| **Remote work** | Get it in writing if negotiated |
| **Vacation days** | Confirm exact number in contract |

---

**Remember:** Negotiation is expected in German tech. Companies budget 10-20% above the initial offer for negotiation. Not negotiating signals that you don't know your market value.

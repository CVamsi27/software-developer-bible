# Discuss and Negotiate an Actual Offer

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Specialized-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)

Many engineers freeze when a European offer arrives because internet advice contradicts itself: *"Always counter 20% higher"* vs. *"German companies never negotiate."* Both extremes are wrong. Some employers have budget room on base salary, others have fixed bands but can add a sign-on bonus, relocation allowance, or equity grant, and occasionally a first offer is already strong and worth accepting. Use [Compensation Benchmarking](80.05-comp-eu-benchmarking.md) to evaluate the numbers first; this guide shows how to run the actual negotiation conversation cleanly and lock every promise into writing.

## First Principles

An offer is a bundle of distinct levers — base salary, level, bonus, equity/VSOP, relocation, remote flexibility, and start date — not a single number. Effective negotiation starts by identifying which **specific term** creates a real gap for you, backing your ask with clear role/market evidence, and offering a constructive alternative if that single lever is locked.

## 1. Audit Every Clause Before You Respond

Never counter on the phone the second you hear the headline number. Thank the recruiter warmly, ask for the full written summary, and verify nine concrete details first:

- Legal employing entity and exact role level/title
- Annual gross base, currency, and payment schedule (12 vs. 13/14 installments)
- Bonus target vs. guaranteed minimum, plus payout conditions
- Equity instrument (RSU, ESOP option, or German VSOP), strike price, vesting/cliff, and leaver rules
- Relocation package (lump sum vs. reimbursement, cap, tax treatment, and clawback period)
- Contracted weekly hours, paid vacation days, and on-call/overtime policy
- Remote vs. hybrid location policy (and permitted work countries)
- Probation period length and notice period during vs. after probation
- Decision deadline

Keep your real offer figures and personal floor strictly in your private workspace.

## 2. Pick One Primary Lever That Solves Your Real Constraint

| Your actual gap | Best lever to discuss | Why it works |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Monthly net cash is tight** | **Base salary** (or role level calibration) | Base compounds every year and sets bonus/pension bases |
| **Upfront move or forfeited bonus hurts year 1** | **Sign-on bonus or relocation lump sum** | One-time budget is often easier for HR to approve than breaking a salary band |
| **Equity value is unclear** | **Full grant math** (valuation, strike, dilution, leaver terms) | Clarifies whether equity is meaningful upside or paper noise |
| **Remote / family schedule constraint** | **Written hybrid/remote days or flexible start date** | Low direct cash cost to the employer, high daily value to you |
| **Embassy / visa delay risk** | **Written start-date contingency clause** | Protects you from losing the offer if a consulate appointment slips |

## 3. Three Copy-Paste Negotiation Scripts

Adapt the wording to your real numbers and facts — **never invent a fake competing offer or fake current salary**.

### Script A — Closing a base salary gap

> *"Thank you for sending the offer — I really enjoyed meeting the team and I'm excited about owning [specific platform/problem]. Looking closely at the senior scope we discussed and comparable senior full-stack roles in [city/market], my main gap is base salary. Is there room to move base from €X to €Y? If we can get close there, I'm ready to sign."*

### Script B — Bridging upfront relocation or forfeited bonus (when base band is fixed)

> *"I understand the base band for this level is fixed at €X. Moving internationally involves upfront housing deposits and [lost bonus / moving expenses] before my first paycheck. Would the team be open to a €Y sign-on or lump-sum relocation allowance to bridge year one?"*

### Script C — Clarifying startup ESOP / VSOP terms before deciding

> *"To evaluate the equity grant fairly alongside base salary, could you share the current fully diluted share count (or latest preferred valuation), the strike/base price, the vesting/cliff schedule, and the post-termination exercise or good-leaver terms?"*

If the employer says a term is firm, ask politely whether a secondary lever has room — and remember that **accepting a fair first offer without pushing unnecessarily** is completely valid when it already meets your target.

## 4. Keep Visa Thresholds Separate From Your Market Ask

Never anchor your salary ask to a country's statutory visa minimum (for example, the German EU Blue Card floor). Visa thresholds are **legal minimums**, not senior market rates — and clearing a salary floor alone does not guarantee degree recognition or embassy approval. Check exact legal criteria in [Visa & Relocation Playbook](80.06-visa-relocation-playbook.md) and confirm employer paperwork owners via [Check German Employer Relocation Support](80.20-germany-visa-relocation-companies.md).

## 5. Lock Every Agreed Change Into the Final Contract

Use email to confirm complex terms so nothing gets lost between verbal calls and HR paperwork. Before signing:

- Verify every negotiated adjustment (base, sign-on, relocation cap, remote days, visa-delay start clause) appears **verbatim** in the contract or official grant letter.
- Read probation notice periods, non-compete / restrictive covenants, intellectual-property clauses, and relocation clawback schedules carefully (see [German Workplace Culture](80.25-german-workplace-culture.md) for statutory vs. contractual notice rules).
- Do not resign your current job until the signed agreement and visa/start-date plan are clear.

## Summary

Read the full written offer before countering, separate guaranteed recurring cash from one-time payments and illiquid equity, pick the one lever that solves your actual constraint, make a calm evidence-backed ask without fabricating competing offers, and verify that every agreed change lands in the signed written contract.

## Start with the exact vacancy

A company can have several teams, languages and hiring locations. Its general reputation does not establish whether a particular vacancy fits you.

Open the employer’s current posting and record its URL, role identifier, location, required skills and date checked. If the posting has disappeared, mark its availability as unknown or closed according to the evidence. An old evaluation is not permission to treat it as open.

## First Principles

Vacancies change independently of a company’s reputation, and an application outcome does not reveal its cause by itself. A defensible next action therefore needs a dated role-specific observation and a separate statement of uncertainty. Keeping one canonical record prevents an old status in a second list from directing the next submission.

## Separate facts from assumptions

Use three columns when evaluating a role:

| Decision | Evidence to record | What remains uncertain |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Can I work in this location? | Posting’s country, office attendance and remote boundaries | Whether the employer will accept relocation or your intended residence |
| Can I do the work? | Required responsibilities matched to an actual artifact or experience | Unfamiliar responsibilities and how much support the team provides |
| Is the language suitable? | Explicit language requirement or recruiter confirmation | Daily team language when the posting does not say |
| Is relocation feasible? | Applicable route and employer’s stated support | Eligibility, documents, salary conditions and processing time |
| Is this worth applying to now? | Live role, meaningful fit and a feasible next step | Response likelihood; do not invent a numerical probability |

A label such as “English-friendly” or “Blue Card-ready” is an assumption unless its source applies to this role. Use the [visa playbook](80.06-visa-relocation-playbook.md) to check the route. A qualified candidate still needs a qualifying job and the required application documents; “no employer sponsorship required” can hide practical employer responsibilities.

## Diagnose a rejection without inventing its cause

A fast rejection does not reveal which filter caused it. Location, work authorization, missing experience, a filled vacancy or an internal candidate are possible explanations, not established facts.

For example, if the posting requires residence in Germany and your application states an overseas location, record that mismatch. If no such requirement appears, ask for feedback when appropriate and leave the cause unknown. Do not rewrite truthful experience into keywords merely because a hypothesis sounds plausible.

Change one meaningful input you can defend: clarify relocation intent, answer work-authorization questions accurately, or replace a vague CV claim with an artifact-backed example. Preserve the submitted version so later results refer to the actual application.

## Keep one record through the application

The canonical records are:

- Targets: research and priorities.
- Application tracker: submitted role, date, version and observed outcome.
- Outreach playbook: contact context and prepared follow-ups.
- CV: source experience and supported claims.

Before applying, reconcile these records by role identifier. A rejection for one vacancy does not establish a permanent company-wide ban, and a new vacancy does not erase an earlier rejection. Follow the employer’s instructions and any recruiter guidance.

## Use the tools without creating another source of truth

The external `career-ops` checkout is a tool. Personal data lives in the private workspace repository, reached through symlinks. Consult its installed command documentation before running a scan or generating an output; command names and configuration can change.

After an authorized tool run, verify the links and generated artifacts here:

```bash
bash ~/repos/personal/doctor.sh
```

Review generated text and PDFs before submission. A generated score cannot establish visa eligibility, prove a technical claim or confirm that a vacancy remains open. Commit personal content in the private workspace repository; never commit or push in the external `career-ops` fork.

## Review the funnel by observed outcomes

At a weekly review, count live opportunities, submitted applications, human responses, interviews and closed roles from the tracker. Keep the period and denominator explicit: “two responses from ten applications submitted in September” is more useful than an unexplained response percentage.

Choose the next action from the evidence: verify an old posting, resolve a requirement, prepare a relevant example, or follow up at the agreed time. Fixed message quotas and invented fit scores can encourage activity without improving decisions.

## Summary

Evaluate the vacancy rather than the company label. Record sources and uncertainty, preserve the submitted version, and update the canonical tracker when an observed event changes the status.

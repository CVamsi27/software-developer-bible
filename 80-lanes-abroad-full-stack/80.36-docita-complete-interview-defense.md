# Docita Interview Defense — Explain One Decision Under Follow-up

> **Purpose:** prepare a short, defensible account of a Docita decision. Start from the [workflow walkthrough](80.16-docita-resume-defense.md), attach an [evidence card](80.17-docita-interview-evidence-pack.md), then use this guide to test whether the explanation survives a changed assumption.
>
> Project implementation notes belong in the [Docita deep dives](../projects/docita/INDEX.md). Personal experience and submitted claims belong in the [personal workspace](personal/README.md).

## Choose one decision, not the whole platform

A long inventory of modules is difficult to follow and does not show why a decision was useful. Pick a concrete operation, such as booking an appointment, recording a payment callback or sending a reminder. State the user-visible result and the failure you needed to prevent.

For example: “A payment callback may arrive twice. Recording the same provider payment twice must not increase the invoice balance twice.” That gives the listener a problem to reason about before introducing transactions, uniqueness constraints or an outbox.

## First Principles

A mechanism’s presence in source does not establish its deployed behavior or business outcome. A failure may occur between operations that looked like one successful action to the user. A defensible explanation must therefore separate the invariant, the exact boundary that enforces it and the evidence for the resulting behavior.

## Build the explanation from five pieces

| Piece | What to explain | Evidence to bring |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Constraint | What must remain true, and under which failures? | Requirement or a reproducible failing case |
| Decision | Which mechanism enforces the constraint? | Exact handler, transaction, constraint or policy |
| Alternative | What would another design change? | A relevant trade-off, not an invented benchmark |
| Limit | Which failure is outside this mechanism’s guarantee? | An adverse test or explicit boundary |
| Outcome | What changed after the decision? | Dated measurement or observed result; leave it unknown if unavailable |

A source file supports an implementation claim. A passing test supports the behavior exercised by that test. Neither alone establishes that the feature is enabled in production or that a business outcome occurred. Use the [claim crosswalk](80.18-resume-claim-crosswalk.md) to keep those distinctions clear.

## Trace a payment callback through the failure boundary

Use this as a reasoning example; confirm the exact current implementation before describing it as your deployed behavior.

```text
Callback arrives with provider payment identifier
  → verify authenticity and expected merchant context
  → begin transaction
      record payment under a database-enforced unique identity
      update the invoice according to its allowed state transition
      append any required outbox record
  → commit
  → acknowledge the callback
  → relay processes committed outbox records
```

Explain what happens when the process stops at each boundary:

1. **Before commit:** the transaction’s database writes roll back. A retry must be safe.
2. **After commit, before acknowledgment:** the sender may retry. The unique identity and duplicate-handling path must prevent another financial effect.
3. **During relay delivery:** delivery can repeat. The consumer needs its own durable protection for effects it performs.
4. **After a remote service accepts a request, before the worker records success:** the outcome is uncertain. A local deduplication row cannot undo a second remote effect; use the remote service’s idempotency or reconciliation contract when available.

Checking for an existing payment before inserting is insufficient under concurrent callbacks unless the database also enforces the identity. An outbox makes the local business write and event record atomic; it does not promise exactly-once delivery or universal commit ordering.

The canonical product notes are [payment flows](../projects/docita/04-payment-flows.md) and [async processing](../projects/docita/03-async-processing.md).

## Make each mechanism’s boundary explicit

Use these checks to sharpen a draft explanation rather than reciting a technology list:

| Topic | Boundary to establish | Canonical reading |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tenant context | Where is identity established, and what prevents missing or bypassed scope? AsyncLocalStorage carries context; it does not authorize access. | [Tenant isolation and RLS](../projects/docita/02-multi-tenancy-rls.md) |
| RLS | Which database role and policies apply? Set transaction-local context and run the protected operations on that transaction’s connection. A rollout plan is not universal enforcement. | [Tenant isolation and RLS](../projects/docita/02-multi-tenancy-rls.md) |
| Queue claiming | How does the claim persist after a short transaction, expire after failure and reject stale completion? `SKIP LOCKED` alone is not a lease or remote-effect guarantee. | [Async processing](../projects/docita/03-async-processing.md) |
| Patient data | Which fields and paths are protected, where are keys held, and what stays readable? Encryption at rest does not protect data after an authorized process decrypts it. | [Project overview](../projects/docita/01-architecture-decisions.md) |
| Clinical workflow | Which checks are implemented, and which require clinical judgment or external verification? A few validation functions do not establish clinical safety or legal compliance. | [Prescription safety](../projects/docita/07-prescription-safety.md) |
| Real-time delivery | How are room access, reconnection and missed events handled? A connected socket does not establish durable delivery. | [Workflow walkthrough](80.16-docita-resume-defense.md) |
| AI controls | Which actual call path enforces permission, payload boundaries, budgets and cancellation? A flag, timeout setting or schema alone does not establish all of them. | [Advanced design decisions](80.21-docita-google-level-prep.md) |
| CI | What does a gate detect and miss, and did it run on this revision? Script counts do not prove correctness. | [Evidence pack](80.17-docita-interview-evidence-pack.md) |
| Origin access | What prevents direct requests from forging proxy headers? A client-supplied `CF-Connecting-IP` header alone does not authenticate Cloudflare. | [Project overview](../projects/docita/01-architecture-decisions.md) |

## Describe alternatives without false limits

A PostgreSQL queue has no universal “1,000 jobs per second” ceiling. Capacity depends on job cost, contention, indexing, claim size, cleanup and database resources. Name a measured bottleneck and the requirement that would justify another delivery system.

Likewise, a broker does not remove the database-to-message atomicity problem, MongoDB supports transactions, and a SPA does not make every authenticated screen faster. Discuss the property needed for this operation and the operational cost of obtaining it.

Distinguish a rejected option recorded in an actual decision document from an alternative you are considering now. Do not invent an evaluation, incident or measurement to make the story sound complete.

## Keep numbers and incidents tied to their sources

For every metric, retain its date, scope and measurement method. Source test cases, browser-matrix executions, passing tests and production workflows are different quantities. A CV bullet is a claim to substantiate, not independent proof of itself.

For an incident, establish what was observed, what caused it, what changed and how the fix was checked. If an earlier note describes a hypothetical failure, present it as a design scenario. Keep personal drafts in the [story bank](personal/story-bank.md) and reconcile claims before reusing them.

## Practice with a changed assumption

Prepare a brief account using the five pieces above, then have the reviewer change one condition: two callbacks arrive together, a worker loses its lease, a tenant context is absent, or the provider accepts a request before a crash.

Draw the resulting sequence. Point to the operation that maintains the invariant and the outcome that remains uncertain. If the explanation fails, repair that specific gap and repeat with a different condition. Smooth delivery and memorized numbers do not substitute for a correct mechanism.

Use the separate [category revision](REVISION.md) for questions and answers, and the [mock interview guide](80.30-mock-interview-script.md) to run the session.

## Summary

One decision is convincing when the listener can follow the problem, the enforcing mechanism, its limits and the evidence. Bring the implementation and its verification state; report unknown outcomes honestly.

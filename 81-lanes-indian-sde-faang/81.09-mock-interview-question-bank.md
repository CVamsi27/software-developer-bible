# Mock Interview Question Bank

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Deferred-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



Use these **90 practice slots** to rehearse coding, design and experience discussions. Coding slots 49 and 50 revisit earlier tasks with changed contracts; they are not two additional unique problems. The batches and suggested times organize this bank, not an employer's question distribution or hiring bar.

Choose a task that exposes an observed gap. Confirm the actual interview format before setting a timer, and reserve time afterward to repair what failed.

## First Principles

A rehearsed answer can hide a missing step. A mock exposes that step only when you must derive the answer, check it and respond to a changed assumption. The observer needs a contract and evidence to distinguish an error in reasoning from an implementation mistake or an unsupported experience claim.

## Coding Questions (50)

For each task, state the input and output contract, derive an invariant, implement it, test relevant boundaries and account for time and space. The listed insight is a cue to reconstruct that reasoning, not a complete answer. Practice budgets are adjustable examples.

### Turn the repeated tasks into changed-contract attempts

- **Slot 49, Serialize and Deserialize Binary Tree:** after the ordinary round trip, define how the decoder rejects truncated, extra or malformed tokens. Explain what the serialized format must preserve.
- **Slot 50, LRU Cache:** after the single-threaded implementation, introduce overlapping operations. A hit changes recency; identify the shared invariant that lookup and eviction must preserve together.

Use the shared [coding-pattern chapters](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/INDEX.md) for complete mechanisms and implementations. After a successful familiar task, change one premise: allow negative values, missing targets, parallel edges, mutable input or different numeric bounds.

### Deepen three designs through one uncertain outcome

**URL shortener:** define code uniqueness and the redirect contract. Hashing a URL does not prevent short-code collisions; concurrent creators need a storage constraint and an explicit retry or existing-mapping policy. Decide how custom aliases, deleted links and cached redirects behave. Follow the [canonical case study](../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.01-url-shortener.md). Do not call networked database operations constant-time end-to-end requests without a stated cost model.

**Chat:** distinguish accepted, durably stored, delivered and read. Trace a server crash after storage but before acknowledgment: the sender needs a stable message identity, and replay must not create another displayed message. Sequence numbers need a defined scope and assigner; they do not establish global order by themselves. Presence expires independently of message durability, and end-to-end encryption requires its own key and trust design. Continue in [chat-system design](../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.15-chat-system.md).

**Ride matching and payment:** a recent location helps choose candidates but does not grant ownership of a driver. Two matching workers need an atomic assignment transition. If the provider accepts a charge before a timeout, the outcome is uncertain: use the provider's retry identity and outcome lookup, then reconcile local state. A queue or database label does not make that remote effect execute exactly once. Follow [ride matching](../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.03-uber.md) and [payment outcomes](../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/02-case-studies/30.2.2.06-payment-gateway.md).

For every design prompt, introduce a duplicate request, delayed owner, unavailable dependency or changing permission. Explain which invariant remains protected and which behavior degrades. Derive capacity from the stated workload rather than importing another company's scale.

## Build an answer from your own evidence

For a mistake, state what actually failed, your responsibility, the immediate mitigation and the later repair. Code rollback may not reverse a database mutation. Explain the recovery boundary and the checks actually run. Do not copy an invented outage duration, six-month incident record or improvement percentage.

For company interest, connect a verified team responsibility to work you can discuss. State the question you still need answered about the role. A generic speech about scale or culture cannot establish why this particular team fits.

Use [behavioral preparation](../70-interview-toolkit/70.1-behavioral/INDEX.md) for the complete method and [resume preparation](81.15-resume-negotiation.md) for claim support. A qualitative observation is useful when it is the evidence you actually have.

## Run a mock and produce a repair

Use the confirmed format when available. For an illustrative 45-minute coding rehearsal, allocate five minutes to the contract, five to the approach, twenty to implementation, ten to checks and five to complexity and follow-ups. Adjust the allocation rather than forcing two long problems into the same window.

For design, follow the operation from requirements through ownership, failure, capacity and recovery; spend time where the unresolved constraint lies. For experience, give a short account and let the observer ask about your contribution, alternatives and evidence. Record only with participants' consent.

After the session, write one observed gap in [the practice log](81.08-progress-tracker.md), choose a repair in its canonical chapter and schedule a changed prompt. Use [the weekly calendar](81.21-week-study-schedule.md) for timing rather than maintaining another mandatory final-week schedule here.

| Observation | Focused repair | Later check |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Search loop does not shrink for one boundary | Define the interval and trace each update | Empty input, two values and an absent target |
| Design lists services but no ownership | Identify the enforcing state transition | Two concurrent requests plus an uncertain reply |
| Experience result has no baseline | Find the actual evidence or revise the claim | Explain measurement conditions and limitations |

A finished timer, polished narration or solved familiar problem is one observation. It does not establish transfer to every task or predict the interview decision.

## Summary

Choose a relevant prompt, establish its contract, derive the answer and test a changed condition. Preserve the observed gap and repair it before another mock. Keep design guarantees tied to mechanisms and experience claims tied to real evidence.

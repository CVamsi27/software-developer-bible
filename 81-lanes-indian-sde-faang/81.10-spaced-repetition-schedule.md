# Spaced Repetition — Schedule the Next Recall Attempt

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Deferred-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



## Start with recall, then use the result

After learning a mechanism, close the chapter and explain or apply it without looking. Check the answer, repair the specific gap, and schedule another attempt after a delay. The [learning and retention guide](81.13-learning-guarantee-system.md) explains the broader learning loop; this file manages when to return.

## First Principles

Recognizing a familiar explanation does not establish that you can produce it later. A delayed attempt supplies evidence about recall under that delay, while a changed example tests transfer. A useful review schedule therefore reacts to observed performance rather than assigning every topic the same permanent interval.

## Choose a review task small enough to diagnose

“Review graphs” is too broad to reveal what failed. Use a focused task, such as explaining why BFS gives shortest paths for equal-weight edges, deriving a DP state, or tracing a duplicate payment callback.

For small concept prompts, answer aloud or on paper before checking. For coding, derive the invariant and implement a changed case rather than recalling the exact source listing. Label any hints used; assisted success is useful evidence but differs from unaided recall.

## Use an initial schedule as an experiment

For a newly repaired topic, you might try a recall check the next day, another a few days later, then a later check if recall remains sound. These are starting choices, not validated optimum intervals for every learner or task.

| Observed attempt | Next action |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Correct explanation and application without a hint | Consider a longer delay; test a variant at a later review |
| Correct label but missing mechanism | Repair the explanation and check again sooner |
| Needed a hint or copied the solution | Record the assistance; repeat unaided after repairing the gap |
| Repeated failure on the same boundary | Study that boundary in the source before scheduling more repetitions |

Research supports practice testing and distributed practice across many learning conditions; it does not establish a universal “retain 80% after thirty days” promise or one ideal 1/3/7/14/30 calendar. See [Dunlosky et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/).

## Record an actual due date

Keep one review queue alongside the [progress tracker](81.08-progress-tracker.md):

| Topic or task | Last attempt | Observed result | Next due date | Reason for interval |
| ------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Your focused prompt | Actual date | Unaided, hinted or incomplete; note the gap | Calendar date | Why this delay is appropriate now |

Be clear whether a delay is measured from the original lesson or the most recent attempt. Do not preprint a twelve-week calendar whose “seven-day” entries occur before seven days have elapsed.

If reviews exceed the available time, reduce new material or prioritize the gaps relevant to the next assessment. Repeatedly marking overdue work complete makes the queue less useful.

## Let the tool schedule cards, but inspect the learning

A card scheduler can help maintain due dates. Follow the current [Anki deck-options documentation](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) for its configuration and scheduling behavior instead of imposing a separate fixed manual calendar on the same cards.

Use the separate [category revision](REVISION.md) and [flashcard guide](81.14-flashcard-decks.md) for prompts. A successful card answer does not prove you can solve a whole design or coding task; retain longer practice sessions in the [weekly schedule](81.21-week-study-schedule.md).

## Summary

Attempt recall before rereading, record what happened and choose a concrete next date. Adjust intervals and new material to performance and available time; keep retention claims tied to actual observations.

# Learning and Mastery System

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Deferred-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



A mastery loop makes gaps visible: study one mechanism, attempt it without
notes, check the result, and revisit the weak part later. It improves the
quality of practice; it cannot guarantee retention, solve every variation or
predict an interview outcome.

## First Principles

1. **Recognition and retrieval are different tasks.** Familiar prose can feel understood even when the mechanism cannot be reconstructed. A closed-book explanation exposes that gap.
2. **Correctness on a familiar example does not establish transfer.** A new input, constraint or failure case tests whether the learner understands the invariant rather than remembers a solution.
3. **Practice produces evidence only with feedback.** An incorrect reconstruction repeated without checking can reinforce the mistake. Compare against a trusted source and record the specific correction.

## What the Evidence Supports

Practice testing and distributed practice have broad support in learning
research. Their effects depend on the material, delay, feedback and assessment;
there is no universal "reading retains 10%, teaching retains 90%" conversion
chart. Use retrieval and spacing as study methods, then measure performance on
your own tasks. [Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)

Explaining a concept is useful when it reveals a missing causal step. Fluency
alone is not a test of correctness: a polished explanation can still be wrong.
Pair it with a worked example or a checked implementation.

## Run One Learning Loop

1. **Choose a small target.** For example: why a binary-search interval shrinks, rather than "learn all search algorithms".
2. **Read the mechanism once.** Identify the invariant, assumptions and failure boundary. Follow a sibling link only when that prerequisite is missing.
3. **Close the source.** Reconstruct the explanation and one example. Put practice answers in the section's separate revision companion.
4. **Check and repair.** Compare the mechanism and output, not the wording. Record one concrete gap and revisit that part of the chapter.
5. **Test a different case.** Change a constraint or use an unfamiliar problem; explain why the original approach still applies or must change.
6. **Schedule another retrieval.** Start with a later-day review; shorten the interval after a miss and lengthen it after successful delayed retrieval.

A possible 40-minute session is ten minutes reading, fifteen solving, ten
checking and five recording the next review. This is a planning example, not
a required pace. Use the [daily timer](81.11-daily-study-timer.md) for the
canonical schedule rather than maintaining another calendar here.

## What Counts as Progress

| Evidence | What it shows | What it does not prove |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Explain the mechanism without notes | The key causal steps are retrievable | Every detail is correct |
| Implement and pass boundary cases | The tested contract works | All inputs or variations work |
| Solve an unfamiliar related problem | Some transfer beyond memorization | Readiness for every interview |
| Explain a failure and trade-off | The assumptions are understood | A design is universally optimal |
| Repeat after a delay | Retention at that measured interval | Permanent retention |

Choose advancement criteria for the actual role. A timed medium problem,
production debugging task and system-design discussion test different skills.
A result such as "two of three correct" is a local practice threshold, not a
scientifically calibrated mastery percentage.

## Avoid the Practice Traps

- **Rereading everything after one miss:** revisit the missing prerequisite or invariant, then test it again. A chapter is not a punishment to restart repeatedly.
- **Never moving on until perfect:** rotate topics and return to gaps. Requiring universal mastery can stop breadth and transfer practice.
- **Memorizing templates without preconditions:** a two-pointer pattern needs a monotone way to rule out candidates; it is not simply "any sorted array". Binary search needs an ordered or monotone decision boundary.
- **Testing only rehearsed problems:** retain some unseen cases and have another person challenge the assumptions when possible.
- **Treating confidence as correctness:** use an error log and checked outputs alongside self-ratings.

## Keep One Progress Record

Use the [progress tracker](81.08-progress-tracker.md) for dates, evidence and
weak topics, and the [spaced review plan](81.10-spaced-repetition-schedule.md)
for the next retrieval. Record the task, the failure cause, the correction and
the delayed retest result. Avoid parallel daily, weekly and monthly forms that
repeat the same status in several documents.

## Summary

- Reconstruct a mechanism without notes, then check it against evidence.
- Use new constraints and delayed attempts to test transfer and retention.
- Fix the specific gap; do not restart the whole syllabus after every miss.
- Use one tracker and an adaptive review schedule.
- Practice supports learning; fixed retention percentages and interview guarantees do not.

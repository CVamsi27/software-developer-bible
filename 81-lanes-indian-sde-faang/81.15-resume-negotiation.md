# Resume & Negotiation — Support the Claim and Compare the Offer

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Deferred-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



## Definition

A resume connects relevant experience to a specific role through claims you can explain and support. Offer negotiation clarifies the proposed responsibilities and terms, then requests a change based on your priorities and available evidence.

> **Textbook Definition:** Employment negotiation is the comparison and adjustment of proposed role conditions through supported requests and explicit agreement; resume claims and compensation terms require separate evidence.

## First Principles

A reader cannot infer your contribution from a technology list, and a percentage without a baseline does not establish impact. An offer’s headline value can also combine cash paid now with uncertain or delayed value. Both decisions therefore require a clear scope, supporting evidence and explicit conditions.

## Write one supported achievement before changing the layout

Start with an actual contribution: the problem, your action and the observed result. Use a number only when you can explain where it came from, the period and what it measures.

| Draft | Better supported wording |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “Worked on the billing API” | “Added duplicate-payment protection to the billing callback handler using a database uniqueness constraint and concurrent callback tests.” |
| “Improved performance by 40%” | State the endpoint, workload, before/after measurement and your change if those measurements exist. Otherwise describe the verified change without inventing a percentage. |
| “Caught 98% of regressions with 95% coverage” | Report the actual coverage measure and exercised behaviors separately. Coverage does not establish the fraction of future regressions caught. |

These are wording examples, not experience to copy into your resume. Keep personal claims in the canonical CV and story bank.

## Make the document easy to read and parse

Use clear experience, skills and education sections with consistent dates. Select the detail relevant to the vacancy; neither years of experience nor a company name establishes a universal page limit.

Follow the application’s accepted file formats. Check the final PDF visually and inspect its extracted text for missing content or scrambled reading order. A simple layout reduces parsing risks; “all ATS systems cannot parse tables” is an unsupported absolute.

Use the role’s vocabulary where it truthfully describes your work. Do not add tools you cannot discuss or infer a rejection cause from an automated response. Preserve the submitted version so future discussion refers to the actual claims.

For employer-specific instructions, use the current application portal: [Google application help](https://support.google.com/googlecareers/answer/6095391?hl=en) and [Microsoft hiring guidance](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips.html). Confirm details for your role rather than treating a generic company profile as a hiring rubric.

## Compare terms by year and certainty

Ask for a written breakdown. Separate these components before adding them:

| Component | What to establish |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Base salary | Currency, pay period, annual amount and employment location |
| Bonus | Target, conditions, eligibility, first-year proration and whether payment is guaranteed |
| Equity | Instrument, grant value or units, vesting, valuation basis, liquidity and departure conditions |
| Sign-on payment | Payment date, repayment obligations and relevant conditions |
| Relocation | Covered costs, payment or reimbursement timing and any repayment terms |
| Role and level | Actual scope, team, reporting line and how responsibilities match the proposed level |

Avoid counting the same bonus twice under different labels. Separate recurring compensation from a one-time sign-on payment. Private-company equity and publicly traded shares have different liquidity; a headline grant value is not cash available today.

For dated market comparisons, use [compensation benchmarking](81.22-compensation-benchmarking.md). Compare equivalent role, location, year and package structure; verify applicable tax and contract questions with current local sources before relying on a calculation.

## Ask for the change that matters

Choose a supported priority: base salary, start date, relocation timing, scope or another term. Research and a competing offer can support a request; neither is a universal prerequisite, and there is no guaranteed percentage increase.

For example:

> “The team and scope fit what I’m looking for. Based on comparable roles and the responsibilities we discussed, could we review the base salary at [supported amount]? If that band is fixed, could we discuss [specific alternative]?”

Replace the placeholders with your real evidence and priorities. Mention another offer only if it exists, and compare its terms accurately. Do not promise to sign immediately unless you are actually ready to do so.

Clarify a deadline if you need more review time. Accepting a suitable first offer can be reasonable; repeated negotiation is not itself a sign of a better decision.

## Close on a complete written agreement

Confirm any revised terms in the offer documents. Resolve unclear repayment clauses, location expectations and start-date dependencies before accepting. Keep the final version and record the assumptions behind your comparison.

A negative anonymous review or a recruiter’s “final offer” statement is information to assess, not a decision rule by itself. Ask role-specific questions and evaluate the evidence against your own priorities.

## Summary

Make relevant claims that survive follow-up. Compare offers by component, year and conditions, then request a supported change and confirm the result in writing.

# Amazon Interviews — Connect Decisions to Evidence

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Deferred-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



Prepare the technical work and the reasoning behind your past decisions. Amazon publishes role-specific preparation guidance and Leadership Principles; use those sources alongside your actual invitation.

## Definition

An evidence-based experience answer connects the situation, your responsibility, actions and observed result. A principle helps interpret the decision; it is not a substitute for the decision itself.

> **Textbook Definition:** Behavioral evidence links an actual situation to personal actions and supported consequences, allowing an interviewer to assess judgment against the role's stated expectations.

## First Principles

An interviewer cannot directly observe your past work. Concrete actions, alternatives and outcomes make the account assessable. Invented numbers or a memorized principle label remove that evidence rather than strengthening it.

## Establish the role-specific assessment

Amazon’s SDE II preparation page, checked on 2 October 2026, describes an online assessment and a loop with four 55-minute interviews. It also identifies coding, design and behavioral preparation. That is guidance for the named role, not a universal schedule for every Amazon level, region or recruiting route. Confirm the actual invitation with your recruiting contact.

Use [coding practice](81.06-leetcode-study-plan.md) and [design preparation](81.03-system-design-apis-security.md). Avoid unsupported claims that Amazon coding is easier than another company’s or that a company-tagged list supplies reliable question probabilities.

## Select experience before assigning principle labels

Choose a real contribution with enough detail to withstand follow-up. Record the original constraint, your responsibility, an alternative considered, the action taken and the observed outcome. Add metrics when they exist and explain their source; a qualitative outcome is acceptable when it is what you actually observed.

Use the official principles to examine the decision:

| Principle | Evidence to look for in your own work |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Customer Obsession | How the decision addressed a specific user problem |
| Ownership | What you took responsibility for and how you followed through |
| Dive Deep | Which observations changed your diagnosis |
| Earn Trust | How you communicated uncertainty, a mistake or a disagreement |
| Learn and Be Curious | What you changed after learning something relevant |

These are preparation prompts, not predictions of interviewer assignments or frequency. Explain your contribution while acknowledging collaborators. Replacing every “we” with “I” can misrepresent shared work.

See [behavioral preparation](../70-interview-toolkit/70.1-behavioral/INDEX.md) for the common method; keep personal evidence in your own preparation notes.

## Rehearse an operational decision: an expired worker

Suppose a worker receives a queue message, then pauses longer than the visibility timeout:

```text
worker A receives → A pauses → message becomes visible
→ worker B receives → A resumes
```

The timeout controls delivery visibility. It does not prove that A stopped executing or cannot modify an external resource. Design the business operation to tolerate repeat processing and delayed owners. A queue’s visibility timeout is not a general distributed mutex.

For a DynamoDB-backed lease, distinguish application expiry from item cleanup. TTL deletion is asynchronous and typically occurs within days. A lock protocol needs conditional ownership and expiry checks, safe renewal/release, and resource-side protection where stale owners could act. Waiting for TTL deletion does not provide timely exclusive ownership.

Follow [system design](../30-architecture/30.2-system-design/INDEX.md) for the complete failure model. Naming DynamoDB, Redis or SQS does not establish the protocol.

## Explain why a design fits the operation

Start with a user operation and its correctness boundary before selecting AWS services. A checkout needs a clear order identity, payment outcome and retry contract; a product catalog can have different consistency needs.

State the workload and derive estimates from it. Include failure recovery, observability and cost assumptions. Avoid claiming a fixed saving percentage from a product choice without a measured workload and dated price evidence.

## Summary

Use the confirmed role guidance, prepare real decisions with supporting evidence, and trace technical choices through duplicate delivery and delayed execution. Principle labels and service catalogs cannot supply missing reasoning.

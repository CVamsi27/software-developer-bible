# Weekly Study Schedule — Fit Practice Into Available Time

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Deferred-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



## Definition

A weekly schedule assigns your available study time to the gaps relevant to a confirmed interview process. The [coding study plan](81.06-leetcode-study-plan.md) chooses the practice sequence; this guide turns that sequence and other assessed work into a feasible calendar.

> **Textbook Definition:** A study schedule allocates a finite time budget to learning, application and later retrieval, revising that allocation using observed gaps and approaching assessments.

## First Principles

Time allocated to one task is unavailable to another, and a failed attempt may need more repair time than expected. A useful schedule must therefore start from a real time budget and adapt when evidence or availability changes. Counting completed problems alone does not establish that a mechanism can be explained or transferred.

## Confirm what the interview will assess

Record the role, expected stages, allowed language and tools, and interview dates. If the format is unknown, confirm it with the recruiter and keep that uncertainty visible. Do not allocate fixed percentages to coding, design and behavioral work based on a company label.

Make one diagnostic attempt in each confirmed format. Identify the specific gap: choosing an invariant, implementing a boundary condition, explaining a transaction or supporting a personal claim. Choose work that repairs that gap.

## Reserve time before assigning topics

Here is an **illustrative ten-hour week**, not a minimum or an optimum:

| Activity | Hours | Result |
| --------------------------------------------- | -----: | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two focused coding sessions | 3 | A derived solution, edge-case checks and an explanation |
| One design or role-specific technical session | 2 | A trace with its enforcing mechanism and failure boundary |
| Experience story and role research | 1 | A supported account relevant to the actual role |
| One mock and debrief | 2 | Observed gaps and a repair action |
| Later recall, weak-topic repair and planning | 2 | Evidence of retention and a revised next week |
| **Total** | **10** |  |

Eight such weeks allocate 80 hours. They do not guarantee readiness or an offer. Reduce or expand the budget according to your actual availability; redistribute work when the confirmed process assesses different skills.

Keep breaks, work commitments and travel outside the focus-hour total rather than silently counting them as study. A disrupted day should trigger a smaller plan, not an unrealistic catch-up quota.

## Progress through outcomes rather than mandatory week numbers

| Stage | Work | Move forward when |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Locate the gap | Language fluency, complexity and a diagnostic coding task | You can state what failed and where to study it |
| Repair the mechanism | Focused pattern or technical chapter, then a changed example | The explanation and implementation hold under the changed condition |
| Integrate skills | Design discussion, project account or mixed coding mock | You can connect the mechanism to the requirement and its limits |
| Adapt to the role | Product research and confirmed employer format | Practice reflects the actual responsibilities and instructions |
| Check logistics | Environment, accommodations, permitted tools and timing | The setup works and unresolved instructions have been clarified |

Use the [coding pattern reading order](../70-interview-toolkit/70.2-coding-patterns/INDEX.md), [core CS](81.02-core-cs-fundamentals.md), [system design guide](81.03-system-design-apis-security.md) and [behavioral chapters](../70-interview-toolkit/70.1-behavioral/INDEX.md) for the relevant stage. Do not relearn all topics every week.

## Record the attempt and revise the next session

Use the [progress tracker](81.08-progress-tracker.md) for the task, observed error, assistance used, repair and later check. Use the [daily timer](81.11-daily-study-timer.md) when recording time helps you plan realistically.

At the weekly review, ask whether the repaired mechanism transfers to a new example and whether the next assessment changes priorities. Keep the result factual: “needed a hint to define the dynamic-programming state” gives a useful next step; “seven out of ten means ready” does not establish an employer’s bar.

## Prepare for the final session

As the interview approaches, select recall and repair work from actual gaps. Check the meeting link, equipment, coding environment, required documents and allowed assistance. Request clarification or accommodations when needed. Leave a realistic margin for rest and setup instead of forcing a last-minute problem quota.

## Summary

Choose a real time budget, allocate it to confirmed assessments and update the calendar using observed performance. Use one practice sequence and one tracker; a schedule organizes work rather than predicting its outcome.

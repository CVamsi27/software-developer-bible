# Compensation Benchmarking — Compare the Same Year and Conditions

[![Role priority](https://img.shields.io/badge/role--priority-Deferred-lightgrey)](../docs/topic-importance.md)
[![General importance](https://img.shields.io/badge/general--importance-Important-blue)](../docs/topic-importance.md)



## Definition

A compensation benchmark is a dated comparison of pay for a defined role, location and scope. It helps evaluate an offer when its components and conditions are comparable; it does not establish what every employer must pay or what a counteroffer will achieve.

> **Textbook Definition:** A compensation benchmark compares dated observations for equivalent role scope, location and payment conditions, separating recurring guaranteed value from conditional, deferred or one-time components.

## First Principles

An annual salary, a four-year equity grant and a one-time payment describe different periods. Adding their headline values hides when compensation becomes available. Benchmarking therefore requires matching both job scope and the time and conditions attached to each component.

## Collect comparable evidence before choosing a range

Use the employer’s posted range when available, recruiter-confirmed terms and dated market observations. For each observation, record:

| Field | Why it matters |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Country, city and employment arrangement | Pay and benefits can vary by location; a remote label does not establish one global band |
| Role scope and employer level | Titles and numeric levels do not map exactly across companies or follow years of experience alone |
| Observation date | An old package can misrepresent the current market |
| Base, target bonus, equity and sign-on separately | A headline total can hide conditional or one-time value |
| Source and sample limitations | A self-reported package may be incomplete or unrepresentative |

Keep a range with its evidence instead of copying an unsourced company salary table. Use the actual role description to compare responsibilities and confirm the proposed level with the recruiter.

## Build a year-by-year comparison

Consider this **hypothetical** offer, in one currency: annual base 100,000, target bonus 10,000, sign-on 20,000, and an initial equity grant valued at 80,000 with an assumed 25% vesting in each of four years. Hold the share valuation fixed only for this arithmetic illustration.

| Year | Base | Target bonus | Sign-on | Initial equity vesting at assumed value | Gross comparison total |
| ---- | ------: | -----------: | ------: | --------------------------------------: | ---------------------: |
| 1 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 20,000 | 20,000 | 150,000 |
| 2 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 0 | 20,000 | 130,000 |
| 3 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 0 | 20,000 | 130,000 |
| 4 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 0 | 20,000 | 130,000 |

The total across these four years is 540,000 under the stated assumptions. The recurring base remains 100,000, the target bonus is conditional, and the equity’s realized value may change. Do not call the comparison total guaranteed cash.

Use the actual vesting schedule. Dividing every grant by four is wrong when vesting is uneven, starts later or has other conditions. If a refresher grant is confirmed, add only the portion scheduled to vest in that year; do not count the whole grant repeatedly. Keep unconfirmed raises and refreshers in a separate scenario.

## Distinguish the equity instrument from its headline valuation

A restricted stock unit is a contractual entitlement governed by its grant terms; vesting, settlement and any liquidity conditions determine when shares or value become available. An option grants a right to buy shares under specified conditions and an exercise price. They are not interchangeable.

Read the grant documents for the schedule, departure conditions, settlement, exercise window where relevant and restrictions on sale. A private-company valuation does not establish immediate liquidity. For publicly traded shares, market value can move; see [Investor.gov: stocks](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks).

## Keep gross pay, withholding and final tax separate

Payroll withholding is a payment toward tax, not necessarily the final tax rate. A bonus withholding percentage cannot by itself tell you the net cost of an offer. Rules depend on jurisdiction, residence, instrument and timing.

For a US employment example, consult the current [IRS employer withholding guidance](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15). The [IRS withholding estimator](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator) has eligibility limits, including excluding nonresident tax status. For other locations, use the applicable authority rather than importing US assumptions.

Check repayment terms on sign-on and relocation payments. Do not assume they are prorated, universally negotiable or tax-efficient to split across years.

## Turn the comparison into a decision

Separate your priorities: recurring cash, short-term cash needs, uncertain equity, location, work arrangement and role scope. Record what would make the package acceptable and which uncertain term needs clarification.

Use [resume and negotiation](81.15-resume-negotiation.md) for the conversation. There is no universal recruiter discretionary percentage or reliable promise that walking away will improve an offer. Confirm revised terms in writing.

## Summary

Compare equivalent roles and dated observations. Model actual payment and vesting schedules by year, separate guaranteed and conditional value, and keep tax assumptions explicit.
